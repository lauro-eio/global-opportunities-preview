Crafting Formal Policy Updates

Imagine you are part of a national committee tasked with reviewing a new international trade policy. Your role is to draft a short amendment or clarification clause (1-2 sentences) that directly addresses a potential impact on a local industry or job security within your country.

Your clause must:
1. Be written in a high-register, formal, legalistic style.
2. Incorporate at least one 'prepositional compound' structure (e.g., hereby, wherein, thereof) with precise adverbial positioning.
3. If relevant to your clause, include a fronted negative phrase (e.g., under no circumstances) with correct subject-auxiliary inversion.

Consider the impact of terms like 'tariff barriers' or 'anti-dumping duties' on a local sector you are familiar with (e.g., manufacturing, agriculture, services).

Tasks

Before you draft your clause, match these key trade terms to their definitions to ensure clarity in your policy language.

Match each key trade term to its correct definition.

  • tariff barriers — Taxes or duties imposed on imported goods, designed to restrict trade and protect domestic industries.
  • anti-dumping duties — Special tariffs levied on imported goods priced significantly below fair market value, to protect local markets.
  • trade sanctions — Economic or trade restrictions imposed by one country on another, often for political reasons or to enforce international law.
  • supply chain disruption — Any unforeseen interruption or breakdown in the process of getting products or services from their origin to the final consumer.
