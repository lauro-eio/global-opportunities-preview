L-IN

Lead-In: Collaborative Problem Solving

Teacher notes

Begin by projecting the lead-in brief. Ensure students understand the core challenge: an intricate operational bottleneck no single person fully grasps.
Divide students into small groups (3-4).
Ask groups to discuss the questions, encouraging them to draw on their own workplace experiences.
Facilitate a brief whole-class feedback session, noting down key strategies and challenges mentioned.
Introduce the idea of formalizing these discussions into written communication for clarity and action.

Student handout

Solving Complex Challenges Together

Your team is facing an intricate operational bottleneck that no single person understands completely. This issue is causing delays and impacting productivity across multiple departments. To resolve it, you need to draw on everyone's unique insights.

Discuss in your groups:
1. How would you start a conversation to gather insights from colleagues who only know part of the problem?
2. What challenges might you face when trying to get everyone to share their knowledge openly?
3. What kind of information would you need to collect from different people?
4. Why is it important to capture these insights in writing?

MOD

Model Analysis: Strategic Collaboration Summary

Teacher notes

Distribute the 'Interdepartmental Synergy Report' to students.
Explain that this report is a summary written after a series of collaborative discussions aimed at resolving a complex operational bottleneck.
Give students 5-7 minutes to read the report silently.
Instruct students to then complete the multiple-choice and short-answer tasks individually, focusing on the purpose, audience, and key strategies presented in the model.
After individual work, put students in pairs to compare answers and discuss any discrepancies.
Lead a whole-class review of the answers, clarifying any points and highlighting the effective use of strategic communication and knowledge synthesis.
Pay particular attention to the language used to acknowledge diverse input and propose actionable solutions.

Student handout

Reviewing a Collaboration Brief

Read the following report, written after a series of meetings to address a complex interdepartmental issue. This report aims to summarize the collective findings and proposed solutions.

After reading, answer the questions that follow.

Materials

Interdepartmental Synergy Report: Optimizing Workflow Integration

To: Project Stakeholders
From: Operations Strategy Team
Date: October 26, 2023
Subject: Summary of Collaborative Initiatives for Workflow Integration Challenge

1. Executive Summary
This report synthesizes insights from recent interdepartmental collaboration sessions focused on addressing the persistent workflow integration bottleneck between Manufacturing, Logistics, and Quality Assurance. Our objective was to leverage collective intelligence to devise a comprehensive solution. The sessions highlighted specific friction points and identified actionable strategies for resolution, emphasizing critical systems thinking.

2. Background: The Integration Bottleneck
The current hand-off process between Manufacturing and Logistics has led to frequent delays and miscommunications, impacting delivery timelines and customer satisfaction. Quality Assurance also reported inconsistencies in data transfer, complicating post-production analysis. No single department held all the necessary information to diagnose the root cause or propose a complete fix.

3. Collaborative Approach & Knowledge Synthesis
We convened representatives from all three departments. The methodology involved structured brainstorming, root cause analysis, and cross-functional process mapping. Key to our success was the open exchange of departmental perspectives, enabling genuine knowledge synthesis. Each team member contributed their specialized understanding, creating a holistic view of the operational challenge.

4. Key Findings & Strategic Recommendations
Through this collaborative process, we identified several critical areas for improvement:

a) Standardized Data Protocol: Implementing a unified digital protocol for information transfer between Manufacturing and Logistics. This will reduce manual errors and improve real-time visibility.

b) Cross-Training Initiative: Developing a cross-training program for key personnel in Logistics and Quality Assurance to better understand each other's operational requirements. This fosters empathy and improves problem anticipation.

c) Dedicated Integration Liaison: Appointing a temporary liaison for the next quarter to oversee the implementation of new protocols and facilitate ongoing strategic communication between departments.

d) Regular Review Cycles: Establishing bi-weekly check-ins for the next three months to monitor progress, address emerging issues, and reinforce collaborative efforts.

5. Expected Outcomes
These recommendations are projected to reduce workflow integration delays by 15% within the next three months, improve data accuracy by 20%, and enhance overall team synergy. We believe this demonstrates the power of leveraging diverse team knowledge to solve complex challenges effectively.

6. Next Steps
The Operations Strategy Team will present these recommendations to senior management next week. We will then schedule a follow-up meeting with departmental heads to finalize implementation plans.

PREP

Preparation: Planning a Strategic Proposal

Teacher notes

Remind students of the initial bottleneck discussion from L-IN.
Explain that they will now plan their own strategic proposal based on a hypothetical collaborative discussion.
Divide students into new pairs or small groups (3-4).
Guide them through brainstorming content for their proposal, using the model report structure as a guide (Executive Summary, Background, Collaborative Approach, Findings/Recommendations, Expected Outcomes, Next Steps).
Introduce the column_banks with useful phrases. Encourage students to select phrases that fit the C2 level and professional tone required.
Circulate and provide support, prompting students to think about strong arguments and clear communication for their proposed solutions.
Emphasize the importance of using the target lexical tags where appropriate.

Student handout

Building Your Solution Strategy

Imagine you've successfully facilitated a series of discussions about the operational bottleneck from our lead-in. Now, you need to consolidate the insights and propose a solution. Think about the model report we just analyzed.

Work in groups to plan your own strategic proposal. Consider the following points:
1. Audience: Who needs to read this proposal? (e.g., senior management, department heads)
2. Purpose: What do you want this proposal to achieve?
3. Key Findings: What were the main problems identified through collective intelligence?
4. Proposed Solutions: What specific actions or changes are you recommending? How do they leverage knowledge synthesis?
5. Expected Outcomes: What positive results do you anticipate from your solutions?
6. Structure: How will you organize your proposal for maximum clarity and impact?

Use the phrases in the bank below to help you craft a professional and effective proposal.

FP

Free Practice: Drafting a Strategic Proposal

Teacher notes

Remind students of their planning from the PREP stage.
Instruct them to write their strategic proposal individually, using their notes and the language from the column banks.
Emphasize adhering to the specified audience (senior management/department heads), purpose (proposing solutions based on collective intelligence), and approximate length.
Monitor students as they write, offering support on language, structure, and content, but avoid direct correction at this stage.
Encourage them to focus on clarity, professional tone, and effectively communicating how diverse insights led to the proposed solutions.

Model Answer (for teacher reference only, approx. 280 words):
Subject: Strategic Proposal for Supply Chain Integration & Communication Bottleneck
To: Senior Leadership, Department Heads (Procurement, Logistics, Production)
From: Interdepartmental Task Force
Date: October 26, 2023

1. Executive Summary
This proposal outlines a strategic approach to resolve the long-standing supply chain integration bottleneck impacting lead times and inventory management. Through focused interdepartmental collaboration, we leveraged collective intelligence from Procurement, Logistics, and Production to achieve comprehensive knowledge synthesis. Our recommendations prioritize streamlining information flow and fostering proactive strategic communication.

2. Background & Collaborative Insights
Analysis revealed disconnects in real-time inventory data sharing and order processing. No single department possessed the full picture, leading to reactive problem-solving. Our task force convened a series of workshops, encouraging open dialogue and critical systems thinking to map the entire supply chain.

3. Key Recommendations
a) Integrated Digital Platform: Implement a unified software solution for shared inventory visibility and order tracking across all departments.
b) Weekly Coordination Huddles: Establish mandatory 15-minute weekly huddles between team leads from affected departments to pre-empt issues.
c) Role-Based Cross-Training: Develop a program to familiarize key personnel with upstream/downstream processes, enhancing mutual understanding.

4. Anticipated Outcomes & Next Steps
These measures are projected to reduce order processing errors by 10%, improve inventory turnover by 5%, and significantly boost cross-functional efficiency within six months. We believe this represents an effective application of diverse team knowledge. The Task Force seeks approval to begin piloting the integrated platform next month.

FBDC

Feedback & Correction: Refining Your Proposal

Teacher notes

Explain the two-stage feedback process: first content, then language.
Pair students (preferably with someone they didn't work with in PREP) and instruct them to exchange their written proposals.
Guide students to use the feedback questions provided, focusing on the clarity, completeness, and strategic value of the content first.
Circulate and facilitate discussions, ensuring constructive criticism and positive reinforcement.
After content feedback, instruct students to re-read their own proposals, focusing on language accuracy, professional register, and effective use of target vocabulary.
Collect proposals for final teacher feedback on language accuracy.

Student handout

Polishing Your Professional Proposal

Now it’s time to get feedback on your strategic proposal and refine it. A well-communicated proposal is crucial for gaining support for your solutions.

Part 1: Content Feedback (Peer Review)
Exchange your proposal with a partner. Read their proposal carefully and discuss the following questions:
1. Clarity: Is the operational bottleneck clearly explained? Are the proposed solutions easy to understand?
2. Logic: Does the proposal flow logically from identifying the problem to presenting solutions and expected outcomes?
3. Collaboration: Does the proposal effectively convey how collective intelligence and knowledge synthesis were used to arrive at the solutions?
4. Impact: Are the expected outcomes persuasive? Do the solutions seem achievable?
5. Audience & Purpose: Is the tone appropriate for senior management? Does it clearly aim to solve a workplace challenge?

Part 2: Language & Register Review (Self-Correction)
After receiving feedback on content, review your own proposal. Focus on:
1. Lexis: Have you effectively used vocabulary related to strategic learning, interdepartmental collaboration, critical systems thinking, and strategic communication?
2. Professionalism: Is the language consistently professional and appropriate for a C2-level workplace document?
3. Accuracy: Check for any grammatical errors, spelling mistakes, or awkward phrasing.

Make any necessary revisions to improve your proposal.
