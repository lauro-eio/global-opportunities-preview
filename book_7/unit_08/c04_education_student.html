Match the term to its definition.

  • reflective practice — Regularly thinking about your work and learning from experiences to improve.
  • competency framework — A structured list of skills, knowledge, and behaviors needed for a specific job or role.
  • performance analysis — Reviewing how well tasks or projects were done to identify strengths and areas for improvement.
  • individual development plan — A formal plan outlining an employee's learning goals and the steps to achieve them.
  • skill matrix — A visual tool showing the skills an individual or team has, and their proficiency levels.

First Impressions & Core Themes

Listen to the conversation between two colleagues, David and Sarah, who are discussing a recent logistics project. What is the main point they are trying to address during their review?

Materials

Post-Project Reflection: The Warehouse Optimization Initiative

David: Sarah, thanks for meeting. That warehouse optimization initiative was... challenging.
Sarah: Challenging is one word for it, David. I think we delivered, but honestly, it felt like we were building the plane while flying it.
David: Exactly. And that's where I want to focus. I'm trying to do a bit of reflective practice on my end. Looking back, what do you think were the biggest hurdles?
Sarah: From my perspective, a lack of consistent data analysis skills among some of the leads. And understanding the full scope of the new inventory management software. We had a steep learning curve mid-project.
David: That aligns with my thoughts. I'm starting to map out a competency framework for the team for next year. This project really highlighted some gaps. For instance, advanced spreadsheet manipulation and problem-solving under pressure.
Sarah: Absolutely. My team really struggled with the unexpected delays because they lacked that specific troubleshooting agility. If we'd done a proper performance analysis beforehand, we might have spotted that we were under-skilled in certain areas.
David: Yes, that's the ideal. I'm thinking about how we integrate something like a skill matrix into our quarterly reviews. Not just 'can you do X?' but 'how proficient are you, and what training do you need?'
Sarah: That would be incredibly useful. I've been meaning to put together an individual development plan for Mark and Elena, specifically around supply chain analytics. This project just made it urgent. Knowing those gaps earlier would have saved us a lot of stress and overtime.
David: My goal is to prevent that 'building the plane' feeling next time. Proactive upskilling, guided by a clear understanding of our current capabilities and future needs. It’s about being strategic.
Sarah: Agreed. Let's make sure our next project isn't just about delivering, but also about growing strategically.
