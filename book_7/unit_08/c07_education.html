TCH

Teach: Clarifying Concessive Linkers (Albeit/Notwithstanding)

Teacher notes

1. Address specific errors identified in T1. Focus on the distinction between 'Albeit/Notwithstanding + Noun/Adjectival Phrase' and 'Although/Despite + Clause/Gerund'.
2. Explain the form: 'Concessive linker (Albeit/Notwithstanding) + noun phrase/adjectival block, main clause'. Emphasize the lack of 'but' or 'that' directly following.
3. Discuss the meaning: 'Structuring formal executive evaluations, corporate learning re-engineering plans, and educational reform policies while systematically addressing layout limitations.' Explain how these linkers introduce a significant systemic barrier or philosophical constraint, yet the core argument remains valid.
4. Use the concept check questions orally: 'Are we introducing a significant systemic barrier or philosophical constraint first? (Yes)' and 'Does the core argument regarding knowledge systems remain valid despite this concession? (Yes)' to confirm understanding.
5. Guide students through the practice tasks, encouraging self-correction and peer discussion based on the rules clarified.

Student handout

Mastering Concessive Structures for Formal Reports

From our diagnostic, it's clear we need to focus on the precise usage of concessive linkers like Albeit and Notwithstanding in formal writing. These linkers are powerful tools for executive evaluations and strategic reports, allowing us to acknowledge a challenge or limitation without undermining our main point.

Key Rules:
1. Albeit / Notwithstanding are followed directly by a noun phrase or adjectival block, NOT a full clause with 'that' or a coordinating conjunction like 'but'.
2. They introduce a concession or counterpoint that is significant but does not invalidate the main statement.

Look at the examples below and then complete the practice sentences.

Test 2: Drafting a Strategic Post-Mortem Summary

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will apply the target language in a more integrated, productive context related to the unit's theme of 'learning from operational setbacks'.
2. Instruct students to draft a brief summary or analysis, focusing on incorporating the concessive linkers 'Albeit' and 'Notwithstanding' naturally and correctly.
3. Circulate and monitor, providing support as needed but primarily observing for correct application of the target structures.
4. Provide a sample answer or key points for discussion after students have completed their drafts. Focus on:
- Correct form (linker + noun/adjectival block).
- Appropriate use to introduce significant, yet non-fatal, constraints.
- Professional register and coherence.
Sample structure for student response:
'The Q3 project review revealed critical oversights. Albeit significant initial challenges, the team managed to stabilize client relations. Notwithstanding budget constraints, the new software integration provided valuable insights into workflow bottlenecks. This post-mortem highlights areas for continuous improvement.'
