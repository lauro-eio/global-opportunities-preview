Teach: Mastering Rhetorical Subordination and Concessive Modifiers

Teacher notes

1. Review the answers from T1, highlighting the common errors observed.
2. Focus on Rhetorical Subordination post Concessive Modifiers.
3. Form: Explain that words like notwithstanding, albeit, despite, and in spite of introduce a contrast or concession.
- Notwithstanding / Despite / In spite of: Generally followed by a noun phrase, gerund phrase, or the fact that + clause. NOT a full clause directly.
- Albeit: Usually followed by a clause or an adverbial/adjectival phrase (often shortened).
- Address the redundancy of but after albeit or similar concessive clauses (e.g., 'Albeit X, but Y' is incorrect).
4. Meaning (CCQ 1): Ask students: "Are we acknowledging a substantial organizational hurdle, intuition block, or historical vulnerability first?" (Yes). Emphasize that these structures acknowledge a counter-argument or obstacle, then pivot to assert a main point.
5. Meaning (CCQ 2): Ask students: "Does the core institutional legacy assertion or corporate mandate remain valid despite this concession?" (Yes). Reinforce that the main clause often reaffirms a core strategy or commitment, showing resilience or strategic thinking despite challenges.
6. Provide examples relevant to the workplace context.
7. Students practice rewriting sentences to incorporate these structures.
8. Focus on the nuance these structures add: acknowledging complexity while asserting conviction or strategy.

Student handout

Refining Your Executive Summaries

In high-level business communication and project reviews, precision and rhetorical effectiveness are key. We often need to acknowledge complexities or challenges while still asserting a clear strategy or conclusion. This requires careful use of concessive modifiers and subordinate clauses.

Let's review the structures we use to achieve this:

1. Notwithstanding / Despite / In spite of: These are followed by a noun phrase or a gerund phrase (verb + -ing). They highlight a contrast. For example:
* _Notwithstanding the budget constraints, the team delivered the project on time._
* _Despite facing significant logistical hurdles, the rollout was successful._
2. Albeit: This means 'although' or 'even though'. It can be followed by a subordinate clause or a shortened adjectival/adverbial phrase. It's often used in formal contexts to add a nuance of reluctant acceptance or mild opposition.
* _The report was comprehensive, albeit somewhat overdue._
* _Albeit the market showed initial resistance, the product gained traction._

Key Principle: These structures allow you to acknowledge a challenge or a different perspective (the concession) while still emphasizing your main point or strategy. It shows thorough thinking and confidence, rather than ignoring difficulties.

Be careful not to use redundant conjunctions like 'but' after an 'albeit' clause. For example, 'Albeit the project was complex, but it succeeded' is incorrect. Simply remove 'but'.

2. Remind them to use a formal, professional register and to incorporate concessive structures where appropriate to acknowledge challenges while asserting strategic direction.

6. Sample Model Answer for Teacher (for reference only, not to be displayed to students directly):
"Subject: Executive Brief: Q3 Infrastructure Upgrade Learnings

Team, we've completed the Q3 Infrastructure Upgrade, a project of significant strategic importance. Notwithstanding initial delays due to unforeseen supply chain disruptions, the core objectives were met within a revised timeline. The debriefing session highlighted critical insights regarding vendor management and root cause identification for technical glitches. These findings are now integrated into our lessons learned repository and will inform a more robust procurement framework for Q4. Albeit the project incurred a marginal cost overrun, the enhanced system stability and improved performance metrics demonstrate a substantial return on investment. This experience reinforces our commitment to continuous improvement through an iterative process of planning and execution. We anticipate applying these refined methodologies to the upcoming Q4 operational projects."
7. Briefly discuss student drafts or peer review to check for correct usage of concessive structures and appropriate register.

Executive Brief: Project Learnings & Next Steps

You are a project lead tasked with preparing a brief internal memo for senior management. This memo needs to update them on a recent operational project. This project faced significant hurdles but ultimately yielded valuable lessons and positive outcomes, even if not entirely as planned.

Your memo should:

1. Briefly outline the project's purpose and its primary challenges.
2. Acknowledge the difficulties using concessive modifiers (e.g., Notwithstanding, Albeit).
3. Explain how these challenges led to crucial insights or lessons learned.
4. Reaffirm the positive outcome or strategic value, demonstrating strategic foresight and a commitment to continuous improvement.
5. Use a formal, professional register appropriate for an executive audience.

Aim for a concise memo of 100-150 words. Focus on clarity, conciseness, and the effective use of advanced concessive structures to convey both challenges and success.
