L-IN

Lead-In: Reflecting on Equipment Failures

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs or small groups.
2. Read the prompt aloud and ask students to discuss their experiences.
3. Encourage them to share specific examples of machinery or systems.
4. Facilitate a brief whole-class share-out on common themes or lessons learned.

Student handout

Learning from Unexpected Breakdowns

Think about a time when a crucial piece of machinery or equipment broke down suddenly, causing a halt in production or service at your workplace.

Discuss the following questions with your partner:

1. What was the immediate impact of the breakdown?
2. What data points or warning signs might have been present but overlooked at the time?
3. In hindsight, what predictive approaches or proactive measures could have potentially prevented this crisis?
4. How might the implementation of proactive maintenance schedules based on historical data change such outcomes?

MOD

Model Dialogue: Advocating for Proactive Maintenance

Teacher notes

1. Ask students to read the dialogue to understand the main points.
2. Students can read the dialogue silently or in pairs (one as Liam, one as Anya).
3. After reading, instruct students to complete the multiple-choice question to check gist comprehension.
4. Review the answer together and clarify any unknown vocabulary, focusing on the target lexis.

Student handout

A Discussion on Strategic Foresight

Read the following conversation between Liam, a Production Manager, and Anya, a Lead Technician, discussing a recent incident and a path forward.

Liam: Anya, that unplanned downtime last week on Line 3 really hit us hard. We lost a full shift of operational uptime. What’s the post-mortem looking like?

Anya: It was an unexpected bearing failure, Liam. While we conduct regular checks, it wasn't due for a preventative overhaul until next quarter. We missed some subtle wear indicators that a diagnostic testing routine could have flagged earlier.

Liam: So, it was a blind spot. We rely heavily on the manufacturer's recommended asset lifecycle estimates, but perhaps that's not robust enough for our usage patterns. We need to avoid another costly surprise.

Anya: Exactly. My proposal is to implement a more dynamic proactive maintenance schedule. We can integrate sensor data for real-time monitoring and historical failure rates to predict potential issues. It's about moving from reactive fixes to strategic foresight.

Liam: That sounds like a significant shift. What would be the immediate steps? Do we have the resources for increased diagnostic testing?

Anya: We can start with the critical path equipment. We'd invest in specialized sensors and training for advanced analysis. The long-term savings from averting unplanned downtime would far outweigh the initial costs. It ensures continuous operational uptime.

Liam: I see the value in that. Let's draft a proposal to outline the new schedule, focusing on data-driven triggers rather than just fixed intervals. We need to demonstrate how this elevates our asset lifecycle management.

Anya: Agreed. This is about building resilience and ensuring we're ahead of the curve.

Materials

Tasks

Liam and Anya agree that the best way to prevent future unplanned downtime is to:

  • Stick strictly to the manufacturer's recommended maintenance intervals.
  • Increase the frequency of reactive repairs as problems occur.
  • Implement a more dynamic, data-driven proactive maintenance schedule.
  • Only conduct diagnostic testing when equipment visibly breaks down.
PREP

Preparation: Structuring a Proposal for Change

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will prepare for a discussion or role-play about implementing proactive maintenance.
2. Draw attention to the 'Useful Phrases' and 'Key Vocabulary' columns.
3. Instruct students to take notes, organizing their ideas using the provided prompts.
4. Circulate and assist with vocabulary or idea generation.

Student handout

Preparing Your Argument for Proactive Maintenance

Prepare for a discussion where you will propose or discuss implementing a new proactive maintenance schedule for a critical piece of equipment in your workplace. Think about a specific machine or process you know well.

Consider the following points in your preparation:

* Identify a specific piece of equipment where proactive maintenance would be beneficial.
* Recall specific past issues or risks associated with this equipment.
* Suggest specific proactive measures (e.g., sensor monitoring, new diagnostic tests).
* Outline the benefits (e.g., reduced downtime, extended asset lifecycle).
* Address potential challenges (e.g., cost, training).

FP

Fluency Practice: Implementing Proactive Maintenance

Teacher notes

1. Explain the role-play scenario and assign roles (e.g., Manager and Lead Technician).
2. Remind students to use the ideas and vocabulary from the PREP stage.
3. Monitor students' discussions, noting down good language use and errors (for FBDC).
4. Do not interrupt fluency; let them complete the task.

Student handout

Putting Strategic Foresight into Practice

You will now work in pairs to discuss implementing a new proactive maintenance schedule. One of you will be a Manager (e.g., Production Manager, Operations Lead) and the other will be a Lead Technician or Maintenance Supervisor.

Scenario:
Your company has recently experienced several costly unplanned downtime incidents due to unexpected equipment failures. You need to agree on a strategy to move towards a more proactive approach.

Your Task:

Manager: You are concerned about costs and disruption but are open to new ideas if a strong case can be made. You need to understand the practicalities and benefits.

Lead Technician/Supervisor: You believe firmly in the benefits of proactive maintenance schedules. You must persuade the Manager by explaining how to implement such a schedule, referencing equipment lifespan, usage patterns, and historical data, and clearly outlining the benefits (e.g., increased operational uptime, extended asset lifecycle) while addressing potential challenges.

Aim to agree on a set of concrete steps for moving forward with a proactive maintenance strategy.

FBDC

Feedback: Content and Language

Teacher notes

1. Begin with open class feedback on the content of their discussions: What solutions did they propose? What were the main agreements/disagreements? (Content feedback first).
2. Then, provide delayed error correction based on notes taken during FP.
3. Focus on common errors related to the target lexis (operational uptime, preventative overhaul, etc.) and general fluency issues.
4. Write example corrections on the board if appropriate, or repeat incorrect sentences correctly for students to self-correct.
5. Praise effective use of the target language and productive communication strategies.

Student handout

Reflecting on Our Strategies

Reflect on your discussion about implementing proactive maintenance schedules.

1. What specific proactive maintenance strategies did you agree upon for your chosen equipment?
2. What were the most convincing arguments for adopting these strategies?
3. Were there any challenges or concerns that were difficult to resolve?
4. Think about the vocabulary and phrases you used. Were you able to incorporate the target language effectively?
