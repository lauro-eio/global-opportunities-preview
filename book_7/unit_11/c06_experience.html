Lead-In: Future-Proofing Your Career

Teacher notes

Introduce the concept of career foresight. Encourage learners to share personal experiences and challenges. Guide discussion to link individual actions to broader industry trends. Ensure a professional and practical tone. The discussion should directly address the lead-in brief.

Student handout

Navigating Tomorrow's Workplace

Imagine your industry undergoing a radical transformation in the next decade.

1. What new skills or knowledge might become essential for someone in your role or field? Think about industry disruption you've already seen or anticipate.
2. How are you proactively positioning yourself for these future demands? What does professional upskilling mean to you?
3. Consider transferable competencies – what skills do you have now that could be valuable in different future roles?

Understanding a Proactive Plan

Read the internal memo below from an Operations Coordinator to their Manager regarding future skill development. Then answer the questions that follow.

Materials

Internal Memo: Strategic Skill Development – Operations Team

To: Eleanor Vance, Operations Manager
From: Javier Rodriguez, Operations Coordinator
Date: October 26, 2024
Subject: Proactive Skill Development for Future Operational Demands

Dear Eleanor,

I am writing to propose a proactive approach to skill development within our operations team, given the anticipated shifts in the logistics sector. Recent discussions and industry reports highlight a growing trend towards automation and integrated digital platforms, suggesting a potential industry disruption in how we manage inventory and dispatch.

To ensure our team’s long-term viability and maintain operational efficiency, I believe it is crucial to focus on professional upskilling in areas such as data analytics for logistics optimization, basic programming for automated system oversight, and advanced digital communication tools. These skills, while not core to our current daily tasks, will become essential competencies as our infrastructure evolves.

Specifically, I’d like to explore opportunities for myself and my colleagues to acquire training in transferable competencies like process automation software (e.g., RPA basics) and advanced spreadsheet modeling. These would directly enhance our ability to adapt to new systems, understand complex data sets, and identify efficiencies, thereby improving our contributions to the company’s strategic goals.

Investing in these areas now will align our team with emerging market demand for digitally proficient logistics professionals and strengthen our internal capabilities. I am keen to discuss this further and outline a potential timeline and resources for such development.

Sincerely,
Javier Rodriguez
Operations Coordinator

PREP

Preparation: Outlining Your Career Strategy

Teacher notes

Guide students to brainstorm and outline their own career strategy, mirroring the structure of the model text.
1. Encourage them to consider their own industry and role, and to think critically about future trends.
2. Provide the column banks as a resource for language.
3. Circulate and assist with idea generation and vocabulary selection.

Student handout

Building Your Future-Focused Vocabulary

Before you write your own proposal, brainstorm ideas and outline your plan based on your industry and role. Use the vocabulary banks to help you with appropriate professional language.

Brainstorming Questions:

1. What specific industry disruption or change do you foresee in your sector in the next 5-10 years?
2. What new skills or transferable competencies will be in market demand because of these changes?
3. What specific actions can you take for professional upskilling or personal branding in these areas (e.g., courses, projects, mentoring)?
4. How will this plan ensure your long-term viability and benefit your employer?

Free Practice: Drafting Your Career Progression Memo

Teacher notes

Instruct students to write their own memo or email based on their outline and the model text.
1. Remind them of the audience, purpose, and length requirements.
2. Circulate, offering support with structure and language, but avoid direct correction at this stage.
3. Emphasize using the professional register and relevant vocabulary.

Student handout

Your Proactive Skill Development Proposal

Now, draft an internal memo or formal email proposing your strategic skill development plan. Refer to the model text and your outline from the previous stage.

Your Writing Brief:

* Audience: Your manager or HR department.
* Purpose: To propose a plan for your professional upskilling and career development, anticipating future changes in your industry.
* Genre: Internal memo or a formal email.
* Length: Approximately 200-300 words.

Key points to include:

1. Briefly describe a potential future change or industry disruption relevant to your role.
2. Identify essential new skills or transferable competencies that will be in market demand.
3. Propose specific actions for your professional upskilling (e.g., courses, certifications, on-the-job training).
4. Explain how this plan contributes to your long-term viability and benefits the company.

FBDC

Feedback: Refining Your Career Strategy

Teacher notes

Guide students through peer review.
1. First, focus on content and clarity of ideas – do the proposals make sense and meet the brief?
2. Then, move to language accuracy and appropriate register, ensuring that the target vocabulary is used effectively.
3. Collect pieces for final teacher feedback or highlight common errors for whole-class correction.

Student handout

Peer Review: Enhancing Your Proposal

Exchange your memo/email with a partner. Read each other's proposals and provide constructive feedback using the points below.

Content Check:

1. Is the anticipated industry disruption clear and relevant to the writer's field?
2. Are the proposed skills and actions logical, practical, and well-explained?
3. Is the benefit to both the individual's long-term viability and the company's strategic goals clear?
4. Does the proposal sound proactive and well-reasoned?

Language Check:

1. Is the tone professional and appropriate for a manager/HR?
2. Are terms like professional upskilling, transferable competencies, market demand, personal branding, and long-term viability used effectively and accurately?
3. Are there any grammatical errors or awkward phrasing that could be improved?
