Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

Match the project management terms on the left with their definitions on the right.

  • critical path analysis — A method to identify the sequence of tasks that must be completed on time for the entire project to finish on schedule.
  • contingency reserve — Money, time, or resources set aside to cover unexpected costs, delays, or issues in a project.
  • scenario planning — The process of considering different possible future situations and how a project might be affected by each of them.
  • risk register — A document that lists potential risks to a project, detailing their likelihood, impact, and plans to deal with them.
  • project scope creep — When the requirements, objectives, or deliverables of a project expand beyond what was originally agreed, often leading to delays and increased costs.
  • unforeseen variable — An unexpected factor or element that was not predicted and can significantly affect a project's outcome.

Understanding the Core Issues

You will now listen to a conversation between a project manager, Maria, and a team leader, Ben. They are discussing an upcoming project. Listen for the main purpose of their meeting.

Choose the best option.

Materials

The Mid-Project Review Meeting

Maria: "Thanks for meeting, Ben. We're in the final planning stages for the new inventory system rollout, and I want to do a thorough risk register update. Before we commit, I need to know we've considered all angles. What's keeping you up at night about this?"

Ben: "Well, Maria, my biggest concern is avoiding project scope creep. We started with a clear goal: optimize stock levels and reduce manual tracking. But lately, I've heard talk about integrating the delivery route optimization, and then adding a customer feedback module. While these are good ideas, they weren't in the original plan and could push our timeline way out."

Maria: "Agreed. We need to hold the line on the initial scope. Any specific technical vulnerabilities you've identified? What about the integration with our legacy warehouse management software?"

Ben: "That's a definite unforeseen variable waiting to happen. Our current system is old, and even with extensive testing, there's always a chance of compatibility issues cropping up during live data migration. We also haven't fully nailed down the critical path analysis for the data transfer phase. If that goes wrong, everything else grinds to a halt."

Maria: "Right. And we absolutely need to factor in a solid contingency reserve for that. Not just financially, but in terms of manpower and alternative data entry procedures if the automatic transfer fails. Have you done any scenario planning for a complete system outage during the first week?"

Ben: "We've sketched out a few 'worst-case' scenarios, but nothing concrete. A full outage would be devastating, halting all outgoing shipments. We'd need a manual fallback procedure that could handle at least 50% of our daily volume, even if it's less efficient."

Maria: "Okay, let's put that high on the risk register. And what about training? The team isn't as tech-savvy as some of our other departments. Could that lead to operational delays?"

Ben: "Absolutely. User error during the initial phase is a high probability risk. We've got a training plan, but uptake might be slower than expected. This could effectively be another form of project scope creep if we have to divert resources to constant re-training."

Maria: "Good point. We need to ensure our contingency reserve also includes extra support staff for the first month. Let's make sure we document all these points and assign owners. We can't afford any surprises on this one."
