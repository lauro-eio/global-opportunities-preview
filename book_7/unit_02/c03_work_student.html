When Priorities Clash

Have you ever experienced a disagreement or conflict at work where your priorities clashed with a colleague's or another department's?

Think about a specific situation. For example, was it about:
* Scheduling: conflicting shift times, overlapping use of equipment, or leave requests?
* Workload/Overtime: differing expectations on project deadlines or additional hours?
* Safety Standards: a disagreement on procedures or protocols?
* Conflicting Targets: different departmental goals leading to friction?

Discuss with a partner:
1. What was the situation? Briefly describe it.
2. What caused the conflict? What were the opposing priorities?
3. How was it initially handled (or not handled)?
4. What was the outcome? Was a compromise reached?

A Productive Conversation

Read the following conversation between David, a production supervisor, and Emily, a logistics coordinator, who are trying to resolve a conflict over shared equipment.

David: Emily, do you have a moment? I need to discuss the use of the main forklift next week.
Emily: Certainly, David. What's on your mind?
David: My team absolutely needs that forklift all day on Monday to load the new custom orders. It’s a tight turnaround, and missing that window will mean significant penalties for us.
Emily: I understand the pressure, David, but that's going to be a real problem. My logistics team relies on that very forklift for the morning deliveries. We have strict client deadlines, and any delay impacts our entire supply chain.
David: I appreciate your priorities, and I respect the demands on your team, but this production run is critical for our quarterly targets. We can't afford to push it back.
Emily: And we can't afford to miss our delivery commitments. Perhaps there's a way we can compromise? Could your team start later in the day, or maybe we could split the usage?
David: Splitting the day could work, but it would make our loading process much less efficient. What if your team uses it from 6 AM to 10 AM, and then my team takes over from 10 AM until the end of the day?
Emily: That's a fair proposal, David. It would mean a bit of adjustment for my morning crew, but I think we can make that work. I appreciate your willingness to negotiate.
David: Thanks, Emily. I really value your flexibility. It helps us avoid unnecessary conflict and ensures we both meet our essential targets.
Emily: Agreed. It's about finding a solution that works for the whole operation.

Negotiating a Resolution

You are going to roleplay a workplace negotiation. Read your role card carefully. You and your partner have conflicting priorities and need to negotiate a solution that works for both of you.

Role A: Warehouse Operations Lead
Your team needs exclusive access to the main loading dock and pallet jacks all day Friday to prepare a large, urgent international shipment. The client is high-priority, and any delay will result in severe financial penalties and damage to your company's reputation. You cannot afford to lose more than two hours of access.

Role B: Inventory Management Supervisor
Your team has scheduled a mandatory, full-day inventory audit for Friday, requiring exclusive access to the main loading dock and pallet jacks. This audit is legally mandated and must be completed by Friday evening. Rescheduling would be a logistical nightmare and could lead to compliance issues.

Your Goal: Successfully negotiate a solution that addresses both your team's critical needs and your partner's. Aim for a fair compromise.

Use the phrases from the 'Tools for Tricky Talks' to help you.

Reflecting on Your Negotiation

Think about your negotiation:
1. Did you manage to reach a compromise or find a solution that satisfied both parties?
2. What was the most challenging part of the negotiation?
3. Which phrases from the 'Tools for Tricky Talks' did you find most useful, or wish you had used?
