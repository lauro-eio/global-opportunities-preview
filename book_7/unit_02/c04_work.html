L-IN

Lead-In: The Shifting Sands of Employment

Teacher notes

1. Write 'Career Security' on the board.
2. Ask students: 'What does career security mean to you? How important is it?' Allow pairs to discuss for a few minutes.
3. Bring class together and elicit some ideas.
4. Introduce the concept of temporary contracts, agency work, and side gigs. Ask: 'Has anyone here ever worked on a temporary contract, done agency work, or taken on a side gig to supplement their main income? What was that experience like?'
5. Encourage students to share briefly, focusing on the feelings of stability or instability, and the reasons for taking such work.
6. Transition by saying: 'Today, we're going to listen to someone discuss navigating these changes in the modern workplace and making career choices.'

Student handout

Your Work Story

We're going to talk about career security and different types of work arrangements.

Discuss with a partner:

1. What does 'career security' mean to you?
2. Have you ever worked on a temporary contract, through an agency, or had a side gig? What was that experience like?
3. What are the advantages and disadvantages of these kinds of work compared to a permanent, full-time job?

Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • career pivot — A significant change in professional direction or industry.
  • gig economy — A labor market characterized by temporary or freelance jobs, rather than permanent ones.
  • job security — The likelihood that an employee will keep their job without being dismissed.
  • insecure work — Employment that is unstable, temporary, or lacks sufficient benefits and protections.
  • fixed-term contract — An employment agreement that lasts for a specific, predetermined period of time.
  • upskilling — The process of learning new skills to improve job performance or career opportunities.
GIST

Gist Listening: Aisha's Career Journey

Teacher notes

1. Tell students they will listen to a woman named Aisha talk about her career.
2. Ask students to listen for the main idea: What is Aisha's primary message about modern careers?
3. Play the audio once.
4. Have students answer the multiple-choice question individually.
5. Check the answer as a class and briefly discuss why it is the main idea.

Student handout

Aisha's Main Message

You are going to listen to Aisha talking about her career. Listen once to get the main idea.

What is Aisha's main message about modern careers?

Materials

Aisha's Career Journey

When I started out, a job was usually for life. My first role was a full-time logistics coordinator for a manufacturing firm, permanent contract, steady progression. Then the sector started shifting. Automation meant fewer roles, and the pressure was on to upskill. I saw colleagues being let go or offered fixed-term contracts for specialist projects. It made me reconsider my whole path – it was time for a career pivot.

I decided to get certified in supply chain analytics. It was a big leap, financially and emotionally. The market for analytics specialists felt like a completely different world – lots of project-based work, much of it through agencies. It felt a bit like dipping my toe into the gig economy, even though I wasn't technically freelancing. My first few roles were short, three to six-month contracts. You're constantly networking, always looking for the next project. It certainly challenges your sense of job security.

But there’s also freedom. I’ve worked with three different companies in the last two years, each time learning new systems and problem-solving strategies. It's a trade-off: less permanence, but more varied experience. I've had to become more adaptable, more proactive about managing my own career trajectory. It’s not just about one company's ladder anymore; it’s about building a diverse portfolio of skills and experiences that make you valuable across different industries. It's a new kind of stability, one built on continuous learning rather than a single employer.

Tasks

What is Aisha's main message about modern careers?

  • Permanent jobs are no longer available in logistics.
  • Automation has made all jobs insecure.
  • Adapting to change and continuous learning are key to career stability.
  • Freelancing is the only way to find work now.

FBDC

Feedback and Discussion: Future-Proofing Careers

Teacher notes

1. Initiate a class discussion about Aisha's story.
2. Ask students if they relate to Aisha's experience or observations about job security and career pivots.
3. Discuss the 'trade-off' Aisha mentions – less permanence versus more varied experience. Ask: 'Would you make that trade-off? Why or why not?'
4. Ask students to consider: 'What strategies could workers use to manage insecure work or make a successful career pivot in their own industries?'
5. Briefly review any vocabulary items from the lesson that students found challenging or particularly useful.
6. Provide light feedback on students' listening comprehension strategies or use of target vocabulary during the discussion.

Student handout

Your Thoughts on Career Pivots

Let's discuss Aisha's story and what it means for today's workers.

1. Do you agree with Aisha's view of job security in today's world? Why or why not?
2. Aisha talks about a 'trade-off' between less permanence and more varied experience. What are your thoughts on this? Would you make that trade-off?
3. What strategies do you think are most effective for managing insecure work or making a successful career pivot in your industry?
