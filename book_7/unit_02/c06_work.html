L-IN

Lead-In: Communicating Operational Changes

Teacher notes

Introduce the concept of operational changes in the workplace and the need for clear communication. Guide learners to think about a small, practical change they've experienced or could imagine. Emphasize the audience (team members) and purpose (inform, reassure, instruct) of such a communication, steering away from abstract strategic discussions.

Student handout

New Procedures: Announcing Change

Imagine a small change is happening in your workplace – perhaps a new safety step, an updated checklist, or a revised shift handoff process.
1. What kind of small operational change could impact your team? Think of something practical and everyday.
2. Why is it important to communicate this change clearly to your team?
3. Who is the audience for this message, and what do they need to know or feel?
4. What might be some challenges in communicating this change effectively?

MOD

Model Analysis: Understanding an Operations Memo

Teacher notes

Learners will read a model memo announcing an operational change. Guide them to identify the key structural components, the tone, and how the memo ensures clarity and reassurance for the team. Discuss the professional register and how it addresses potential concerns without being overly formal or dismissive. Use the MCQ to check comprehension of these elements.

Memo Breakdown: Clarity and Reassurance

Read the following memo that announces an operational change to a team. Pay attention to how the information is presented and the tone used.

Materials

Memo: New Shift Handoff Procedure

To: All Operations Team Members
From: [Manager's Name]
Date: October 26, 2024
Subject: Important Update: Streamlined Shift Handoff Procedure Implementation

Dear Team,

This memo outlines an important update to our shift handoff procedure, designed to enhance clarity and efficiency across all shifts, starting November 4, 2024. Over the past few weeks, we've observed areas where our current handoff process could be improved, particularly regarding information transfer and resource allocation. Your feedback during recent team meetings has been invaluable in identifying these points.

The new procedure introduces a standardized digital checklist for each outgoing shift. This checklist will prompt you to cover all critical operational points, ensuring seamless continuity for the incoming team. Key changes include:

* Mandatory Digital Checklist: Available on tablets at each station. Completion is required before concluding your shift.
* Dedicated 5-Minute Overlap: This will be built into your schedule, allowing direct verbal communication regarding urgent updates or exceptions not captured by the checklist.
* Updated Logbook System: The physical logbook will now be used primarily for equipment maintenance reports and unusual incidents, simplifying its previous multi-purpose role.

We understand that any change to established routines requires adjustment. This new process is designed to minimize disruption while significantly improving our overall operational flow and reducing potential errors. Our aim is to provide a more organized and less stressful handoff experience for everyone.

Training sessions for the new digital checklist will be held on October 29th and 30th during regular shift hours. Attendance is mandatory for all operations team members. Please sign up for a session with your shift lead by October 28th.

Your commitment to adapting to this new procedure is crucial for its success. We are confident that these adjustments will benefit us all by creating a more efficient and reassuring work environment. If you have any questions or require further clarification, please speak with your shift lead or myself directly.

Thank you for your continued hard work and dedication.

Best regards,
[Manager's Name]
Operations Manager

Teacher notes

Guide learners in outlining their own memo. First, they should decide on a specific operational change. Then, brainstorm key information for each section. Provide useful phrases for different parts of the memo. Emphasize incorporating elements of clarity and reassurance based on the model. Free Practice: Writing Your Operations Change Memo

Teacher notes

Learners will draft their operations change memo. Remind them to apply the structure, tone, and phrases discussed in the previous stages. Emphasize clarity, professional register, and reassurance. The model answer in teacher_notes is a guide, not for direct student use, ensuring students generate their own content. Advise students to write approximately 150-200 words.

Student handout

Your Memo: Announcing New Operations

Now it's time to write your own operations change memo. Using your plan from the previous stage, draft a memo to your team.

Your Task: Write an operations change memo of approximately 150-200 words.

Audience: Your operations team.
Purpose: To clearly inform them about a new operational procedure, explain why it's happening, outline the changes, and reassure them while providing next steps.
Key points to include:
* A clear subject line.
* A concise introduction stating the change and its effective date.
* Bullet points for the key changes/steps.
* Language that addresses potential concerns and offers reassurance.
* Information about training or where to get more support.
* A professional closing.

Focus on being clear, direct, and supportive.

FBDC

Feedback and Correction: Refining Your Memo

Teacher notes

Organize learners into pairs or small groups for peer feedback. Provide clear criteria for feedback, first focusing on content (clarity, completeness, reassurance) and then on language (professional register, accuracy). Facilitate a whole-class discussion on common strengths and areas for improvement. Collect memos for final teacher feedback.

Student handout

Reviewing Your Message

Now that you've drafted your memo, it's time to get feedback and make improvements.

Part 1: Peer Feedback (Pairs/Small Groups)
Exchange your memo with a partner. Read their memo and consider the following questions:
1. Is the purpose of the memo clear? Do you understand the operational change being announced?
2. Is the memo easy to follow? Are the key changes clearly identified?
3. Does the memo sound professional and appropriate for the workplace?
4. Does it offer any reassurance or address potential team concerns?
5. Are there any parts that are unclear or could be expressed more simply?

Part 2: Self-Correction
Based on your partner's feedback and your own review, make revisions to your memo. Pay attention to:
* Clarity: Is your message direct and easy to understand?
* Completeness: Have you included all necessary information (what, why, how, when, who to contact)?
* Tone: Does it sound professional and supportive?
* Accuracy: Check for any spelling, grammar, or punctuation errors.
