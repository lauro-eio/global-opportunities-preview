Lead-In: Informing Workplace Change

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking learners about times they've heard significant news (good or bad) at work.
2. Prompt discussion: How was the news delivered? Was it formal or informal? Who delivered it?
3. Focus the discussion on how difficult or large-scale news (like company changes, new policies, or team restructures) is communicated.
4. Ask learners to compare how they might tell a close colleague informally vs. how an official announcement or memo would sound.
5. Emphasize discussing word choice and tone, not predicting future technologies or specific company changes.

Student handout

TCH

Teach: Analysing High-Register Lexis for Workplace Change

Teacher notes

1. Write the target exponents on the board: strategic foresight, market disruption, workforce transition, automation roadmap, reskilling imperative.
2. Elicit or explain the meaning of each phrase, linking them back to the memo where possible. For instance, 'strategic foresight' - the ability to anticipate future trends and prepare for them. Check understanding with CCQs (e.g., 'Is strategic foresight about looking at the past or the future?').
3. Discuss the register: Why are these phrases used in formal communication? What's the informal equivalent? Emphasize the professional, serious tone they convey.
4. Focus on pronunciation:
- Sentence stress: For compound nouns like automation roadmap and market disruption, stress the first part of the compound (e.g., AUtomation ROADmap, MARket disRUPtion).
- Clear articulation: Practice multi-syllable terms like 'imperative,' 'transition,' 'disruption,' 'foresight,' ensuring all syllables are clear.
5. Use the provided tasks to reinforce understanding and appropriacy.

T2

Test 2: Drafting a Section of a Change Communication

Teacher notes

1. Set the scenario: Learners are part of a team drafting an internal memo about a new change in their company.
2. Explain that they need to draft a short paragraph (3-4 sentences) using at least two of the high-register terms learned.
3. Emphasize maintaining a formal, professional, and empathetic tone suitable for internal change communications.
4. Circulate and monitor, noting common errors in usage, register, or pronunciation (if learners read their drafts aloud).
5. Provide delayed error correction at the end, focusing on the target language and register appropriacy.
6. Model answer example: 'Our recent strategic foresight indicates that to address ongoing market disruption, we must initiate a comprehensive workforce transition. This will involve developing a new automation roadmap to enhance our operational efficiency and ensure our continued competitiveness.'
