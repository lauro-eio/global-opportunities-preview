Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • career pivot — A significant change in professional direction or industry.
  • gig economy — A labor market characterized by temporary or freelance jobs, rather than permanent ones.
  • job security — The likelihood that an employee will keep their job without being dismissed.
  • insecure work — Employment that is unstable, temporary, or lacks sufficient benefits and protections.
  • fixed-term contract — An employment agreement that lasts for a specific, predetermined period of time.
  • upskilling — The process of learning new skills to improve job performance or career opportunities.

Materials

Aisha's Career Journey

When I started out, a job was usually for life. My first role was a full-time logistics coordinator for a manufacturing firm, permanent contract, steady progression. Then the sector started shifting. Automation meant fewer roles, and the pressure was on to upskill. I saw colleagues being let go or offered fixed-term contracts for specialist projects. It made me reconsider my whole path – it was time for a career pivot.

I decided to get certified in supply chain analytics. It was a big leap, financially and emotionally. The market for analytics specialists felt like a completely different world – lots of project-based work, much of it through agencies. It felt a bit like dipping my toe into the gig economy, even though I wasn't technically freelancing. My first few roles were short, three to six-month contracts. You're constantly networking, always looking for the next project. It certainly challenges your sense of job security.

But there’s also freedom. I’ve worked with three different companies in the last two years, each time learning new systems and problem-solving strategies. It's a trade-off: less permanence, but more varied experience. I've had to become more adaptable, more proactive about managing my own career trajectory. It’s not just about one company's ladder anymore; it’s about building a diverse portfolio of skills and experiences that make you valuable across different industries. It's a new kind of stability, one built on continuous learning rather than a single employer.
