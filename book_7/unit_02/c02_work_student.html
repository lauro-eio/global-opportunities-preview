Urgent Directives: Your Role on the Board

Imagine you are part of a senior advisory team. A critical report on market disruption and its impact on the workforce has just landed on your desk.

Discuss with a partner:
1. What makes a recommendation sound truly urgent and authoritative, especially to senior leadership?
2. How important is it for your language to be precise and leave no room for misunderstanding when dealing with high-stakes decisions like workforce transition or automation roadmap changes?
3. What kind of words or phrases might you use to express a strong demand or a vital suggestion?

Executive Summary: The Core Message

Read the 'Urgent Directive' memo from the Board of Directors. Don't worry about every detail for now, just try to understand the main purpose of the memo and the most pressing issue it addresses.

Materials

Urgent Directive: Addressing Automation's Impact on Workforce Readiness

To: Executive Leadership Committee
From: Board of Directors
Date: [Current Date]
Subject: Critical Workforce Adaptation & Automation Roadmap Review

Following the recent market disruption analysis and the subsequent urgent briefing from the Strategic Initiatives Division, the Board convenes this directive with immediate effect. It is imperative that we address the accelerating pace of digital transformation and its profound implications for our global workforce.

The Board insists that executive management develop a comprehensive reskilling imperative plan within the next ninety days. This plan must detail actionable strategies for workforce transition and talent redeployment across all affected operational units. It is not sufficient to merely monitor trends; we must proactively shape our future.

Furthermore, we recommend that the proposed automation roadmap be re-evaluated immediately. This re-evaluation must consider not only efficiency gains but also the societal and ethical responsibilities tied to large-scale technological shifts. A detailed report outlining a revised strategy, which ensures fair and equitable workforce transition, is expected by the end of the quarter.

It is absolutely vital that the Human Resources department, in conjunction with Operations, ensure that all affected employees receive clear communication and robust support structures during this period of change. The Board also demands that ongoing strategic foresight exercises integrate robust social impact assessments, moving forward. We must safeguard our people as we innovate.

We understand the scale of this challenge but also recognize the immense opportunity it presents to reaffirm our commitment to our employees and our long-term market position. The future of our enterprise depends that we act decisively and with collective resolve. Executive leadership is expected to present initial findings and a detailed action timeline at the next scheduled Board meeting.

Tasks

Choose the best summary of the Board's main concern in the memo.

The primary purpose of this directive is to:

  • Announce new market trends in digital technology.
  • Demand immediate strategic plans for workforce adaptation due to automation.
  • Congratulate the Strategic Initiatives Division on their analysis.
  • Review past performance metrics of the HR department.

Crafting Urgent Recommendations

The Board's memo uses very specific language to convey urgency and authority. Look at these sentences from the text:

1. 'The Board insists that executive management develop a comprehensive reskilling imperative plan...' (Paragraph 2)
2. '...we recommend that the proposed automation roadmap be re-evaluated immediately.' (Paragraph 3)
3. 'The Board also demands that ongoing strategic foresight exercises integrate robust social impact assessments...' (Paragraph 4)
4. 'The future of our enterprise depends that we act decisively...' (Paragraph 5)

In pairs, discuss:
- What is unusual about the verb form immediately after 'that' in these sentences? (e.g., 'management develop', 'roadmap be', 'exercises integrate', 'we act')
- Why do you think the Board uses this specific verb form rather than, for example, 'management develops' or 'roadmap is'? What pragmatic effect does it create?
- How does this structure reinforce the high-stakes, authoritative nature of the directive?

Putting Policy into Practice

Now, let's practice using this specific, authoritative language structure to formulate urgent directives. Remember the pattern: Subject + demand/suggest/recommend/insist + that + Subject + bare infinitive verb.

Rewrite the following sentences, transforming them into formal, urgent directives using the given verbs and the subjunctive form. Pay attention to using the bare infinitive.

Tasks

Rewrite the sentence as a formal directive using 'demand'.

The executive director must update the automation roadmap immediately. (Use: The board demands that...)

Rewrite the sentence using 'insist' and the correct subjunctive form for the negative.

The company should not compromise its position in global markets. (Use: It is vital that the company insists that...)

Transform the sentence into a recommendation using 'suggest' and the subjunctive.

All teams need to integrate strategic foresight into their planning. (Use: We suggest that all teams...)
