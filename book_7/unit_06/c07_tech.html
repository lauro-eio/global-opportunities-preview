Student handout

Mastering Report Language

When drafting high-level reports, especially on complex topics like AI governance, using advanced structures for emphasis and formality is crucial. One such structure involves fronting a prepositional noun phrase or a negative phrase, which often requires subject-auxiliary inversion.

Look at these examples and the rules:

1. Prepositional Noun Phrase Fronting: To emphasize the context or origin of an action or state, a complex prepositional phrase can be placed at the beginning of the sentence, followed by an inverted verb and then the subject.
Example: **Out of technological singularity arose new paradigms of virtual ethics.**

Concept Check: Is the word order altered to put the focal pre-modifier context first? (Yes)

2. Negative Fronting: When a negative phrase (like Under no circumstances, Never before, Hardly ever) is placed at the beginning of a sentence, the auxiliary verb often comes before the subject.
Example: **Under no circumstances can algorithmic governance ignore the philosophy of tech.**

Concept Check: Does a negative fronted phrase like 'under no circumstances' require subject-auxiliary inversion? (Yes)

Now, complete the practice sentences below to apply these structures correctly, ensuring your language remains formal and precise for an executive audience.
