Lead-In: AI's Role in Workplace Safety

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the unit theme: Ethical AI and its applications.
2. Present the lead-in scenario: AI could prevent workplace accidents/security breaches, but at what cost to privacy?
3. Divide students into small groups for initial discussion.
4. Facilitate a brief whole-class feedback session, noting initial opinions without correcting language.

Student handout

Predictive Tech: Safety, Security, & You

Imagine your company is considering implementing advanced AI systems – for example, facial recognition for secure access, or predictive software to flag potential safety hazards on the factory floor or in public-facing roles. The goal is to enhance safety and security, potentially preventing accidents or incidents before they happen.

Discuss the following questions with a partner:

Test 1: Evaluating AI Policy Language

Teacher notes

1. Distribute the 'Workplace AI Safety Initiative' memo.
2. Instruct students to read the memo and complete the multiple-choice tasks, focusing on choosing the most appropriate and professional phrasing for the context.
3. Emphasize that this is a diagnostic test; they should use their current understanding without any prior teaching.
4. Monitor for areas of difficulty related to register and specific lexical choices. Do not provide answers or explanations at this stage.

Internal Memo: Workplace AI Safety Initiative

TO: All Staff
FROM: Workplace Safety & Innovation Committee
DATE: 15 October 2024
SUBJECT: Proposal for Advanced Predictive Safety Integration

Dear Team,

We are exploring the integration of advanced AI-driven predictive analytics to bolster our existing workplace safety and security protocols. This initiative aims to proactively identify and mitigate risks, thereby creating a safer environment for everyone. For instance, new systems could monitor machinery for unusual patterns signaling potential malfunctions or use facial recognition for enhanced access control.

We understand that introducing new technology, particularly in areas concerning security and monitoring, raises questions. The committee is committed to an open dialogue and ensuring that any implemented system includes robust privacy safeguards. Our priority is to prevent incidents and protect our colleagues while respecting individual rights.

Your feedback will be crucial as we move forward. We will hold an open forum next month to discuss these plans in detail and hear your perspectives. Our ultimate goal is to foster responsible innovation that truly benefits our workforce.

TCH

Teach: Professional Lexis for AI Ethics

Teacher notes

1. Elicit answers to T1 tasks from students, but do not confirm correctness yet.
2. Introduce the target exponents one by one, writing them on the board.
3. For each exponent:
- Clarify meaning using CCQs (e.g., for 'ethical implications': 'Does this refer to just one good/bad outcome, or a broader range of considerations?').
- Discuss register: why is this phrase professional/formal? Contrast with the incorrect options from T1.
- Model pronunciation, focusing on stressed syllables and a slightly falling intonation for authoritative statements (e.g., ethical implications).
4. Use the 'Formal Expressions' column bank to present and clarify the exponents and their nuances.
5. Have students complete the matching task to consolidate meaning and appropriate usage.

T2

Test 2: Debate on AI in the Workplace

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into small groups (3-4).
2. Assign roles within each group (e.g., HR Manager, IT Security Lead, Employee Representative, Operations Manager).
3. Explain the task: As a company committee, they need to discuss and agree on key principles for implementing the new AI safety initiative.
4. Remind students to actively use the target lexical items (ethical implications, addressing algorithmic bias, upholding data privacy, fostering responsible innovation) throughout their discussion.
5. Circulate, monitoring for correct usage, appropriate register, and pronunciation of the target lexis.
6. Provide support as needed, but avoid direct correction unless it impedes communication significantly. Note errors for delayed correction later.
7. At the end, invite each group to briefly present their agreed-upon principles.

Student handout

Crafting Your Company's AI Policy

Your company is moving forward with the Workplace AI Safety Initiative. You are part of a special committee tasked with debating the specific details and outlining the principles that will guide its implementation. Your goal is to reach a consensus on how the AI will be used, balancing safety with employee rights.

Work in groups of 3-4. Assign yourselves the following roles:

* Operations Manager: Focus on efficiency, safety, and operational benefits of AI.
* Employee Representative: Advocate for employee rights, privacy safeguards, and fairness.
* IT Security Lead: Ensure technical feasibility, data security, and explain algorithmic bias risks.
* (Optional) HR Manager: Focus on policy, training, and employee well-being.

Discuss the following points and try to agree on at least three core principles for your company's AI policy. Make sure to use the key expressions we discussed today:
1. How will the company ensure upholding data privacy while using AI for monitoring?
2. What steps must be taken for addressing algorithmic bias to prevent unfair treatment or false positives?
3. How can the company demonstrate fostering responsible innovation in its approach to AI implementation?
4. What are the most significant ethical implications that need to be considered and communicated to all staff?
