Navigating AI Ownership: A Case Study

Read the following internal memo from 'Tech Solutions Inc.' and answer the questions that follow. This memo addresses a situation where AI-generated content has led to questions about ownership and company policy.

Materials

Internal Memo: Guidelines for AI-Assisted Content & IP

To: All Staff
From: Legal & Compliance Department
Date: October 26, 2024
Subject: Guidelines for AI-Assisted Content Creation and Intellectual Property Rights

Dear Team,

As our reliance on generative AI tools grows, it's crucial to establish clear guidelines regarding the authorship and ownership of content developed with their assistance. This memo outlines our current stance and best practices to avoid potential copyright disputes and ensure proper attribution.

Recent internal discussions have highlighted some ambiguities, particularly concerning projects where AI tools played a significant role in content generation, such as design proposals or marketing copy. To maintain originality and protect the company's intellectual property, please adhere to the following:

1. Disclosure: Any work submitted that incorporates substantial AI-generated elements must clearly state this. This includes reports, designs, code snippets, and marketing materials. A simple note indicating 'AI-assisted' or 'Generated with [Specific AI Tool]' is sufficient.

2. Ownership: Content developed by an employee using company-provided AI tools, for company purposes, is considered the property of Tech Solutions Inc. This aligns with our existing employment agreements concerning work product.

3. Attribution: While the ultimate ownership rests with the company, proper internal attribution to the employee who directed the AI and integrated its output is essential for performance reviews and project tracking. For external publications, the company will decide on appropriate disclosure.

4. Originality & Ethical Use: Employees are reminded that AI tools are aids, not replacements for critical thinking or ethical conduct. Ensure that AI-generated content does not inadvertently infringe on existing third-party copyrights. Always review AI output for factual accuracy and potential biases. The responsibility for the final content remains with the human creator.

5. Data Security: Exercise caution when inputting sensitive company data into external AI platforms. Only use approved internal tools or platforms with robust data privacy agreements.

We understand this is a rapidly evolving area. Your cooperation ensures we navigate these challenges effectively and protect our collective intellectual property rights. For further clarification, please contact the Legal & Compliance Department.

Sincerely,
The Legal & Compliance Department

FP

Freer Practice: Drafting an Ethical AI Report

Teacher notes

Remind students of the audience (manager), purpose (raise concerns, suggest solutions), and length.
Emphasize the need for a professional register and clear, concise language.
Circulate and monitor, offering support as students draft their reports/emails.
Do not correct errors at this stage; focus on content and structure.

Model Answer:
Subject: Ethical Considerations and AI-Assisted Content

Dear [Manager's Name],

I am writing to discuss a growing area of concern related to our department's increasing use of generative AI tools for [mention a specific task, e.g., report drafting/marketing materials]. While these tools offer significant efficiency gains, it's crucial we proactively address the ethical challenges, particularly concerning authorship and ownership.

My primary concern relates to potential ambiguity surrounding intellectual property rights for content that is heavily AI-assisted. Without clear guidelines, we risk future copyright disputes and issues with proper attribution.

I recommend we develop a departmental policy that outlines:
1. Requirements for disclosing AI assistance in submitted work.
2. Clear definitions of ownership for AI-generated output within company projects.
3. Protocols for ensuring originality and avoiding accidental infringement.

Establishing these parameters will safeguard our work, ensure professional integrity, and clarify responsibilities. I am happy to elaborate further and contribute to drafting such a policy.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Your Department]

Your Take: An AI Policy Recommendation

You are a team leader in a department that has started using generative AI tools (e.g., for drafting reports, creating initial designs, or summarizing data). Your manager has asked you to provide a brief internal report or email outlining how your department is currently using AI and to raise any potential ethical or practical concerns.

Task: Write an internal email or short report (approx. 150-200 words) to your manager.

Your report/email should:
* State the purpose of your communication.
* Briefly describe how your department is using AI.
* Identify one specific ethical challenge related to authorship, ownership, or intellectual property rights concerning AI-generated content.
* Suggest a practical step or recommendation to address this challenge.

Audience: Your manager (professional, respectful tone).
Purpose: To inform, raise a concern, and suggest a solution.
Length: Approximately 150-200 words.

Feedback & Correction: Refining Your AI Policy

Teacher notes

Pair students and distribute the feedback questions.
Instruct students to read their partner's report/email and provide constructive feedback focusing first on content, then on language.
Encourage students to use the target lexis from the lesson during their feedback.
After peer feedback, collect the reports.
Provide general feedback to the class, highlighting common strengths and areas for improvement regarding content, structure, and language.
Correct specific language errors (grammar, vocabulary, spelling) individually or by using anonymized examples on the board.

Student handout

Peer Review: Strengthening Our AI Ethics

Now that you've drafted your report, it's time to get feedback from a colleague and refine your writing.

Part 1: Peer Feedback (Content Focus)
Swap your report/email with a partner. Read your partner's work and consider the following questions. Provide constructive written feedback.
1. Is the purpose of the communication clear?
2. Does it clearly describe how AI is being used in their department?
3. Is the ethical challenge (e.g., regarding intellectual property rights, authorship, or ownership) clearly identified?
4. Is the suggested solution practical and relevant?
5. Is the tone appropriate for a manager (professional)?

Part 2: Self-Correction & Teacher Feedback (Language Focus)
Review your partner's feedback and make any content improvements. After this, your teacher will provide specific language feedback to help you polish your report for accuracy and professionalism.
