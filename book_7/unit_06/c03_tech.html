L-IN

Lead-In: AI at Work

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking students to briefly share any experiences with workplace monitoring, if comfortable (e.g., CCTV, clocking in/out systems, email scans).
2. Introduce the concept of AI-powered surveillance using the lead-in brief.
3. Divide students into pairs or small groups.
4. Give them 5-7 minutes to discuss the questions.
5. Open up for a brief whole-class discussion, noting down any initial feelings or strong opinions.

Student handout

The AI Eye: Workplace Monitoring

Imagine your every work action tracked by AI. This could include your computer activity, communications, physical movements, and even your tone of voice. We're going to examine the ethical boundaries of AI surveillance in professional environments.

Discuss the following questions with a partner:
1. What are some existing ways workplaces monitor employees, beyond just tracking hours?
2. How would you feel if an AI system monitored your every work action, from emails to breaks?
3. What potential benefits might a company see in using AI for employee monitoring? And what are the major concerns for employees?
4. Where do you think the line should be drawn between a company's need for oversight and an employee's right to privacy?

MOD

Model Dialogue: Debating AI Surveillance

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the model dialogue as a conversation between two colleagues discussing a new AI monitoring system being introduced at their logistics firm.
2. Ask students to read the dialogue individually, focusing on the main points each speaker makes.
3. Put students in pairs to discuss the gist comprehension questions.
4. Conduct a quick class check for comprehension, then move to the detail questions.
5. Assign the detail comprehension tasks (short text/MCQ) for students to complete individually.
6. Review answers as a class. Highlight any bolded vocabulary from the dialogue and check understanding.

Read the following dialogue between two employees, Maria and David, who work at a busy logistics company. They are discussing a newly announced AI-powered monitoring system.

After reading, complete the comprehension tasks.

Materials

New AI Monitoring System: A Discussion

Maria: Can you believe this new AI monitoring system they’re rolling out? It feels like we're about to be living in a digital panopticon.

David: I understand your concerns, Maria. HR presented it yesterday as a way to enhance productivity and identify bottlenecks, using advanced performance metrics. They say it will make our operations more efficient.

Maria: Efficient, maybe, but at what cost? To me, it's a blatant privacy invasion. Every keystroke, every communication, even how long we spend on a break… it’s too much. What about trust?

David: They did emphasize that the system is designed to analyze patterns, not individual incidents. And they assured us about data integrity – that the information collected would be secure and used solely for operational improvements.

Maria: Secure? Who has access to this data? And what about employee consent? We were informed, but did anyone really 'consent' when the alternative feels like falling behind or being viewed suspiciously?

David: Good point. The memo did state that aggregate data would be anonymous. It’s supposed to flag systemic issues, not micromanage individuals. But I agree, the idea of constant oversight is unsettling, even if the intentions are positive.

Maria: Intentions or not, it changes the dynamic. It feels like they're solving a problem of trust by removing trust entirely. I’m not convinced it will improve morale or genuine productivity.

David: Perhaps not. It certainly opens a big ethical can of worms.

Teacher notes

3. Go through the 'Key Vocabulary' column, ensuring students understand each term and how to use it in context.

FP

Freer Practice: Workplace AI Debate

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs (or groups of three, with one student acting as a neutral mediator/note-taker if preferred).
2. Assign roles: one 'Employee Representative' and one 'Company HR/Operations Manager'.
3. Reiterate the scenario and task. Emphasize using the phrases and vocabulary from the PREP stage.
4. Give students 15-20 minutes for their debate. Circulate, monitor for language use, and make notes for delayed correction, but do not interrupt fluency.

Student handout

The Big Debate: AI Monitoring at Work

Scenario: Your company is proposing to implement a new AI-powered monitoring system across all departments. This system would track computer usage, communication patterns, and efficiency data for all employees.

Task: In pairs, role-play the following situation:

Role A: Employee Representative / Shop Steward
You are deeply concerned about the potential negative impact on employee well-being, privacy, and trust. Your goal is to argue against the implementation of such an extensive system, or at least to negotiate significant safeguards and limitations. Focus on employee rights and workplace dignity.

Role B: HR Director / Operations Manager
You believe this system is crucial for improving efficiency, identifying areas for training, and ensuring fair performance evaluations. Your goal is to justify the system's implementation and address employee concerns while upholding company obligations. Focus on clear performance metrics and data integrity.

Debate the ethical boundaries and practical implications of this AI monitoring system. Try to find common ground or clearly present your opposing viewpoints. Remember to use the vocabulary and phrases you prepared!

FBDC

Feedback and Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Start with open-class content feedback: 'What were the key arguments from each side?', 'Were any compromises reached?', 'Which arguments were most persuasive?'.
2. Ask students about the challenges of the debate: 'Was it easy to argue your point?', 'Did you find it difficult to use the new vocabulary?', 'What did you find difficult about responding to counter-arguments?'.
3. Move to delayed correction. Write selected errors (from monitoring) on the board – focusing on pronunciation, lexical accuracy, and fluency – without identifying the student.
4. Ask students to correct the errors individually or in pairs.
5. Go through corrections as a class.
6. Praise good use of target vocabulary and effective debating strategies observed during the task.

Student handout

Take a moment to reflect on your debate.

2. What new vocabulary or phrases did you manage to use effectively?
3. What did you learn about the complexities of AI monitoring in the workplace from this discussion?
