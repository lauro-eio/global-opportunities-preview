Lead-In: Envisioning Urban Greening Contributions

Teacher notes

1. Begin by displaying the lead-in brief on the board or screen.
2. Ask students to read the brief individually.
3. Facilitate a whole-class discussion, encouraging students to share their initial thoughts and ideas.
4. Prompt students to think about specific roles or tasks their team in public works might undertake.
5. Encourage them to consider challenges and opportunities.

Understanding the Urban Greening Memo

Read the following internal memo from a municipal services department. Pay attention to its purpose, structure, and the technical language used.

Materials

Internal Memo: Proposal for Enhanced Stormwater Management

To: City Council Urban Development Committee
From: Head of Public Works, Environmental Services Division
Date: October 26, 2023
Subject: Proposal for Enhanced Stormwater Management through Green Infrastructure

This memo outlines a proposal for a comprehensive approach to improve our city's stormwater management systems, integrating green infrastructure solutions. The current drainage system, while functional, faces increasing pressure from extreme weather events, leading to localized flooding and increased pollutant runoff into our waterways. This initiative seeks to address these challenges proactively, enhancing our urban environment and public health.

Our division has conducted an extensive review of best practices in metropolitan areas with similar climate profiles. We propose the phased implementation of several key strategies:

1. Installation of Rain Gardens and Permeable Paving: Strategically place rain gardens in public parks and along arterial roads to capture and filter rainwater. Convert existing impermeable surfaces in city parking lots and public squares to permeable paving, allowing for natural water infiltration and reducing surface runoff.

2. Expansion of Urban Biodiversity Corridors: Integrate native plant species into new and existing green spaces, creating urban biodiversity corridors. These not only absorb excess water but also provide habitats for local wildlife and contribute to improved air quality. This will align with our city's long-term sustainability goals.

3. Community Engagement in Public Recycling Programs: Develop targeted workshops and volunteer initiatives to educate residents on the benefits of these new systems and encourage participation in extended public recycling programs that include green waste composting. Enhanced public awareness is crucial for the success and longevity of these projects.

Anticipated benefits include a significant reduction in flood incidents, improved water quality in local rivers, a noticeable increase in urban biodiversity, and greater community involvement in environmental stewardship. We have also projected a long-term cost saving due to reduced infrastructure repair needs.

We respectfully request the Committee's review and approval to proceed with a pilot phase for these initiatives in Q2 2024. Further details regarding budget allocation and project timelines will be presented upon request.

PREP

Preparation: Structuring a Proposal and Key Lexis

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into small groups.
2. Present the 'Proposal Structure' column bank. Discuss the function of each section (introduction, problem, proposed solutions, benefits, call to action).
3. Present the 'Useful Lexis' column bank. Ask students to identify which lexical items are from the previous reading and which are new.
4. Challenge groups to brainstorm additional relevant vocabulary or phrases for each section of a proposal, related to their specific area of public works expertise.
5. Encourage them to outline a potential proposal related to their own specific public works context, using the structure and some of the lexis.

FP

Free Practice: Drafting a Section of an Urban Greening Proposal

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the writing task clearly, ensuring students understand the audience, purpose, and length.
2. Remind students to refer back to the model text and the planning tools (structure and lexis banks) from the PREP stage.
3. Emphasize the importance of using professional register and incorporating specific lexical items related to urban environmental initiatives.
4. Circulate and monitor, providing support and answering questions as students write.
5. Remind students of the word count guideline and time limit. Encourage them to focus on getting their ideas down first, then refining the language.

FBDC

Feedback: Peer Review and Refinement

Teacher notes

1. Pair students up or assign small groups for peer feedback.
2. Instruct students to first read their partner's proposal for content: Does it clearly state its purpose? Are the proposed solutions logical? Are the benefits clear? Encourage constructive comments on ideas and clarity.
3. After content feedback, guide them to review for language: Is the tone professional? Are the new lexical items used appropriately? Are there any grammatical errors or awkward phrasing?
4. Circulate to provide teacher feedback, addressing common issues and offering targeted suggestions for improvement.
5. After feedback, allow students time to revise their proposals based on the comments received.
6. Optionally, collect revised proposals for further teacher assessment.

Student handout

Reviewing and Improving Proposals

Now that you've drafted your proposal, it's time to get feedback from a classmate and make improvements.

Step 1: Content Feedback (5 minutes per proposal)
Read your partner's proposal. Discuss the following questions:
1. Is the purpose of the proposal clear? What is the main idea?
2. Are the proposed actions or solutions logical and practical for a public works team?
3. Are the anticipated benefits clearly explained and convincing?
4. What is one positive aspect of the proposal's content?
5. What is one suggestion to make the content even clearer or more impactful?

Step 2: Language Feedback (5 minutes per proposal)
Now, focus on the language used:
1. Is the tone of the proposal appropriate and professional?
2. Are terms like green infrastructure, urban biodiversity, stormwater management, or public recycling programs used correctly?
3. Are there any grammatical errors, awkward phrases, or unclear sentences that could be improved?

Step 3: Revise Your Proposal
Based on your partner's feedback, make revisions to improve the clarity, impact, and accuracy of your proposal. Focus on incorporating relevant vocabulary and ensuring a professional tone.
