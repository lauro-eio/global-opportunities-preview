Match the terms with their definitions.

  • food waste reduction — Strategies and actions taken to minimize the amount of unused or spoiled food.
  • local procurement — The practice of obtaining goods and services from suppliers within a limited geographical area.
  • biodegradable packaging — Materials designed to decompose naturally into the environment without causing pollution.
  • water stewardship — The responsible planning and management of water resources to ensure their sustainable use.

First Listen: Understanding the Overall Goal

You will hear Maria, an operations manager, talking to her team about their hotel's new environmental goals. Listen for the main purpose of her meeting. Do not worry about understanding every word.

Materials

The Green Leaf Hotel: Aiming for Eco-Certification

Good morning, everyone. Thanks for coming. As you know, we're serious about achieving our 'Emerald Eco-Certification' this year, and that means looking at every aspect of our operations. I want to discuss some key areas where we can make significant progress.

First, food waste reduction. Our kitchen currently generates a considerable amount of organic waste. We're implementing a new system for portion control, and crucially, partnering with a local charity to redistribute edible surplus food. This isn't just about saving money; it’s about responsible consumption. We also need to get better at composting everything that can't be reused or donated.

Next, local procurement. We've been buying produce from all over the country. Moving forward, we're committing to sourcing at least 60% of our fresh ingredients from local farms and suppliers within a 50-mile radius. This reduces our carbon footprint significantly and supports the community. It might mean adjusting some menu items seasonally, but I believe our guests will appreciate the freshness and the story behind their meal.

Then there's biodegradable packaging. For our takeaway options and room service amenities, we're transitioning entirely to compostable or recyclable materials. No more single-use plastics. This includes everything from coffee cups to soap wrappers. It's a noticeable upfront cost, but the long-term environmental benefit and brand image are well worth it. We’ll need staff to educate guests where necessary, for example, on how to dispose of the new packaging correctly.

Finally, water stewardship. We've already installed low-flow showerheads, but there's more. We need to encourage guests to reuse towels, and more importantly, our kitchen and laundry teams need to be vigilant about water usage. We're investing in a greywater recycling system for irrigation, but daily habits are paramount. Staff training on efficient cleaning practices and leak detection will be vital. Every drop counts.

These changes are ambitious, but they're essential for our certification and for our commitment to global stewardship. Let's work together to make Green Leaf a true leader in eco-hospitality operations.
