Lead-In: Setting the Site for Sustainability

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the scenario from the lead-in brief, ensuring students understand the context of aggressive waste diversion targets on a construction site.
2. Elicit initial ideas and challenges from students regarding waste segregation and recycling.
3. Divide students into pairs or small groups for discussion using the provided prompts.
4. Facilitate a brief open-class feedback session, noting down any relevant vocabulary students use, but do not correct register or present target lexis at this stage.

T1

Test 1: Identifying Professional Language for Environmental Issues

Teacher notes

1. Distribute the memo. Ask students to read it and understand the main message.
2. Explain that the memo contains some phrases that are a bit too informal for a high-level internal communication about such a critical topic.
3. Instruct students to complete the multiple-choice tasks, choosing the most appropriate and professional alternative for each bolded phrase.
4. Do not provide answers or teach vocabulary at this stage. The goal is to diagnose existing knowledge of register and appropriacy.

Student handout

Site Update: Professional Communication Check

Read the internal memo below from a Project Manager to a Site Supervisor. The Project Manager needs to convey the seriousness of new environmental targets. Some of the language used is too informal for a professional context.

Choose the most appropriate and professional option to replace each bolded phrase in the memo.

Materials

Internal Memo: Project Horizon Waste Management Review

Subject: Urgent Review: Site Waste Management - Project Horizon

Hi Mark,

Following the recent site waste audit for Project Horizon, I need to bring your attention to some pressing concerns. Our current approach to waste segregation is a bit sloppy, and we're seeing too many materials getting mixed up. This is having a serious negative effect on our targets for waste diversion from landfills.

It's absolutely essential that we tighten things up. As you know, our organisation is committed to sustainable operational practices, and we must ensure our site performance reflects this. We're falling behind on our efforts towards cutting down carbon emissions, and there are some significant gaps in making sure we follow all environmental rules. Clients are increasingly scrutinising our resource efficiency initiatives, so our reputation is at stake.

Could you please schedule a mandatory briefing for your team by the end of the week? We need to clearly communicate the importance of these standards. Also, we must improve our thinking about the environmental cost of new materials, in line with our green building codes. Let's ensure everyone understands the urgency.

Best,
Sarah Chen
Project Manager

Tasks

Replace 'waste segregation is a bit sloppy' with the most appropriate professional alternative.

Our current approach to 'waste segregation is a bit sloppy'.

  • waste segregation is somewhat untidy
  • waste segregation lacks precision
  • waste segregation is a little messy
  • waste segregation needs tidying up

Replace 'negative effect' with the most appropriate professional alternative.

This is having a serious 'negative effect' on our targets.

  • damaging impact
  • bad outcome
  • detrimental impact
  • unfavourable consequence

Replace 'cutting down carbon emissions' with the most appropriate professional alternative.

We're falling behind on our efforts towards 'cutting down carbon emissions'.

  • reducing carbon output
  • carbon footprint mitigation
  • lessening carbon release
  • making carbon emissions smaller

Replace 'making sure we follow all environmental rules' with the most appropriate professional alternative.

There are some significant gaps in 'making sure we follow all environmental rules'.

  • conforming to green guidelines
  • being compliant with eco-standards
  • adhering to environmental regulations
  • obeying ecological laws

Replace 'thinking about the environmental cost' with the most appropriate professional alternative.

We must improve our 'thinking about the environmental cost' of new materials.

  • environmental spending analysis
  • green accounting considerations
  • ecological impact assessment
  • environmental expense evaluation
TCH

Teach: Mastering Professional Environmental Lexis and Register

Teacher notes

1. Review answers from T1, highlighting the chosen correct options.
2. Present the target exponents: 'sustainable operational practices', 'carbon footprint mitigation', 'adhering to environmental regulations', 'resource efficiency initiatives', 'ecological impact assessment'.
3. For each exponent:
a. Elicit its meaning, using concept checking questions (CCQs) to ensure understanding (e.g., 'Does 'carbon footprint mitigation' mean increasing CO2, or reducing it?').
b. Discuss its appropriacy – when and where would you use this phrase? (Formal, professional reports, meetings, documentation).
c. Model and drill pronunciation, emphasizing the primary stress within each key phrase (e.g., sustainable, carbon, adhering, resource, ecological). Focus on clear articulation.
4. Use the Column Banks to clearly contrast the formal target phrases with their informal or more general equivalents, further clarifying register.
5. Complete the clarification tasks, either individually or in pairs, to reinforce understanding of meaning and context.

T2

Test 2: Applying Formal Environmental Terminology in a Presentation

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs. Explain the task: one student takes on the role of a Site Supervisor/Project Manager, and the other is a visiting client or senior manager.
2. The 'Supervisor/Manager' prepares a 2-3 minute verbal update on their project's environmental progress, ensuring natural and accurate use of the target exponents. They should also be ready to answer questions.
3. The 'Client/Senior Manager' listens attentively, takes notes, and prepares relevant follow-up questions to ask after the update.
4. Circulate and monitor closely for accurate use of target lexis, appropriate register, clear articulation, and primary stress. Provide discreet error correction where necessary.
5. After the presentations, facilitate a brief open-class feedback session, highlighting strong examples of language use and common areas for further practice.

Model Answer Key Points (for teacher reference – not for student distribution):
- Introduction: Start with a formal greeting and clearly state the purpose of the update.
- Overview of Progress: 'I'm pleased to report significant advancements in our sustainable operational practices on Project Horizon.'
- Specific Examples: Discuss new waste segregation protocols, material reuse, and energy efficiency.
- Carbon Footprint: 'These measures are directly contributing to our carbon footprint mitigation efforts, reducing our overall emissions by [X] percent.'
- Compliance: 'We remain diligent in adhering to environmental regulations, ensuring full compliance with all local and national guidelines.'
- Efficiency: 'Our recent resource efficiency initiatives, such as the on-site water recycling system, have also proven highly effective.'
- Material Choices: 'All new material selections have undergone a rigorous ecological impact assessment to ensure minimal environmental impact.'
- Conclusion: End with a summary and offer to answer questions.

Student handout

Project Update: Sustainability Report

Your project has made significant strides in improving its environmental performance, particularly in waste management. Your task is to prepare a brief verbal update (2-3 minutes) for a visiting client or a senior manager on how your team is successfully implementing sustainable practices.

In your update, make sure to refer to:
- Specific sustainable operational practices you've adopted on-site.
- How your work contributes to carbon footprint mitigation.
- Your success in adhering to environmental regulations.
- The impact of recent resource efficiency initiatives.
- Any recent ecological impact assessments that guided your material choices.

Be prepared to present this update to your partner, who will act as the client/manager and ask follow-up questions.
