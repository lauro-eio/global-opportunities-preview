L-IN

Lead-In: Resource Scarcity Challenges

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking students if they have experienced or heard of raw material shortages affecting their or other industries.
2. Introduce the lead-in scenario based on the prompt.
3. Put students in pairs or small groups to discuss the questions.
4. Facilitate a brief whole-class feedback session to share initial ideas.

Student handout

Facing the Raw Material Crunch

Imagine your company is facing a sudden, significant increase in raw material costs due to global scarcity. This is impacting production budgets and profit margins.

Your production team needs to develop immediate strategies to minimize material waste and optimize usage without compromising product quality or production targets.

Discuss in pairs or small groups:
1. How would this scarcity directly affect your daily operations?
2. What immediate steps could your team take to identify and reduce waste in current processes?
3. What are the potential long-term benefits of implementing robust waste reduction strategies beyond cost savings?

MOD

Model Dialogue: Eco-Efficient Production

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will read a dialogue between two managers discussing waste reduction.
2. Ask students to read the dialogue to understand the main points and how they address the challenge.
3. After reading, use the comprehension task to check their understanding of the strategies discussed.
4. Do not pre-teach vocabulary. Address any comprehension issues after the task.

Student handout

Cutting Waste, Boosting Profits

Read the following dialogue between Maria, a Production Manager, and David, a Sustainability Coordinator, discussing how to tackle rising material costs.

Materials

Meeting the Scarcity Challenge

Maria: David, thanks for coming. We're really feeling the pinch with the new raw material costs. Our usual production process simply isn't sustainable anymore.
David: I understand, Maria. It's a common challenge right now. We need to shift our mindset towards lean manufacturing principles more aggressively. Have you considered a full audit of our current waste streams?
Maria: We've done basic waste checks, but nothing comprehensive. I'm thinking about setting up a cross-departmental team. What kind of strategies should we be focusing on?
David: Definitely closed-loop systems. Can we redesign any processes to reclaim and reuse materials internally? For instance, scrap metal from the cutting process – is there a way to melt it down and reintroduce it into production, rather than selling it off as low-value waste?
Maria: That's ambitious, but potentially very impactful. It would require significant upfront investment in recycling infrastructure. What about materials that can't be reused directly?
David: That's where waste valorization comes in. Can we convert that waste into something of value for another industry? Perhaps our sawdust byproduct could be pressed into composite boards for packaging, or even generate bioenergy. It's about seeing waste as a resource, not just a disposal problem.
Maria: So, it's not just about reducing, but also about finding new uses. And keeping track of everything through the entire process?
David: Exactly. We need to map out the entire material lifecycle for our key components, from sourcing to disposal or, ideally, reuse. This helps identify bottlenecks and opportunities for circularity. It's a significant project, but the long-term cost savings and environmental benefits are substantial. We could even look into partnerships with local recycling companies for specialized material processing.
Maria: It sounds like we need a comprehensive plan. This isn't just about cutting costs; it's about building a more resilient and responsible operation. Let's get that audit scheduled.

Tasks

Based on the dialogue, what are two main strategies David suggests to Maria for minimizing waste and optimizing resource use?

List two main strategies suggested in the dialogue.

PREP

Preparation: Strategy Brainstorm

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into groups or pairs.
2. Explain that they will now prepare their own strategies for waste reduction.
3. Direct students to the 'Useful Phrases' and 'Planning Your Strategy' sections.
4. Encourage them to use the provided lexical items and phrases.
5. Give them sufficient time for brainstorming and note-taking. Circulate and assist with vocabulary as needed.

FP

Free Practice: Proposing Waste Solutions

Teacher notes

1. Group students, ideally in threes or fours, assigning roles (e.g., Production Lead, Finance Rep, Sustainability Specialist). If pairs, they can act as consultants.
2. Remind them to use the phrases and concepts from the preparation stage.
3. The goal is fluent communication and negotiation of ideas, not perfect accuracy.
4. Circulate, monitor for communicative effectiveness, and note down language issues for delayed correction. Avoid interrupting fluency.
5. Ensure students focus on practical, actionable strategies relevant to a manufacturing context.

Student handout

The Green Production Meeting

You are now in a cross-departmental meeting to discuss and agree on strategies for reducing material waste and optimizing resource usage. Work in groups of 3-4.

Your Task:
1. Each person presents their best ideas and proposed strategies for tackling the problem of rising raw material costs and waste.
2. Discuss the feasibility, potential impact, and challenges of each proposal.
3. As a group, decide on at least two concrete strategies that your company should implement immediately.
4. Be prepared to explain your chosen strategies and their rationale to the rest of the class.

Remember to:
* Use the key concepts and phrases from the previous stage.
* Listen actively to your colleagues' ideas.
* Negotiate and reach a consensus.

FBDC

Feedback and Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Content Feedback (5-7 minutes):
a. Ask each group to briefly share their chosen strategies and the reasons behind them. Focus on the success of their communication and decision-making.
b. Comment positively on effective problem-solving and collaboration.
2. Language Feedback (5-7 minutes):
a. Refer to notes taken during the FP stage.
b. Write a few common errors (pronunciation, lexical use, grammatical inaccuracies that hindered communication) on the board or screen.
c. Elicit corrections from students. If they struggle, provide the correct form.
d. Focus on errors that affected clarity or were related to the target language of the lesson (discussing strategies, using specific lexis).
e. End with positive reinforcement on overall fluency and engagement.
