L-IN

Lead-In: Initial Risk Assessment

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking students about a significant project or shipment they've been involved with, either directly or indirectly.
2. Elicit initial ideas about potential problems that could arise during such an operation.
3. Present the core questions from the 'lead-in brief' and encourage pair or small group discussion.
4. Monitor for early use of relevant vocabulary and general fluency. Do not correct errors explicitly at this stage.

Student handout

Project Challenges: What If...?

Consider a major project you know about, perhaps a large delivery, a factory overhaul, or launching a new product. Even the best plans can hit snags.

Work with a partner and discuss the following:

1. What are some key 'what-if' scenarios or potential problems you would need to plan for in such a project or shipment?
2. How would you prepare for each of these scenarios to minimize disruption or damage? Think about contingency plans and risk mitigation.
3. What kind of unforeseen variables could emerge that might affect your plans?

MOD

Model Dialogue: Risk Mitigation Strategies

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the dialogue as an example of two managers discussing an operational challenge and their contingency planning.
2. Students read the dialogue silently or in pairs.
3. Ask students to identify the main problem being discussed and the solutions proposed.
4. Highlight any key phrases or vocabulary related to risk mitigation and scenario planning used in the dialogue.
5. Emphasize how the speakers articulate potential problems and solutions in a professional context.

Student handout

Hearing the Experts: Operational Briefing

Read the conversation between two project managers, Alex and Ben, discussing a potential delay for a critical shipment.

Alex: Good morning, Ben. I wanted to touch base about the upcoming East Coast shipment. Our last weather report indicated a high probability of severe storms next week, right when the vessel is scheduled to pass through. It's a significant operational risk.

Ben: Morning, Alex. Yes, I've seen that. We've initiated some scenario planning for it. Our primary contingency plan involves rerouting the vessel via the southern passage, which adds an estimated 48 hours to transit time.

Alex: And what's the impact assessment on that? Will we still meet our client's deadline?

Ben: Potentially, yes, but it's tight. We'd have to inform the client immediately about the potential delay and explain the risk mitigation strategy we're implementing. We also have a secondary plan to air-freight a portion of the most critical components if the delay exceeds 72 hours, though that comes with a much higher cost.

Alex: Okay, so we're considering both time and cost implications. What about any unforeseen variables? Is there a backup if the southern passage also becomes problematic?

Ben: That's where it gets complex. For now, the southern route looks clear. Our team is monitoring it continuously. If that changes, we'd need to reassess and perhaps look at splitting the cargo to different hubs, which introduces new logistical challenges. But we're working on it. The goal is to ensure minimal disruption to the supply chain.

Alex: Excellent. Keep me updated on any developments. Proactive communication with the client is key here.

PREP

Preparation: Structuring Contingency Plans

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now prepare to discuss their own contingency plans for a given scenario.
2. Direct students to the column banks, explaining that these are useful phrases to help them articulate their ideas.
3. Encourage students to think about a specific operational challenge in their workplace or industry.
4. Students should use the column banks to draft notes or bullet points for their plan, focusing on identifying the risk, proposing mitigation, and assessing impact.
5. Circulate and assist with vocabulary, focusing on the lexical_tags.

Student handout

Gearing Up: Planning Your Response

Now, think about a specific operational challenge or potential problem that could occur in your line of work or an industry you are familiar with. It could be anything from a machine breakdown to a staffing shortage, or a sudden change in regulations.

Use the phrases below to help you structure your thoughts and plan what you will say. Prepare to explain your ideas to a partner.

1. Identify the specific operational risk.
2. Outline your primary contingency plan for this risk.
3. Consider any unforeseen variables and how you might address them.
4. Describe the potential impact assessment if your plan isn't successful.

FP

Fluency Practice: Implementing Contingency Planning

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs or small groups.
2. Explain the task clearly: students will take turns presenting their operational risk and proposed contingency plan.
3. Encourage active listening and asking follow-up questions among students, using phrases from the 'General Discussion' column bank if needed.
4. Emphasize fluency and confident communication over grammatical accuracy at this stage. Intervene minimally to facilitate communication if necessary.
5. Remind students to use the vocabulary practiced, such as risk mitigation, scenario planning, and impact assessment.

Student handout

Live Scenario: Navigating the Unexpected

Work with your partner(s). Take turns presenting the operational risk you identified and the contingency plan you prepared.

As you listen to your partner:

* Ask questions about their plan.
* Suggest alternative solutions or consider other unforeseen variables.
* Discuss the potential impact assessment of their proposed risk mitigation strategies.

Remember to focus on clear communication and using the professional language from our preparation.

FBDC

Feedback and Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Start with general content feedback. Ask groups to share one interesting risk or a particularly effective contingency plan that was discussed in their group.
2. Elicit from students how effectively they felt they communicated their ideas and handled questions.
3. Transition to delayed language correction. Refer to notes taken during the FP stage, focusing on common errors related to the target language (e.g., mispronunciation of 'mitigation,' incorrect collocation with 'risk,' appropriate use of modal verbs for possibility/probability).
4. Provide anonymized examples of errors and elicit corrections from the class.
5. Conclude by reiterating the importance of clear and professional communication when discussing operational risks and contingency plans.

Student handout

Reflecting on Our Strategies

Let's reflect on the discussion. Share with the class:

* What was one significant operational risk or challenge that came up in your group's discussion?
* What was a particularly strong contingency plan or risk mitigation strategy that someone suggested?
* How effectively do you feel you articulated your ideas about scenario planning and impact assessment?
