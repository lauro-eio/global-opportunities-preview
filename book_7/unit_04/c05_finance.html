L-IN

Lead-In: Economic Ripples at Work

Teacher notes

1. Begin by presenting the lead-in scenario to the whole class.
2. Ask students to read the prompt and discuss in pairs or small groups.
3. Encourage students to connect the abstract economic decision (interest rate increase) to concrete, practical impacts within their own work experiences or industries (e.g., changes in customer spending, company loan accessibility, staffing).
4. Monitor discussions, noting any informal language used to describe formal concepts, which will be addressed in later stages.
5. Facilitate a brief whole-class feedback session, drawing out key ideas and initial thoughts.

Student handout

Your Workplace & Economic Shifts

Imagine the central bank has just announced an unexpected interest rate increase. This decision, while made at a high level, can have real and tangible effects on your workplace and the wider local economy.

Discuss in pairs or small groups:
1. How might this change affect your current job or a past role you've held? Consider areas like customer spending, company loans for expansion, pricing of goods/services, or even staffing decisions.
2. What might your manager or company leadership be discussing internally about these potential impacts?
3. How would you formally communicate these potential changes to colleagues, a team, or even clients? What kind of language would you use?

T1

Diagnostic: Register in Economic Updates

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the internal memo, explaining that it's a typical form of communication in many workplaces when significant news (like an interest rate hike) occurs.
2. Instruct students to read the memo quickly for gist, then more carefully to identify areas where the language might not be appropriate for a formal business context.
3. Direct students to complete the tasks, choosing the most appropriate or professional option for a formal internal communication.
4. Do not provide answers yet. The purpose of this stage is to diagnose existing knowledge and identify gaps in register awareness regarding economic discourse.

Internal Memo: Post-Interest Rate Hike Briefing

Subject: Urgent Update: Economic Shifts & Company Preparedness

Team,

Following the central bank's unexpected interest rate increase, our finance department has been busy getting their heads around the potential implications for our operations. While it's a bit of a headache for everyone, we've started to evaluate our projected growth rates for the coming quarter.

We anticipate some market volatility trends in consumer spending and raw material costs. This isn't exactly a great situation, and it points to an adverse economic outlook if we don't quickly sort out potential risks now. We'll be making some internal fiscal policy adjustments to our budget and spending plans. Expect further details soon.

Your understanding and cooperation are vital as we navigate these changes.

Regards,

Finance Department

TCH

Clarification: Formal Economic Language

Teacher notes

1. Begin by eliciting or providing the correct answers for the T1 tasks, explaining why the formal options are better in a professional context.
2. Introduce the concept of lexical register in professional communication, especially when discussing finance and economics.
3. Present the target exponents. Model each exponent, focusing on the provided phonological guidance: 'Emphasis should be placed on the key content words within these collocations to convey precise meaning and maintain a professional tone, often with a falling intonation for statements of fact or analysis.' (e.g., projected growth rates, fiscal policy adjustments, mitigate potential risks, market volatility trends, adverse economic outlook).
4. Use the column_banks to explicitly show the contrast between informal and formal expressions, checking understanding with CCQs (e.g., 'Is a 'projected growth rate' a guess or an informed prediction?' 'Does 'mitigate' mean to make something worse or less bad?').
5. Conduct drilling for pronunciation and word stress. Have students repeat the phrases individually and in chorus.
6. Guide students through the tasks, ensuring they understand the meaning, register, and appropriacy of each exponent.

T2

Freer Practice: Presenting a Company's Economic Stance

Teacher notes

1. Set up the role-play scenario, ensuring students understand their roles and the objective: to use the target exponents in a professional context.
2. Emphasize the need for accurate register, objective tone, and clear pronunciation of the target lexis.
3. Instruct students to think specifically about how the central bank's interest rate increase would impact their department or a similar workplace, encouraging them to be specific and relevant to their practical employment contexts.
4. Monitor pairs/groups, listening for correct usage of 'projected growth rates', 'fiscal policy adjustments', 'mitigate potential risks', 'market volatility trends', and 'adverse economic outlook'.
5. Note down any persistent errors for later correction (e.g., delayed error correction at the end of the activity).
6. Provide support and prompting as needed, for example, by asking 'How could you phrase that more formally?' or 'Can you incorporate one of the new phrases here?'
7. Model response idea: 'Good morning, team. Regarding the central bank's recent decision, we need to prepare for potential fiscal policy adjustments impacting our operational budget next quarter. Our initial projected growth rates may need revision, so it's crucial we discuss strategies to mitigate potential risks stemming from the anticipated market volatility trends. While the current adverse economic outlook requires careful planning, I am confident we can navigate these challenges effectively through proactive measures.'

Student handout

Reporting on Economic Impact

You are a team leader in your department (e.g., logistics, sales, operations). Your manager has asked you to prepare a brief internal update for your team, explaining the potential impact of the central bank's recent interest rate increase on your specific department's operations or future plans.

In pairs, take turns being the 'team leader' and 'team member'. The team leader should use as many of the formal expressions we've learned (e.g., projected growth rates, fiscal policy adjustments, mitigate potential risks, market volatility trends, adverse economic outlook) as possible to convey a clear, professional, and objective assessment of the situation.

Team members should listen carefully and ask clarifying questions using polite, professional language.

Think about how this economic shift might affect daily tasks, budgeting, customer interactions, or supplier relationships in your area.
