Lead-In: Global Shifts, Local Impacts

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and briefly introduce the topic of global economic events.
2. Present the first discussion question to the class. Allow a moment for students to recall recent news.
3. Ask students to discuss the questions in pairs or small groups (3-4 minutes per question).
4. Facilitate a brief whole-class feedback session, drawing connections between their personal experiences and the broader economic context.

Student handout

Connecting World Events to Our Work

We often hear about big economic news on TV – like a shipping canal being blocked, or a factory moving to another country. Let's discuss:

1. Have you heard about any recent international economic news? What was it about?
2. How do you think an event like that, happening far away, could eventually affect your job, your company, or your industry right here?
3. What kind of changes might you see in your workplace if, for example, the cost of materials from abroad suddenly increased, or a big overseas customer stopped buying products from your country?

Vocabulary
  • global supply chains
  • trade tariffs
  • labor market dynamics
  • economic indicators
  • job security
Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • global supply chains — The worldwide network that moves products from raw materials to the customer, often crossing many countries.
  • trade tariffs — Taxes added to goods imported from other countries, making them more expensive.
  • labor market dynamics — The way that job availability, wages, and the number of workers changes over time in an economy.
  • economic indicators — Official statistics (like unemployment rates or sales figures) that show how well an economy is doing.
  • job security — How likely it is that an employee will keep their job, based on the company's health or wider economic conditions.

Gist Reading: Understanding the Main Idea

Teacher notes

1. Instruct students to read the article quickly to get the main idea, without focusing on every detail.

Quick Read: What's the Article About?

Read the article below quickly to understand its main message. Don't worry about every single word. Then, choose the best title for the article.

Materials

The Unseen Cost: How Global Tariffs Hit Local Workshops

For decades, FurniCraft, a medium-sized furniture manufacturer in the Midlands, has been a pillar of the community, providing steady employment for over fifty local residents. Their success was built on sourcing high-quality, affordable timber and fabrics through global supply chains, primarily from Southeast Asia. However, recent international trade tariffs imposed by several major economies have significantly driven up the cost of these essential imported materials.

'We've always prided ourselves on fair prices for our customers and fair wages for our team,' says Maria Rodriguez, FurniCraft's operations manager. 'But with a 25% tariff on our raw timber, our production costs have skyrocketed. We can't simply pass all of that onto the customer, or we'll lose business.'

This squeeze is putting immense pressure on the company. FurniCraft's financial team closely monitors various economic indicators, but the outlook remains challenging. While they're exploring local alternatives for materials, these are often more expensive or don't meet their production volume needs.

The immediate impact is being felt by the workforce. Overtime hours have been cut, and a planned expansion that would have created new roles has been put on hold. There's a growing sense of unease regarding job security among the skilled carpenters and upholsterers. The company has also had to reconsider its hiring plans, slowing down the usual churn and reducing opportunities for new apprentices. This directly affects labor market dynamics in the region, as fewer entry-level positions are available, and experienced workers worry about their future.

FurniCraft’s story is not unique. Many local businesses that rely on imported goods or export their products are facing similar challenges, demonstrating how quickly distant economic policies can create tangible local hardship. It’s a stark reminder that in an interconnected world, a policy decision in one country can echo globally, impacting the livelihoods of workers thousands of miles away.

Tasks

Which statement best describes the main idea of the article?

  • FurniCraft is planning to expand its operations globally.
  • Local companies face challenges and job insecurity due to international trade policies.
  • The furniture industry is thriving despite global economic issues.
  • Maria Rodriguez is confident about finding cheap local material alternatives.
DET

Detailed Reading: Analyzing Specifics and Implications

Teacher notes

1. Instruct students to re-read the article carefully to find specific details.
2. Students answer the 'short text' comprehension questions individually or in pairs.
3. Check answers for the comprehension questions as a class.
4. Divide students into small groups for discussion of the open-ended prompts in 'Student Content'.
5. Circulate and monitor discussions, offering support and noting any interesting language use.
6. Lead a whole-class feedback session for the discussion questions, noting down key points and encouraging use of target vocabulary.

Model answers for discussion questions:
1. Why pass on costs: Customers have choices, can't afford higher prices, competitors might not raise prices as much, company risks losing market share.
2. Long-term solutions: Innovate product design to use less material, invest in automation for efficiency, negotiate long-term contracts with diverse suppliers (local and international), seek government support/subsidies, explore new niche markets.
3. Effect on other local businesses: Reduced spending by FurniCraft employees in local shops, decreased orders for local service providers (e.g., transport, maintenance), potential ripple effect on local labor market dynamics if FurniCraft reduces workforce further, possibly leading to a downturn in the local economy.

FBDC

Feedback and Consolidation: Discussing the Wider Impact

Teacher notes

1. Lead a whole-class discussion using the prompts in 'Student Content'. Encourage all students to participate and express their opinions.
2. Provide content feedback, summarizing the key takeaways from the discussion and linking back to the reading.
3. Offer brief language notes: praise effective use of new vocabulary (e.g., global supply chains, trade tariffs, job security), address any common pronunciation issues with new terms, and highlight strong communicative phrases.
4. Do not focus on explicit grammar correction.

3. What have you learned from today's discussion and reading that might change how you view news about international economic events?
