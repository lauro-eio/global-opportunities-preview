Lead-In: Global Supply Chain Challenges

Teacher notes

1. Begin by eliciting the importance of imported raw materials for various businesses.
2. Introduce the concept of cross-border logistics and the complexities involved.
3. Present the lead-in scenario and guide students through the discussion points.
4. Encourage students to share their hypothetical scenarios and proposed solutions, focusing on professional communication.
5. Note down any emergent vocabulary related to supply chain or finance.

Student handout

Navigating International Trade Risks

Your company relies on imported raw materials. Imagine a critical shipment is delayed or becomes unexpectedly expensive.

Discuss the following with a partner:
1. Describe a hypothetical scenario where a misunderstanding in contractual terms (e.g., who pays for shipping, insurance) or a sudden currency shift (e.g., the value of the foreign currency changes) severely impacts your supply chain.
2. How would this impact your company's operations and finances?
3. How would you professionally address this situation with your suppliers and internal teams?
4. What steps could you take to prevent such issues in the future?

Tasks

Match each term related to global economics and risk analysis to its correct definition.

Match the terms to their definitions:

  • customs_declarations — Official documents detailing goods being imported or exported, used for assessing duties and ensuring regulatory compliance.
  • Incoterms_2020 — Internationally recognized rules that define the responsibilities of sellers and buyers for the delivery of goods under sales contracts.
  • payment_terms — The agreed-upon conditions under which payment is to be made for goods or services, including timing and method.
  • foreign_exchange_fluctuations — Changes in the relative value of one currency compared to another, impacting the cost of international transactions.
  • supply_chain_resilience — The ability of a supply chain to anticipate, withstand, and recover from disruptions, adapting to maintain operations.
GIST

Gist Listening: Identifying Core Challenges in Global Procurement

Teacher notes

1. Explain to students that they will listen to a conversation about a problem in cross-border logistics.
2. Instruct them to listen for the main problem being discussed, without focusing on details.
3. Play the audio once.
4. Have students complete the multiple-choice question to check their overall understanding.
5. Conduct a quick whole-class check for the answer, asking students why they chose their option.

Student handout

First Listen: What's the Main Issue?

You will hear a conversation between Elena, a Procurement Manager, and Mark, a Finance Specialist. Listen once to understand the main issue they are discussing. Then, choose the best option to summarize the core problem.

Materials

Crisis in Copper: A Procurement and Finance Briefing

(Opening scene: Elena and Mark are on a video call, looking stressed.)

Elena: Thanks for making time, Mark. We have a significant issue with the latest copper shipment from Chile. The invoice from 'Andean Resources' came in, and it's considerably higher than anticipated, even with the agreed-upon **payment_terms**.

Mark: I saw the alert come through. It looks like a classic case of **foreign_exchange_fluctuations** hitting us harder than our hedging strategy accounted for this quarter. The Chilean Peso has strengthened significantly against the Euro since we locked in the last contract.

Elena: Exactly. And that's compounded by the freight charges. We assumed DAP (Delivered at Place) according to our standard operating procedure, but their updated **Incoterms_2020** declaration for this particular batch seems to indicate EXW (Ex Works), pushing the responsibility for main carriage and insurance onto us.

Mark: EXW? That's a massive oversight on their part, or ours, if we didn't flag it during the negotiation review. That changes everything from a cost and risk perspective. Have you checked the **customs_declarations** carefully? Sometimes the Incoterm is explicitly stated there, and it might contradict the initial commercial invoice if there's been a miscommunication.

Elena: I've scrutinized them. The **customs_declarations** align with EXW for this specific consignment. It means we’re liable for all costs and risks until the goods arrive at our warehouse, starting from their factory gate in Santiago. That includes export packaging, loading, domestic transport in Chile, port handling, main carriage, insurance, and import clearance in Rotterdam. This isn't just about the currency; it's a fundamental misunderstanding of contractual obligations.

Mark: This is precisely why **Incoterms_2020** clarity is paramount. A single three-letter abbreviation can shift hundreds of thousands in costs. We need to review the original procurement contract for 'Andean Resources'. Is there a discrepancy between the master agreement and this specific purchase order?

Elena: That's my next step. But beyond this immediate crisis, it highlights a vulnerability in our **supply_chain_resilience**. If a critical raw material shipment can be so easily impacted by both currency shifts and ambiguous contractual terms, we need more robust checks.

Mark: Absolutely. We need to initiate a formal dispute with Andean Resources, politely but firmly. We should reference the master contract and previous consignments where DAP was clearly stipulated. At the same time, our finance department can explore options for short-term currency swaps to mitigate immediate losses from the **foreign_exchange_fluctuations. For the future, tighter integration between procurement, legal, and finance on every** new supplier contract is non-negotiable.

Elena: Agreed. And we need to clarify our **payment_terms** for future contracts to explicitly link them with the Incoterm used. Perhaps moving to a Letter of Credit (LC) for high-value imports could offer more security, though it adds administrative overhead. The priority now is getting this copper shipment cleared without further delays or unexpected charges, and then rectifying the contractual framework to prevent recurrence. This kind of misstep can seriously erode profitability and trust.

Mark: Right. I'll draft a formal communication for Andean Resources, outlining our concerns regarding the Incoterms discrepancy. You focus on expediting the customs clearance under the current, unfortunate EXW terms to minimize disruption. Let's reconvene end of day.

Elena: Will do. Thanks, Mark.

(End of conversation.)

Tasks

What is the primary challenge Elena and Mark are discussing regarding the copper shipment?

The core problem they are facing is:

  • The copper quality is substandard, leading to production delays.
  • A combination of unexpected currency shifts and a dispute over shipping responsibilities.
  • Their main supplier has gone out of business, requiring a new source.
  • The shipment has been lost in transit and cannot be traced.
DET

Detailed Listening: Pinpointing Specific Contractual and Financial Risks

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will listen again, this time for specific details.
2. Instruct students to read the questions before listening to know what information to focus on.
3. Play the audio a second time.
4. Give students time to complete the short-text questions. Encourage them to note down key phrases or numbers.
5. Conduct a whole-class feedback session, reviewing answers and discussing specific parts of the transcript if necessary.
6. For the final inference question, allow pair discussion before a whole-class share, focusing on the reasoning behind their answers.

Student handout

Second Listen: Digging into the Details

Listen to the conversation again. This time, answer the questions below with specific information from the dialogue.

1. What specific Incoterm did the company assume, and which Incoterm was declared for the current shipment?
2. What is the main reason for the unexpected increase in the invoice due to currency issues?
3. According to Mark, what broad implications does the EXW Incoterm have for the company regarding cost and risk?
4. What long-term vulnerability in the company's operations does Elena identify as a result of this issue?
5. Beyond the immediate problem, what strategic changes do Elena and Mark suggest for future contracts to enhance security?

FBDC

Follow-Up Discussion: Professional Problem-Solving

Teacher notes

1. Lead a discussion, allowing students to react to the content of the conversation.
2. Connect the listening scenario back to the initial lead-in discussion and students' hypothetical situations.
3. Ask students to evaluate Elena and Mark's approach to the problem.
4. Facilitate a discussion on the importance of clear contractual terms and risk mitigation strategies.
5. Provide brief feedback on any language points (vocabulary, pronunciation of key terms) that arose during the activity.

Student handout

Applying Your Understanding

Reflect on the conversation between Elena and Mark, and discuss the following:

1. How effectively did Elena and Mark identify and analyze the problems? What were their immediate priorities?
2. What does this conversation teach us about the importance of precision when negotiating international contracts, especially regarding **Incoterms_2020 and payment_terms**?
3. Besides the immediate actions, what broader strategies did they propose to improve **supply_chain_resilience**?
4. Imagine you are Elena or Mark. How would you communicate this situation to senior management? What solutions would you propose for the short-term and long-term?
5. What lessons can your own company or industry learn from this scenario regarding managing **foreign_exchange_fluctuations and customs_declarations**?
