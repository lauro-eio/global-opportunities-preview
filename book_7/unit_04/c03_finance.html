L-IN

Lead-In: Operational Cost Challenge

Teacher notes

1. Write the '10% cost reduction target' on the board.
2. Divide students into pairs or small groups.
3. Give them 3-4 minutes to discuss the prompt, encouraging them to think about their own work contexts or hypothetical scenarios.
4. Bring the class together for a brief whole-class feedback session, asking groups to share their initial thoughts.

Student handout

Our Department's Cost Challenge

Imagine your department has been given a critical task: reduce operational costs by 10% this quarter. This is a significant challenge, and your team needs to act quickly.

Discuss with your partner:
1. Which specific section of a financial report (e.g., income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement) would you scrutinize first to identify potential areas for cutbacks or optimization?
2. Why would you start there?
3. What kind of expenditure or revenue streams would you typically find in that section that might offer opportunities for savings or improvement?

MOD

Model: Analyzing Expenditure Reports

Teacher notes

1. Tell students they will listen to/read a short dialogue between two colleagues discussing cost reduction.
2. Play the audio or have students read the dialogue individually.
3. Give students the comprehension tasks.
4. Check answers as a class, clarifying any unfamiliar vocabulary, but avoid explicit grammar teaching.

Student handout

Listening to Financial Discussions

Read the following conversation between Maria, a Production Manager, and David, a Senior Team Lead, as they discuss their Q3 financial review.

Maria: "David, thanks for coming in. The Q3 figures are out, and we're looking at a serious push for cost efficiency. Senior management wants a 10% reduction across the board. I've been poring over the income statement and our recent expenditure analysis."

David: "No problem, Maria. I saw the initial report. My first thought went straight to our operating expenses. We've had a consistent rise in logistics costs due to supplier changes and fuel price volatility. That seems like a prime area for immediate review."

Maria: "Agreed. Also, looking at the balance sheet, I noticed our inventory carrying costs are up. We might be holding too much stock, impacting our profit margins. We need better budgetary oversight there."

David: "Absolutely. I also think we should review our supplier contracts. There might be opportunities to renegotiate terms or explore alternative suppliers to reduce our cost of goods sold. It's all about extracting actionable insights from these reports."

Maria: "Exactly. And the cash flow report shows a slower return on some of our receivables. We need to tighten that up to improve our overall resource allocation."

David: "It's going to be challenging, but with focused expenditure analysis and smart resource allocation, I'm confident we can hit that 10% target."

PREP

Preparation: Brainstorming Solutions

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now prepare for a role-play about addressing a cost reduction target.
2. Direct students to the 'Useful Phrases' and 'Areas for Review' sections.
3. Give them 5-7 minutes to brainstorm ideas and make notes for their assigned roles (to be given in FP stage) using these phrases and their own ideas.
4. Circulate and assist with vocabulary and ideas, but avoid correcting language errors directly at this point.

FP

Fluency Practice: The Cost Reduction Meeting

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into groups of 3-4. Assign roles: one student acts as the 'Department Head' and the others are 'Team Leads' from different sub-departments (e.g., Logistics, Procurement, Operations, Sales).
2. Explain the scenario: The Department Head has called a meeting to discuss proposals for achieving the 10% cost reduction target. Team Leads must present their findings and proposals based on their departmental financial data (hypothetical).
3. Emphasize fluency and communication over accuracy at this stage. Encourage students to use the phrases from the PREP stage.
4. Give students 10-12 minutes for the discussion. Circulate, monitor, and make notes on language for delayed correction, but do not interrupt fluency.

Student handout

The Q4 Cost Strategy Meeting

Your department is holding its quarterly strategy meeting. The main agenda item is achieving the 10% operational cost reduction target. Each of you has reviewed hypothetical financial data for your area and needs to present your findings and proposals.

Roles:
* Department Head: Lead the meeting, listen to proposals, ask questions, guide the discussion towards actionable solutions.
* Team Lead (e.g., Operations/Logistics/Sales/Procurement): Present your analysis of your area's financial data, identify potential cost-saving opportunities, and propose specific actions. Be prepared to justify your proposals and discuss their impact on operational efficiency and profit margins.

Your Task:
Work together to reach a consensus on at least three concrete strategies for achieving the 10% cost reduction. Focus on drawing actionable insights from your 'financial statements' and discussing effective resource allocation.

Remember to listen to your colleagues, ask clarifying questions, and build on each other's ideas to develop a robust plan.

FBDC

Feedback & Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Content Feedback (5-7 mins):
a. Ask each group to report back on the 2-3 key strategies they agreed upon to achieve the cost reduction target. Discuss the viability and practicality of their solutions.
b. Facilitate a brief whole-class discussion on which strategies seemed most effective or innovative.
2. Language Feedback (5-7 mins):
a. Write common errors or good language examples you noted during the FP stage on the board (without identifying specific students).
b. Elicit corrections or ask students to identify the good examples and explain why they were effective.
c. Focus on errors that hindered communication or were related to the target language of the lesson (e.g., misuse of financial terms, less precise phrasing).
d. Provide positive reinforcement for effective communication and use of new vocabulary.

Student handout

Reflecting on Our Strategy

Now that you've completed your strategy meeting, let's reflect on your discussions and the language you used.

1. Content Reflection:
* What were the most challenging aspects of reaching a consensus?
* Which of your proposed strategies do you think would be most effective in a real-world scenario?
* Did you feel you successfully identified actionable insights for operational efficiency?

2. Language Focus:
* Think about the phrases and vocabulary we practised in the PREP stage. Did you manage to use them effectively?
* Were there any moments during your discussion where you struggled to express a financial concept or proposal clearly?
* What specific financial terms or phrases did you find most useful during your discussion?
