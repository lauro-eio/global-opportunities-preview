Supporting Our Team: A Discussion

Imagine an employee whose close family member requires full-time palliative care. This employee needs extended leave to provide end-of-life care.

Discuss the following questions with a partner or in a small group:

1. What ethical responsibilities, if any, do employers bear in supporting staff through such profoundly challenging personal circumstances?
2. What kind of employee support might be necessary in this situation, beyond just time off?
3. What challenges might a company face when an employee requires compassionate leave for an extended period?
4. How can a workplace balance the needs of the individual employee with the needs of the team and the business?

Understanding Company Communication

Read the internal memo below from HR regarding the company's compassionate leave policy. Pay attention to its purpose, tone, and how it addresses supporting employees during difficult times.

Materials

Internal Memo: Supporting Employees Through Personal Challenges

Date: October 26, 2024
To: All Department Managers
From: Human Resources Department
Subject: Enhancing Our Employee Support for Extended Leave

Dear Managers,

We are writing to clarify and reinforce our commitment to employee support, particularly concerning situations requiring extended leave for end-of-life care for a close family member. We understand that these are profoundly challenging personal circumstances, and our goal is to ensure our policies reflect both our ethical responsibilities and a supportive workplace culture.

Our updated bereavement policy and family medical leave guidelines are designed to provide clear frameworks while allowing for flexibility and empathy. For situations involving end-of-life care, employees may be eligible for a combination of compassionate leave, family medical leave, and flexible working arrangements. This approach aims to support their work-life balance during critical times.

Key points for managers:

1. Open Communication: Encourage employees to speak openly with HR and their immediate manager about their needs as early as possible. This helps us plan for coverage and offer appropriate assistance.
2. Resource Guidance: Direct employees to HR for detailed information on their leave entitlements, available company resources, and external support networks.
3. Team Planning: Work with your team and HR to manage workloads effectively during an employee's absence. This might involve temporary reassignments or flexible scheduling for remaining team members, ensuring continuous operational efficiency while minimizing stress on those providing cover.
4. Reintegration Support: Upon an employee’s return, initiate a supportive reintegration process. This could include a phased return to work or a review of their workload to ease the transition.

We recognize the critical role managers play in fostering a supportive environment. Your sensitivity and proactive approach are invaluable. Please consult with HR for any specific cases or further clarification on these policies.

Sincerely,

The HR Department

Tasks

Match the term related to employee leave with its correct definition.

Match the following terms:

  • Compassionate leave — Time off granted to an employee to deal with family illness, death, or other emergencies.
  • Bereavement policy — A company's official rules and guidelines regarding time off for mourning a deceased family member.
  • Family medical leave — A type of leave that allows employees to take unpaid, job-protected time off for specific family and medical reasons.
  • Work-life balance — The state of equilibrium where a person's professional and personal lives are equally prioritized.
  • Employee support — Resources and assistance provided by an employer to help staff with personal and professional challenges.

Writing Your Support Guidelines

Your task is to draft an internal email from the HR department to all department managers. The email should:

* Audience: Department Managers
* Purpose: Inform and guide managers on how to effectively support employees who require extended leave to provide end-of-life care for a close family member, reinforcing the company's commitment to employee support and outlining the bereavement policy and compassionate leave options.
* Approximate Length: 150-200 words.

Structure your email with a clear subject line, a professional opening, key points for managers to consider, and a supportive closing. Remember to use appropriate vocabulary and maintain an empathetic yet professional tone. You can refer to the model memo and the useful phrases from previous stages.

Improving Our Communication

Now, exchange your drafted email with a partner. Read your partner's email carefully and provide constructive feedback using the questions below. Think about both the message and the language used.

Part 1: Content and Purpose
1. Does the email clearly communicate the company's approach to extended leave for end-of-life care?
2. Is the tone appropriate (professional yet empathetic) for the sensitive nature of the topic?
3. Does the email provide clear, actionable guidance for managers on how to support their employees?

Part 2: Language and Style
4. Are the key terms like compassionate leave, bereavement policy, and employee support used correctly and effectively?
5. Are there any sentences or phrases that could be clearer, more concise, or more impactful?

Discuss your feedback with your partner, then consider how you might revise your own email based on the discussion.
