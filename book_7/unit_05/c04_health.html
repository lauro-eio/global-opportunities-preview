L-IN

Lead-In: Ethical Dilemmas in the Workplace

Teacher notes

1. Begin by displaying the lead-in question on the board.
2. Ask students to discuss in pairs or small groups for 3-5 minutes, focusing on their personal experiences or observations.
3. Bring the class together for a brief whole-group discussion, eliciting examples of ethical conflicts regarding workplace rules and individual autonomy during the pandemic.
4. Encourage students to use professional language when expressing their opinions and ensure respect for diverse viewpoints.

Student handout

Workplace Ethics: A Discussion

Recalling the most recent global health crisis, what were some of the key ethical conflicts that emerged regarding workplace rules and individual autonomy?

Consider:
* What specific rules were introduced in workplaces (e.g., related to vaccine mandates or health surveillance)?
* How did these rules affect employees' sense of individual freedom or privacy?
* What were the main arguments for and against such measures from both employee and employer perspectives?

Tasks

Match each term with its definition.

  • vaccine mandates — Official orders requiring vaccination for certain activities or employment.
  • health surveillance — Systematic monitoring of health data in a population, often for public health purposes.
  • remote work policies — Company rules and guidelines governing working from home or outside the traditional office.
  • worker protections — Laws and regulations designed to ensure the safety, health, and fair treatment of employees.
  • public health — The science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life, and promoting health through organized community efforts.
  • individual autonomy — The capacity of individuals to make their own choices and decisions, free from external control.
  • ethical implementation — Putting policies or plans into practice in a way that aligns with moral principles and values.
  • containment measures — Actions taken to prevent the spread of something harmful, like a disease.
GIST

Gist Listening: Understanding Main Arguments

Teacher notes

1. Explain to students they will listen to a segment from a workplace ethics panel discussion.
2. Tell them the first listen is for gist: they need to identify the main topic and the general stance of each speaker.
3. Play the audio once.
4. Students complete the MCQ task individually.
5. Check answers as a class, briefly discussing why each option is correct or incorrect. Do not delve into details yet.

First Listen: The Core Debate

You are going to listen to a segment from a professional panel discussion about workplace ethics during a global health crisis. For your first listen, focus on the overall message.

What is the main point the speakers are debating?

Materials

Ethical Workplace Health Policies

(Panel Moderator): Good morning, everyone, and welcome to our session on navigating health and ethics in the contemporary workplace. We've all experienced the complexities of a global health crisis, particularly its impact on our professional environments. Today, we're discussing the delicate balance between safeguarding public health and respecting individual autonomy in the context of workplace policies. Let's start with Dr. Anya Sharma, a specialist in occupational health.

(Dr. Sharma): Thank you. From a health and safety perspective, the priority during any widespread contagion must always be worker protections and the collective well-being of the workforce. When facing a novel pathogen, measures like clear remote work policies, enhanced hygiene protocols, and even regulated health surveillance become not just advisable, but often essential. The ethical dilemma arises when these measures infringe upon personal freedoms. However, I’d argue that the broader ethical imperative is to prevent widespread illness, ensuring business continuity and protecting vulnerable employees. This often necessitates tough decisions, such as the ethical implementation of targeted vaccine mandates for roles with high public contact or in critical infrastructure.

(Panel Moderator): A very clear stance, Dr. Sharma. Mr. David Chen, as a representative of employee advocacy groups, what's your perspective on this, especially concerning individual autonomy?

(Mr. Chen): Thank you. While I fully agree with the need for worker protections and a safe working environment, we must critically examine the extent to which these measures compromise fundamental individual rights. During the pandemic, many employees felt their personal health choices were being dictated, leading to significant emotional and professional distress. The imposition of vaccine mandates, for example, even when well-intentioned, can create a deeply divisive workplace culture. We saw firsthand how it impacted morale and trust. Employers have a duty of care, certainly, but that duty should not come at the expense of an employee's right to choose, particularly when less intrusive containment measures could be explored. The long-term implications for employee trust and engagement need to be carefully weighed against the immediate public health benefits.

Tasks

Listen to the discussion. What is the main point the speakers are debating?

The primary focus of the discussion is:

  • The logistical challenges of implementing remote work policies during a pandemic.
  • The balance between individual rights and collective health in workplace policy decisions.
  • The financial costs associated with enhanced worker protections.
  • The history of health surveillance in various industries.

FBDC

Feedback & Discussion: Reflecting on Ethics

Teacher notes

1. Review answers to the detailed listening tasks, ensuring full comprehension.
2. Facilitate a general discussion based on the questions in 'student_content'. Encourage students to refer back to the panel discussion and use some of the newly learned vocabulary.
3. Focus on how students express their agreement or disagreement with the speakers' points, using appropriate C2-level discourse markers.
4. Provide feedback on language use, particularly on the target vocabulary and any emergent language related to ethical debates.
5. Conclude by summarizing the key arguments heard and the complexity of ethical decision-making in the workplace.

Student handout

Discussing the Implications

Let's reflect on the discussion you just heard.

1. Whose argument did you find more convincing, Dr. Sharma's or Mr. Chen's, and why? Refer to specific points they made.
2. In your professional experience, what are some of the long-term implications of imposing strict health surveillance or vaccine mandates on worker protections and morale?
3. Beyond what was discussed, what other containment measures or remote work policies do you think employers could ethically implement to balance public health with individual autonomy?
