TCH

Teach: The Analytical Subjunctive for Institutional Welfare Regulations

Teacher notes

1. Based on errors from T1, focus clarification on 'The Analytical Subjunctive'.
2. Write the correct 'form' on the board:
- Affirmative: Subject + verb of demand/necessity (insist/mandate/require) + that + subject + bare infinitive
- Negative: Subject + verb of demand/necessity + that + subject + not + bare infinitive
3. Elicit or explain the 'meaning': This structure formulates binding biotechnology mandates, pandemic preparedness protocols, public welfare legislation, and healthcare infrastructure policies – emphasizing urgency and necessity.
4. Use the provided 'concept check questions' to ensure understanding:
- 'Does the verb inside the 'that' clause utilize an 's' for third-person singular subjects?' (Expected answer: No, it uses the bare infinitive).
- 'Does this construction express an urgent institutional mandate or legal necessity for societal health?' (Expected answer: Yes).
5. Work through the practice tasks, guiding students to apply the correct form and meaning.
6. Highlight that this structure is very formal and typically found in written policies, legal documents, or highly formal speech.

Student handout

Crafting Clear Mandates

Let's review 'The Analytical Subjunctive', a formal structure used for expressing institutional demands, necessities, or recommendations. This is common in policy documents, legal texts, and formal regulations, especially when discussing public health, ethical guidelines, or workplace protocols.

Form:
Subject + verb of demand/necessity (e.g., insist, mandate, require, recommend, propose) + that + subject + bare infinitive (base form of the verb, without 'to' or '-s').

Examples:
* The committee insists that every research team follow strict data ownership guidelines.
* It is crucial that the new AI system not infringe on employee privacy.
* The legislation mandates that all companies adhere to ethical surveillance ethics protocols.

Key Point: The verb in the 'that' clause never takes an '-s' for third-person singular subjects (e.g., 'he/she/it'). It always remains in its bare infinitive form.

T2

Test 2: Drafting Ethical AI Monitoring Regulations

Teacher notes

1. Revisit the initial scenario about company smart devices for health monitoring.
2. Instruct students to work in pairs or small groups to draft a short policy statement.
3. Emphasize the use of the Analytical Subjunctive to express binding rules and ethical considerations.
4. Encourage students to incorporate relevant vocabulary (e.g., informed consent, data ownership, surveillance ethics, productivity tracking).
5. Monitor groups for correct application of the target structure and appropriate thematic vocabulary.
6. After drafting, have groups present their statements. Provide feedback on grammar and content.
7. Sample output (for teacher reference): 'It is imperative that all employee data be anonymized before analysis. Furthermore, the company mandates that any monitoring not infringe upon individual privacy rights, and it is proposed that regular audits be conducted to ensure compliance with informed consent protocols.'
