L-IN

Lead-In: Contrasting Workplace Register

Teacher notes

Introduce the scenario: a significant error has occurred at work (e.g., a logistics mistake, a product recall).
Ask students to imagine two different reactions: one in the break room among colleagues, another in an official company memo.
Elicit general differences in language use (e.g., informal vs. formal, direct vs. diplomatic).
Focus discussion purely on word choice and register, not the specifics of the mistake itself.
Emphasize the importance of professional communication, especially in challenging situations.

Student handout

Different Voices, Different Situations

Imagine a situation at your workplace where a significant mistake has occurred – maybe a delivery mix-up, a safety protocol wasn't followed, or a customer complaint went unaddressed.

Think about two different conversations that might happen:

1. In the break room, among colleagues: How would people talk about this mistake? What kind of words would they use? What would be the general tone?
2. In an official memo or statement from management: How would leadership communicate about the same mistake? What kind of language would be expected? How would it sound different from the break room conversation?

Discuss the differences in word choice and overall style. Why do we change the way we speak or write depending on who we're talking to and the situation?

TCH

Teach: High-Register Language of Accountability

Teacher notes

Go through the answers from T1, highlighting the correct register for each statement.
For each target exponent:
* Meaning: Explain the nuanced meaning, contrasting it with the informal equivalent (e.g., "we acknowledge" vs. "we messed up").
* Register: Emphasize why it's high-register and appropriate for formal, diplomatic communication in the workplace. Discuss "functional utility" and "workplace dignity."
* Pronunciation: Model sentence stress for "institutional integrity" and "professional legacy". Model weak forms in "with due respect" (e.g., /ə/ in 'due' and 'respect' often reduced, stress on 'due' and 'respect').
Use CCQs to check understanding of meaning and appropriacy.
Provide clear examples for each exponent.

T2

Test 2: Applying High-Register Language in a Professional Response

Teacher notes

Set up a role-play scenario where students must respond to a critical situation using the target high-register language.
The scenario should be workplace-relevant and require the use of diplomacy, integrity, and accountability.
Pair students or put them in small groups.
Circulate and monitor, noting how well students incorporate the target exponents, their appropriacy, and pronunciation.
Do not interrupt fluency; note errors for later delayed error correction.
Provide a model response after the activity to compare.

Model Response:
"Thank you for raising these concerns. We acknowledge the seriousness of the recent report regarding the protocol deviation. With due respect, it is critical that we address these matters transparently to uphold our institutional integrity. How we navigate this will significantly shape our professional legacy. Therefore, our immediate focus is on a comprehensive review and a communication strategy that employs soft power to ensure all stakeholders are aligned and informed."

Student handout

Crafting a Diplomatic Message

You are a manager at a manufacturing company. A serious quality control issue has been discovered in a recently shipped batch of products, leading to potential safety concerns and customer complaints. Your team has identified the cause, but the news needs to be communicated carefully to both internal stakeholders (e.g., other departments, senior leadership) and potentially external partners.

Task: Work with a partner. One of you is the manager, the other is a concerned stakeholder (e.g., a department head, a key client representative).

As the manager, draft a short, formal verbal response (2-3 sentences) to address the stakeholder's concerns about the quality issue. Your response must:
* Acknowledge the situation.
* Show respect for the stakeholder's concerns.
* Emphasize the company's commitment to quality and responsibility.
* Suggest a diplomatic way forward.

You must use at least three of the following high-register phrases:
* with due respect
* we acknowledge
* institutional integrity
* professional legacy
* soft power

Prepare to share your response with the class. Focus on clear pronunciation and appropriate tone.
