Professional Conversations

Think about your own workplace experiences. We all know that open and direct communication is usually best, but sometimes in meetings, especially with bosses or clients, we need to be more careful with our words.

Discuss these questions with a partner:
1. Have you ever heard your boss or a client use a specific phrase to soften disagreement or suggest a different idea without being too direct?
2. Can you recall any examples? What phrases did they use?
3. Why do you think people choose to use more diplomatic language in these situations instead of just saying 'No' or 'That's wrong'?

Key Phrases for Diplomacy

Before we listen to a meeting, let's look at some important words and phrases related to diplomacy and framing in professional settings. Match each term on the left with its correct definition on the right.

Materials

Vocabulary
  • soften disagreement
  • hedge
  • framing
  • diplomatic language
  • navigate sensitivities
  • constructive feedback
Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • soften disagreement — To express an opposing view gently to avoid conflict.
  • hedge — To avoid giving a direct answer or taking a firm position, often by using cautious language.
  • framing — The way in which an issue or topic is presented to influence how it is perceived.
  • diplomatic language — Careful and polite language used to avoid offending anyone or causing conflict, especially in difficult situations.
  • navigate sensitivities — To handle a situation carefully, being aware of potential emotional or cultural issues.
  • constructive feedback — Advice or criticism that is useful and intended to help or improve something.

Materials

Team Meeting: Operational Efficiency

NARRATOR: Sarah, the Operations Manager at Global Logistics Solutions, is leading a team meeting to discuss a new routing software proposal that some team members are hesitant about.

SARAH: Good morning, everyone. Thanks for joining. Today, we're circling back to the new routing software. I know we’ve had some initial discussions, and there have been a few points where we’ve needed to navigate sensitivities regarding integration. My main goal for today is to ensure we’re all aligned on the strategic advantages, even if there are implementation hurdles.

MARK: Sarah, if I may, I just want to hedge a little on the ‘strategic advantages’ side. While I see the potential, I’ve heard from my team in dispatch that they’re really concerned about the learning curve. They feel it might actually slow down their process in the short term, which could impact our delivery times. We’re already stretched thin, as you know.

SARAH: Mark, I appreciate you framing those concerns from your team. It’s absolutely vital we consider the ground-level impact. Perhaps we could look at this from a slightly different angle: instead of seeing it as a hurdle, how can we frame it as an investment in future efficiency? We're not looking to impose, but to evolve. What if we piloted it with a smaller group first? That might soften disagreement about a full rollout.

LINDA: That’s a very diplomatic approach, Sarah. I also think we need to consider the client perspective. We've built strong relationships, and any perceived dip in service, even temporary, could be problematic. Could we get some constructive feedback from a few trusted clients during a beta phase?

SARAH: Excellent point, Linda. That’s precisely the kind of proactive framing we need. It allows us to manage expectations externally while we streamline internally. So, to summarise, we’re not pushing for an immediate, full-scale shift. We’re exploring a phased approach to soften disagreement and ensure a smooth transition. The ultimate aim is to enhance our overall operational efficiency and client satisfaction, even if it means adjusting our initial timeline.
