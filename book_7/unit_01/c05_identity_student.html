Different Voices, Different Situations

Imagine a situation at your workplace where a significant mistake has occurred – maybe a delivery mix-up, a safety protocol wasn't followed, or a customer complaint went unaddressed.

Think about two different conversations that might happen:

1. In the break room, among colleagues: How would people talk about this mistake? What kind of words would they use? What would be the general tone?
2. In an official memo or statement from management: How would leadership communicate about the same mistake? What kind of language would be expected? How would it sound different from the break room conversation?

Discuss the differences in word choice and overall style. Why do we change the way we speak or write depending on who we're talking to and the situation?

Crafting a Diplomatic Message

You are a manager at a manufacturing company. A serious quality control issue has been discovered in a recently shipped batch of products, leading to potential safety concerns and customer complaints. Your team has identified the cause, but the news needs to be communicated carefully to both internal stakeholders (e.g., other departments, senior leadership) and potentially external partners.

Task: Work with a partner. One of you is the manager, the other is a concerned stakeholder (e.g., a department head, a key client representative).

As the manager, draft a short, formal verbal response (2-3 sentences) to address the stakeholder's concerns about the quality issue. Your response must:
* Acknowledge the situation.
* Show respect for the stakeholder's concerns.
* Emphasize the company's commitment to quality and responsibility.
* Suggest a diplomatic way forward.

You must use at least three of the following high-register phrases:
* with due respect
* we acknowledge
* institutional integrity
* professional legacy
* soft power

Prepare to share your response with the class. Focus on clear pronunciation and appropriate tone.
