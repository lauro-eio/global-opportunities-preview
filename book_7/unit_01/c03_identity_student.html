When Teams Clash

Think about a time at work when two different teams or departments had very different priorities, leading to a clash or misunderstanding. For example, Logistics vs. Sales, or Warehouse vs. Office staff.

Discuss the following questions with a partner:

1. What was the core conflict between the teams?
2. How did people from each team try to communicate their needs or concerns?
3. Were there moments when people seemed to be 'talking past' each other, not truly listening or understanding the other side's perspective?
4. What were the immediate consequences of this communication breakdown?

The Account Team and Logistics

Read the conversation between Sarah, an Account Manager, and Mark, the Logistics Coordinator. They are reflecting on a past issue where client expectations and delivery realities clashed.

Materials

Sarah & Mark: Revisiting a Priority Conflict

Sarah: Mark, good to catch up. I was just thinking about that priority conflict we had last quarter with the Davison order. That was a tough one.

Mark: It certainly was, Sarah. From my side, the team was under immense pressure. We had multiple urgent dispatches, and the Davison order, while important, wasn't flagged for expedited handling until it was almost too late. My team felt quite blindsided.

Sarah: I understand. From our perspective in Account Management, the client had specific expectations, and we hadn't effectively communicated the potential logistical constraints to them upfront. We then tried to push it through, perhaps without fully appreciating the knock-on effect on your operations.

Mark: Exactly. It felt like a direct contradiction of our existing delivery schedule. I remember trying to explain the capacity issues, but it seemed like the sales target was the overriding priority at the time, and our concerns weren't quite landing.

Sarah: And I recall thinking you were being inflexible, which wasn't fair. We were both operating under different pressures. It taught me a lot about the need for early alignment and ensuring we both understand the full scope of a request.

Mark: Absolutely. Now, when the account team submits a new client request, there's much better clarity on timelines and potential challenges. We can negotiate realistic deadlines and manage expectations together. It’s about mutual respect for each other's processes.

Sarah: Right. And knowing when to bring in a third party if we can't resolve a disagreement internally. We've certainly improved our diplomacy since then. No more talking past each other.

Planning Your Diplomatic Approach

You will roleplay a situation where your team has conflicting priorities with another department. Before you start, prepare your arguments and consider how you will communicate effectively.

Scenario: Your company is launching a new product. Your team (choose either Marketing or Production) has a key priority, but it clashes with the other team's needs.

* If you choose Marketing: Your priority is to launch the product with a major campaign next month to capture market share. This requires production to expedite a large initial batch, which might disrupt their current schedule.
* If you choose Production: Your priority is to ensure product quality and meet existing orders. Rushing a new product launch next month could compromise quality and strain resources, affecting other deadlines.

Your Goal: Approach the other team to discuss these conflicting priorities and find a mutually acceptable solution using diplomatic language.

Use the phrases below to help you prepare your points.

The Diplomatic Challenge

Now it's time to put your diplomatic skills to the test!

Instructions:

1. Work with your partner. One of you will represent the Marketing team, and the other will represent the Production team.
2. You both know your team's core priority, which conflicts with the other team's.
3. Your task is to have a conversation, using the phrases and strategies you prepared, to discuss these conflicting priorities.
4. Aim to negotiate a solution that acknowledges both teams' needs and leads to a mutually acceptable outcome.

Remember to:
* Express your own team's needs clearly and respectfully.
* Listen actively to understand the other team's perspective and constraints.
* Suggest potential compromises or solutions.
* Seek clarity and respectful disagreement when needed.

Good luck!

Reflecting on Your Diplomacy

Now it's time to reflect on your conversation and give each other feedback.

Part 1: Content Feedback (with your partner)

1. Did you feel heard and understood by your partner?
2. Were you able to identify and discuss the core conflicting priorities?
3. Did you manage to negotiate a mutually acceptable outcome or a clear path forward?
4. What was one thing your partner said or did that helped move the conversation forward constructively?

Part 2: Language Focus (whole class)
