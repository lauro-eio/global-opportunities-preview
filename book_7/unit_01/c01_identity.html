L-IN

Lead-In: Reading Between the Lines

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking learners about their professional communication experiences.
2. Guide them to recall situations where an email or briefing from a manager or client required careful interpretation.
3. Focus the discussion on what made the wording careful or indirect.
4. Encourage sharing examples of phrases or situations where they had to 'read between the lines'.
5. Emphasize that this is not about abstract theory, but practical workplace communication.

Student handout

Understanding Unspoken Messages

Think about your professional life. Have you ever received an email or a briefing from a manager, client, or colleague that seemed carefully worded?

1. What was the situation? Who was the sender?
2. What made the wording 'careful' or indirect?
3. What do you think the sender really wanted to convey, beyond the words on the page?
4. How did you figure it out? What clues did you use?

GIST

Gist Reading: The Purpose of a Sensitive Briefing

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the reading passage, explaining it's a sensitive interdepartmental briefing.
2. Tell students they will read for gist – to understand the main purpose of the document.
3. Set a strict time limit for skimming (e.g., 2-3 minutes).
4. Students should complete the multiple-choice task individually.
5. Discuss answers as a class, eliciting reasons for their choices and why other options are incorrect.

Materials

Interpreting the Interdepartmental Review Briefing

Subject: Operational Review and Synergy Initiatives - Update

Dear Team Leads,

Following our recent interdepartmental operational review, I am pleased to share an initial briefing on the proposed synergy initiatives. These developments are designed to enhance cross-functional collaboration and streamline our workflows, ultimately fostering a more cohesive and productive environment. We are excited about the potential for improved efficiency and shared best practices across all our divisions.

The attached document outlines the preliminary recommendations. While the language maintains a positive and forward-looking tone, it is crucial that we, as team leaders, approach this with a keen eye for nuance. Several sections subtly highlight areas where current practices might be perceived as less than optimal. For instance, 'optimising resource allocation' in Section 3 might imply a re-evaluation of current staffing levels in certain areas, rather than simply moving equipment around. Similarly, 'enhancing accountability frameworks' could signify a stricter monitoring of individual contributions and team outputs.

Our approach to discussing these changes with our teams will require considerable diplomacy. It’s vital to convey the positive aspirations of these initiatives without inadvertently causing apprehension or undermining team morale. We must uphold the integrity of our teams while integrating these new directives. Your role as a key stakeholder in this process is not just to disseminate information, but to interpret and guide your teams through these adjustments with wisdom and foresight.

Particular attention should be paid to the section on 'Stakeholder Engagement and Communication Protocols.' This emphasis suggests that there may have been instances of miscommunication or lack of coordination in the past. It’s a subtle reminder to ensure all future external communications are carefully vetted and aligned with corporate messaging. The goal is to present a united front and avoid any perceptions of internal disagreement.

We will convene a follow-up meeting next Tuesday to discuss your initial interpretations and strategies for implementation. Please come prepared to share how you plan to read between the lines and address the unstated implications of this briefing within your respective departments. Your considered feedback will be invaluable as we move forward.

Tasks

What is the main purpose of this briefing?

The main purpose of this briefing is to...

  • Announce immediate staff reductions and department mergers.
  • Inform team leads about new initiatives and prepare them for careful communication about these changes.
  • Praise the teams for their excellent cross-functional collaboration.
  • Request a detailed report on past communication failures from team leads.
DET

Detailed Reading: Unpacking the Hidden Messages

Teacher notes

1. Remind students they will reread the same briefing, but this time for specific details and implied meanings.
2. Instruct them to read the briefing again carefully.
3. Distribute the short-text comprehension tasks and allow time for students to complete them.
4. Check answers for the factual detail tasks. Encourage students to quote from the text.
5. Transition to the open discussion prompts. Divide students into pairs or small groups for discussion.
6. Circulate, listening to their interpretations and guiding them to identify the 'unstated implications'.
7. Bring the class back together to share their interpretations for each prompt.
Model Answers for Discussion Prompts:
1. It implies current practices might be inefficient or have issues, and they are looking to change how people work.
2. It suggests there have been communication issues or a lack of alignment in previous external communications, hence the need for 'careful vetting'.
3. It means team leads need to understand the deeper, unstated implications of the document and manage their teams' reactions to potential changes, acting as a buffer or interpreter.

FBDC

Feedback and Consolidation: Applying Diplomatic Reading

Teacher notes

1. Review the key takeaways from the detailed reading and discussion, focusing on the skill of identifying nuance and implied messages.
2. Initiate a broader discussion, asking students to reflect on how this skill is important in their own workplaces.
3. Prompt them to think about how they would apply what they learned from interpreting this briefing to similar situations.
4. Encourage students to share advice for reading sensitive documents effectively.
5. Offer brief, constructive language notes related to expressions used for interpreting or discussing sensitive information, without doing explicit error correction on structure.

Student handout

Putting Diplomatic Reading into Practice

We've looked closely at how to 'read between the lines' in a sensitive briefing. Now, let's think about how this skill applies to your own work.

1. Why is it important for you, in your role, to be able to understand the unstated messages in communications from stakeholders or managers?
2. What specific strategies can you use to identify nuance and implied meanings in future communications?
3. Imagine you are in a similar situation at work. How would you approach discussing the 'unstated implications' of a briefing with your team or colleagues, while maintaining diplomacy and integrity?
