Lead-In: Professional Reputation and Legacy

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking learners to reflect on long-serving colleagues they have known or currently work with.
2. Elicit discussion on how such individuals are remembered or spoken about after they leave, or while still working.
3. Guide the discussion to cover different aspects of their reputation and legacy: praise, cautionary tales, or even myths.
4. Ensure the focus is on professional contexts and workplace dignity, avoiding the 'institutional bad-news' angle from earlier classes.
5. Prompt learners to consider specific instances where a colleague's actions left a lasting impression, good or bad, and how that impact is discussed.

Student handout

Discussing a Colleague's Impact

Think about a colleague who has been with your company or organization for a very long time, or someone who recently retired after a long career.

1. How is this person's reputation talked about by others?
2. What kind of legacy have they left, or are they leaving?
3. Do people speak of their actions with praise, caution, or even create 'myths' around their impact?
4. Can you recall a specific situation where their professional conduct or diplomatic skill significantly influenced an outcome or how they are remembered?

TCH

Teach: Clarifying Inversion Post Negative Adverbials

Teacher notes

1. Review the answers from T1, highlighting the sentences where inversion was required.
2. Concept Check 1: 'Is the structural order of the subject and auxiliary standard or flipped?' (Expected Answer: Flipped)
3. Concept Check 2: 'Does this change of structure emphasize the rarity or absolute gravity of the diplomatic constraint?' (Expected Answer: Yes)
4. Explain the 'Form' clearly:
* Affirmative: Negative adverbial + auxiliary + Subject + main verb (e.g., Seldom has the board observed...)
* Negative: Not until/Under no circumstances + clause/noun + auxiliary + Subject + main verb (e.g., Under no circumstances can a representative compromise...)
5. Emphasize the 'Meaning': Applying high-register, nuanced rhetorical weight to emphasize instances of institutional integrity, professional legacy, or policy limitations in executive reports.
6. Provide further examples and guide learners through sentence transformations. Ensure pronunciation practice if needed, focusing on intonation that reflects the emphasis.

Student handout

Mastering High-Register Emphasis

When we want to add strong emphasis, especially in formal or diplomatic contexts, we can use inversion after certain negative adverbials. This flips the usual subject-verb order, making the statement more impactful and formal.

Form:
* Negative adverbial + auxiliary verb + Subject + main verb
* _Seldom_ has _the board_ observed such an exceptional display.
* _Never before_ have _we_ encountered such a nuanced challenge.
* With phrases like _Under no circumstances_ or _Not until_:
* _Under no circumstances_ can _a representative_ compromise the legacy.
* _Not until the final report_ did _we_ understand the full impact.

This structure adds rhetorical weight, emphasizing the rarity or gravity of the statement, often used when discussing matters of professional integrity, policy, or legacy.

Practice: Rewrite these sentences, starting with the given negative adverbial to apply inversion. Remember to maintain the formal tone.

T2

Test 2: Applying Diplomatic Language in a Professional Assessment

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the task: Learners will draft a short professional assessment or a section of an internal report, focusing on a colleague's legacy.
2. Emphasize the need to incorporate the high-register language and, specifically, at least one instance of inversion post-negative adverbial.
3. Learners should aim for diplomatic and nuanced language, reflecting the unit's focus on 'soft power'.
4. Monitor during writing for correct application of inversion and appropriate vocabulary.
5. After drafting, learners will review a specific sentence to ensure it uses inversion correctly.
6. Sample model answer for an open productive task (for teacher reference only, not for direct student consumption):
'Seldom have we witnessed such consistent dedication to inter-departmental cooperation as displayed by Ms. Davies. Her unique ability to mediate complex disputes effectively solidified her legacy within our team. Under no circumstances should her contributions to fostering a collaborative environment be underestimated, for they have profoundly shaped our current working culture.'
