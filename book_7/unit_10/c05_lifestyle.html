L-IN

Lead-In: Navigating Global Corporate Relocation

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and briefly review the previous lesson's topic if relevant.
2. Introduce the concept of global employee relocation for professional assignments, linking it to the 'Home & Lifestyle' theme.
3. Present the lead-in brief: 'Your company is tasked with relocating a senior manager to lead a new international office for a multi-year assignment. What crucial logistical and support services must HR provide to ensure a smooth transition for the employee and their family, beyond just the job role?'
4. Divide students into small groups (2-3) and ask them to brainstorm and list potential challenges and necessary support services.
5. Facilitate a brief whole-class feedback session, noting down key ideas on the board. Encourage discussion on both practical needs (visa, housing) and less tangible ones (well-being, family adjustment). This prepares them for the focus on work-life integration.

T1

Diagnostic: Identifying Appropriate Relocation Support Language

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will read an internal HR memo regarding a manager's relocation support package.
2. Instruct students to pay close attention to the language used, specifically focusing on which phrases sound most professional and appropriate for a corporate HR context.
3. Distribute the memo and tasks. Give students ample time to complete the multiple-choice questions independently.
4. Do NOT provide answers or explanations at this stage. The goal is to diagnose existing knowledge of register and appropriacy.
5. Monitor students, but avoid explicit correction. Note common difficulties or areas where students choose less formal options for later teaching.

TCH

Teach: Nuances of Corporate Well-being Lexis and Register

Teacher notes

1. Begin by reviewing the answers from T1, providing the correct choices.
2. For each correct answer (the target exponent), highlight it on the board.
3. Meaning & Register: Elicit or explain the precise meaning of each phrase. Contrast the formality and nuance with the incorrect options from T1. Emphasize why the target exponent is more appropriate for a C2 professional, corporate setting (e.g., 'foster work-life synergy' implies strategic action and collaboration, 'uphold personal well-being' suggests active maintenance and respect for an individual's state).
4. Phonology: Model each target exponent phrase clearly. Explain that stress should be placed on content words (e.g., foster work-life synergy, uphold personal well-being, negotiate flexible working parameters, mitigate professional burnout) to emphasize meaning and maintain a professional, considered intonation. Demonstrate a falling tone at the end of statements to convey certainty and authority, as is common in formal workplace communication. Have students repeat chorally and individually.
5. Use Concept Checking Questions (CCQs) for each phrase: e.g., 'If a company wants to foster work-life synergy, does that mean they want you to work all the time? (No) Does it mean they want your work and personal life to support each other? (Yes)'
6. Move on to the clarification tasks to reinforce understanding and appropriacy.

T2

Test 2: Proposing Strategic Relocation Support

Teacher notes

1. Explain to students that they will now work in pairs or small groups to apply the learned professional language.
2. Present the scenario: They are members of a Global Mobility Team tasked with developing a support proposal for an executive relocation.
3. Emphasize that their proposal should be succinct, professional, and must incorporate at least two of the target phrases from today's lesson accurately and appropriately.
4. Give students 10-15 minutes to discuss and draft their proposals. Encourage them to consider the practical needs of the relocating manager and their family.
5. Circulate and monitor, prompting students to use the target language and correcting only significant errors in register or meaning when students are stuck.
6. Invite groups to share their proposals with the class. Provide constructive feedback, focusing on the correct and natural application of the target vocabulary and overall professional register.
7. Model a strong example during feedback if necessary, for instance: 'Our proposal aims to foster work-life synergy by offering comprehensive family support, and we commit to regular check-ins to mitigate professional burnout.'
