L-IN

Lead-In: Setting Professional Boundaries

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and introduce the class topic: comparing global work cultures and their impact on personal boundaries.
2. Ask students to read the lead-in questions and discuss in pairs or small groups.
3. Facilitate a brief whole-class feedback session, drawing out key ideas about dedication, personal time, and global collaboration.

Student handout

Work & Home: Finding the Balance

In some professional environments, extended working hours are a mark of dedication, while in others, strict separation of work and personal time is valued. These differing cultural norms impact expectations for effective global team collaboration and employee well-being, particularly concerning personal boundaries.

Discuss the following questions with a partner:
1. How do you define 'dedication' in your workplace? Does it involve working extended hours, or something else?
2. What are some unwritten rules or expectations about work hours and personal time in your country or industry?
3. Have you ever worked with people from different cultural backgrounds who had very different ideas about work-life boundaries? What challenges did this create?

Key Terms for Global Work

Before listening, let's look at some important terms you'll hear. Match each term to its definition.

Materials

Vocabulary
  • work-life integration
  • cultural intelligence
  • professional etiquette
  • global team collaboration
  • time zone management
  • family leave policies
Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • work-life integration — Blending professional and personal responsibilities into a single lifestyle, often with flexible boundaries.
  • cultural intelligence — The ability to understand and adapt to different cultural contexts and situations.
  • professional etiquette — The formal rules of polite behavior in professional situations.
  • global team collaboration — Working together effectively with colleagues located in different countries and time zones.
  • time zone management — Strategizing and coordinating schedules across multiple time zones to facilitate communication and productivity.
  • family leave policies — Official rules and benefits that allow employees time off for family-related reasons, such as childbirth or care for a sick relative.

Student handout

First Listen: What's the Big Picture?

Listen to the conversation between Maria, an HR specialist, and David, a team lead. Choose the best option that summarizes the main topic of their discussion.

Materials

Balancing Global Work Cultures

Maria: Good morning, David. Thanks for joining. Our global teams are doing great work, but we've had some feedback regarding work-life integration and cultural expectations.
David: Certainly, Maria. It's a common topic. For instance, in some regions, staying late is seen as a sign of dedication, while in others, leaving on time is simply good professional etiquette and a sign of efficiency.
Maria: Exactly. We've noticed this impacting global team collaboration. Our team in Tokyo often works very long hours, whereas our colleagues in Berlin strictly adhere to their contracts, valuing personal time. This creates unspoken tension.
David: I understand. One of my team members mentioned feeling pressured to respond to emails late at night from a different time zone. It really impacts their employee well-being, especially concerning time zone management when scheduling calls.
Maria: That's a key point. We need to foster greater cultural intelligence across our departments. It’s not about one culture being right or wrong; it's about understanding and respecting different professional boundaries.
David: Absolutely. We recently implemented a 'no email after 7 PM local time' policy for non-urgent matters, specifically to improve work-life balance.
Maria: That's a positive step. What about family leave policies? Are they consistent, or do local regulations still dictate too much variation?
David: We're trying to centralize them, Maria, but local laws are complex. Our aim is to ensure fair support for all, whether it's paternity leave in Sweden or extended family care in India. The challenge is communicating these nuances effectively without causing confusion or resentment.
Maria: Right. So, better training on cross-cultural communication and clear guidelines on expected response times are crucial. We also need managers to actively role-model healthy work-life boundaries.
David: Agreed. It's about setting clear expectations, empowering employees, and building a culture where everyone feels valued and understood, regardless of where they are in the world. It’s a continuous effort to achieve true workplace dignity.

Tasks

Choose the main topic of the discussion.

The conversation primarily focuses on:

  • The benefits of working long hours in global teams.
  • The challenges of managing time zones for international projects.
  • How cultural differences in work-life expectations affect global team dynamics and employee well-being.
  • New company policies for holiday and sick leave.

Detailed Listening: Specific Information

Teacher notes

1. Tell students they will listen again to identify specific details and complete the tasks.
2. Play the audio a second time.
3. Have students complete the short_text tasks and the mcq_single task.
4. For the open inference question in student_content (about specific cultural examples), allow students to discuss in pairs, then elicit answers and provide feedback. Emphasize that there are no single 'right' answers, but rather interpretations supported by the text.

Follow-up Discussion: Reflecting on Boundaries

Teacher notes

1. Initiate a class discussion based on the questions in student_content.
2. Encourage students to connect the discussion to their own experiences and the themes from the listening.
3. Provide feedback on language use, particularly encouraging the accurate use of the target vocabulary from the PTV stage.

Student handout

Your Take: Building a Better Workplace

Reflect on the conversation and your own experiences. Discuss the following questions in small groups or as a class:
1. David mentioned a 'no email after 7 PM local time' policy. Do you think this is an effective strategy? Why or why not?
2. What other specific actions or policies could companies implement to improve work-life integration and respect cultural intelligence in global teams?
3. How important do you think 'role-modeling healthy work-life boundaries' by managers is? Provide an example if you can.
4. What is one practical takeaway you can apply to your own professional interactions with colleagues from different cultural backgrounds?
