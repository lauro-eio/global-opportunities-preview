TCH

Teach: Clarifying Fronting and Inversion for Impact

Teacher notes

1. Based on errors from T1, specifically address:
a. Subject-Verb Agreement in Inverted Structures: Explain that when a prepositional/directional phrase is fronted, the verb must agree with the actual subject that comes after the verb. Use examples like 'Deep within the data reside the costs.' (Subject: costs, plural; Verb: reside, plural).
CCQ: Is the logical subject placed before or after the main verb of the inverted clause? (After) How do we make the verb agree? (With the subject, not the fronted phrase).
b. Inversion with Negative Fronting: Explain that when negative or restrictive adverbs/phrases (e.g., 'not since', 'never', 'rarely', 'little') are fronted, auxiliary-subject inversion is mandatory, similar to a question structure. Use examples: 'Not since X have Y faced...'
CCQ: Does a fronted negative time phrase like 'not since' require subject-auxiliary inversion? (Yes). What happens if we don't invert? (Sounds awkward, grammatically incorrect).
2. Provide clear examples for each rule.
3. Students work through the practice tasks, applying the clarified rules.
4. Check answers and provide further examples/drilling as needed.

Student handout

Mastering Emphatic Structures

We often vary standard sentence hierarchy to inject structural focus and narrative weight, especially in reports or evaluations. This is done through fronting and inversion.

Rule 1: Fronted Prepositional/Directional Phrases
When a phrase like 'Deep within...' or 'Among...' starts a sentence, the verb often comes before the subject. The verb must agree with the true subject, which follows the verb.
Incorrect:* Deep within the data resides the challenges.
Correct:* Deep within the data reside the challenges.

Rule 2: Fronted Negative or Restrictive Adverbs
When a sentence starts with a negative or restrictive adverbial phrase (e.g., Not since, Never before, Rarely, Little did I know), we must use subject-auxiliary inversion, just like in a question.
Incorrect:* Not since the crisis local businesses have adapted so quickly.
Correct:* Not since the crisis have local businesses adapted so quickly.

Complete the following practice exercises, applying these rules.

Test 2: Executive Summary - The Future of Local Services

Teacher notes

1. Explain the task: Students will write a short executive summary for a board meeting, analyzing the current state and future of local services in the digital economy.
2. Emphasize the need to incorporate the advanced structures of fronting and inversion, specifically to add emphasis and weight to their conclusions.
3. Instruct students to draft at least two sentences using fronting/inversion for impact. Encourage them to be precise and professional.
4. Circulate and monitor, providing support and noting successful application of the target structures.
5. Optionally, have students share their summaries or key sentences for peer feedback.

Sample sentence using target structure:
* 'Beyond the rhetoric of convenience lies a complex web of challenges for local businesses.'
* 'Not since the rise of the industrial age has the service sector faced such a fundamental restructuring.'
