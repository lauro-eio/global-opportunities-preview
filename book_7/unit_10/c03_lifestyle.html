L-IN

Lead-In: Initial Considerations for Workforce Housing

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs or small groups (3-4).
2. Introduce the scenario from the student content, ensuring they understand the context of the construction project and the need for international labor.
3. Ask them to discuss the three questions provided, encouraging them to share initial ideas and experiences related to housing, integration, and ethical considerations.
4. Monitor discussions, listening for emerging themes and vocabulary.
5. Bring the class back together for a brief whole-class share-out of key points from their discussions.

Student handout

Setting the Scene: Housing Our New Workforce

Imagine our company has just won a major contract, and we need to bring in 200 skilled workers from overseas for a new construction project. This is a big opportunity, but it also comes with significant responsibilities. We need to make sure these workers have not just a place to sleep, but dignified housing and a positive experience integrating into our community.

Discuss with a partner:
1. What are the most important things to consider when finding accommodation for a large group of international workers?
2. What challenges might these workers face when arriving in a new country and community, beyond their immediate job role?
3. How can we ensure they feel respected and are well-integrated, not just housed?

MOD

Model Dialogue: Strategic Planning for Accommodation

Teacher notes

1. Instruct students to read the dialogue individually or in pairs.
2. Ask them to identify the key strategies and considerations mentioned by Aisha and Ben.
3. Focus on the emphasized lexical items: workforce housing standards, temporary residency, regulatory compliance, social cohesion, community outreach, labor mobility.
4. Briefly discuss their answers to the discussion question, ensuring they understand the various facets of the problem.

Student handout

Professional Perspectives: Planning for Dignified Housing

Read the following dialogue between two project managers, Aisha and Ben, who are discussing strategies for the new international workforce housing project.

Aisha: 'Ben, regarding the new influx of international workers for the South Ridge project, we need a robust plan for their living arrangements. It's more than just finding buildings; we're talking about workforce housing standards that reflect our company's values.'

Ben: 'Absolutely, Aisha. Dignity and practicality are key. I've been looking into options that go beyond basic temporary residency. We need to ensure their living conditions are comfortable and safe. And thinking about regulatory compliance is crucial from the outset to avoid any future issues.'

Aisha: 'Precisely. And beyond the physical structures, there's the human element – social cohesion. How do we help these workers integrate smoothly? We can't have them feeling isolated. Perhaps a dedicated community outreach program?'

Ben: 'That's a great point. Maybe partnerships with local community centers or language support programs? We also need to consider the practicalities of labor mobility – how easily can they commute to the site? Is public transport available, or do we need to arrange dedicated shuttles?'

Aisha: 'Good thinking. And what about cultural sensitivity in their living spaces? Shared kitchens, prayer rooms, recreational areas? These details contribute to overall well-being. We should also think about support for their families, even if it's just a temporary stay.'

Ben: 'Agreed. It's a complex puzzle, but by focusing on these ethical and practical strategies, we can ensure a successful and respectful experience for everyone involved.'

Discussion: What specific strategies do Aisha and Ben suggest for the workers' housing and integration?

PREP

Preparation: Gathering Ideas and Phrases

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will prepare for a discussion on developing a comprehensive plan.
2. Go through the column banks, encouraging students to read the phrases aloud and discuss their meaning in context.
3. Ask students to choose 3-4 phrases from each column that they find most useful or that they want to try to use in the next speaking activity.
4. Give students 5-7 minutes to make notes, perhaps jotting down their own specific ideas under each category, using the provided phrases as a starting point. Emphasize that these notes are for guiding their speaking, not for writing a script.

FP

Freer Practice: Developing a Housing & Integration Plan

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into groups of 3-4.
2. Present the roleplay scenario and instructions clearly.
3. Emphasize that they should aim for fluency and use some of the language from the previous stages and the column banks.
4. Give students 15-20 minutes for their discussion and to create a brief summary of their plan.
5. Monitor groups closely, listening for effective communication, range of vocabulary, and areas for delayed correction. Avoid interrupting fluency.
6. Note down examples of good language use and common errors for feedback.

Student handout

Group Challenge: Our Comprehensive Plan

You are part of a special committee tasked with developing a comprehensive plan for housing and integrating the 200 international workers for the new construction project. Your goal is to propose a plan that is both ethical and practical, considering all aspects discussed today.

In groups of three or four, discuss the following:

* Housing: What specific types of accommodation will you secure? How will you ensure high workforce housing standards?
* Integration: What initiatives will you put in place to ensure social cohesion and successful community outreach?
* Logistics & Support: How will you manage labor mobility, temporary residency requirements, and overall regulatory compliance?
* Budget & Resources: What resources will be needed, and how will you justify the investment in dignified living conditions?

Work together to create a summary of your proposed plan. Be ready to share your main ideas with the class.

FBDC

Feedback and Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Content Feedback (5-7 minutes):
a. Ask each group to quickly share the main points of their proposed plan (2-3 minutes per group).
b. Facilitate a brief whole-class discussion, comparing plans and highlighting strong ideas (e.g., 'Group A's idea for community partnerships was very strong' or 'Group B raised a practical point about transport that others missed').
c. Praise effective contributions and problem-solving.

2. Delayed Correction (7-10 minutes):
a. Inform students that you took notes on language during their discussions.
b. Write a selection of common errors (anonymously) on the board or screen. Mix error types (e.g., lexical, pronunciation, sentence structure) but prioritize those affecting clarity or related to target lexis.
c. Ask students to work in pairs or individually to correct the errors. Provide support as needed.
d. Go through the corrections as a class, explaining the correct forms.
e. Also, highlight examples of excellent language use observed during the task, especially uses of the target vocabulary from the column banks.

Student handout

Reflecting on Our Plans and Language

Now that you've completed your discussions, let's reflect on the ideas you generated and the way we communicated them.

1. Content Feedback (Whole Class):
* Each group briefly presents the main points of their housing and integration plan.
* As a class, discuss: Which plan seems most practical? Which plan is most ethical? What were the strongest ideas across the groups?

2. Self-Correction & Peer Feedback (Individual/Pairs):
* Think about your own speaking during the discussion. What did you do well?
* What specific phrases or vocabulary did you try to use? Did you feel confident using them?
* What aspects of your language would you like to improve for next time?
