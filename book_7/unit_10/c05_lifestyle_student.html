Starting a New Chapter Abroad

Imagine your company needs to send a senior manager, Mr. Davies, to lead a new office in another country for several years. This isn't just about a new job; it's about a whole new life for him and his family.

Working in small groups, discuss the following:

1. What are the biggest challenges Mr. Davies and his family might face when moving to a new country for work?
2. What kinds of support or services should the company's HR department provide to make this transition as smooth as possible for both Mr. Davies and his family, beyond just the job duties?
3. Which of these services do you think are most critical for long-term success in the new role and country?

HR Support: Choosing the Right Words

Read the HR memo below regarding the relocation of a senior manager. For each numbered blank, choose the most appropriate phrase to complete the sentence, considering the professional context of a corporate HR communication.

Materials

Internal Memo: Senior Manager Relocation Support (Mr. Davies)

Subject: Comprehensive Relocation Support for Mr. Julian Davies
Date: [Current Date]
To: Global Mobility Team
From: Head of HR

Dear Team,

This memo outlines the enhanced support package for Mr. Julian Davies, who will be relocating to lead our new regional office in Singapore for a multi-year assignment. Our objective is to ensure a seamless transition for Mr. Davies and his family, thereby maximizing his effectiveness in his new role.

Key areas of support include:

1. Work-Life Integration: We are committed to helping Mr. Davies (1) [balance his job and home life / foster work-life synergy / make his work and home life click] effectively. This includes offering resources to manage personal and professional demands. We believe this approach is crucial to his overall success and retention.

2. Personal Well-being: Beyond professional demands, it is vital that we help Mr. Davies (2) [look after himself / uphold personal well-being / keep healthy and happy]. Our package includes access to a well-being program and local support networks to assist with adaptation to the new environment.

3. Flexible Working Arrangements: We understand the complexities of international assignments. As such, Mr. Davies will have the opportunity to (3) [talk about his working hours / negotiate flexible working parameters / sort out his work schedule] with his new regional director, subject to operational requirements. This aims to provide adaptability during the initial transition phase.

4. Mitigating Professional Strain: We recognise the potential for high pressure in a new leadership role. A critical component of our support is to proactively (4) [stop him from burning out / prevent job stress / mitigate professional burnout]. We will provide executive coaching and regular check-ins to monitor workload and stress levels, ensuring long-term sustainability.

Your collaboration is essential to delivering this comprehensive support. Please ensure all teams are aligned with these objectives.

Best regards,

[HR Director's Name]
Head of Human Resources

Speaking Professionally About Support

Now let's look closely at the professional phrases used in the HR memo. Understanding their exact meaning, when to use them, and how to pronounce them is key to effective workplace communication.

Below, you'll find tasks to help you master these expressions. Focus on the subtle differences and the level of formality.

Key Phrases for Discussion:
* foster work-life synergy
* uphold personal well-being
* negotiate flexible working parameters
* mitigate professional burnout

Materials

Tasks

Match each professional phrase with its most accurate explanation of meaning and register.

  • foster work-life synergy — To actively encourage a mutually beneficial relationship between an employee's professional and personal life.
  • uphold personal well-being — To maintain and support an individual's mental, physical, and emotional health, especially in a demanding environment.
  • negotiate flexible working parameters — To formally discuss and agree upon adjustable conditions or boundaries related to working hours or location.
  • mitigate professional burnout — To take steps to reduce the severity or impact of extreme exhaustion and stress caused by work.
