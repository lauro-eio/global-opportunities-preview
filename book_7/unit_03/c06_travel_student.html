What Needs Improving?

Think about your workplace. What small, practical improvements could make things easier or safer? This isn't about big company policy; it's about a specific, tangible change in your daily routine or immediate work area.

For example:
1. Better lighting in a specific corridor or storage area.
2. A clearer sign for a delivery route.
3. A small adjustment to a loading bay rule.
4. A new system for tracking shared tools.

Discuss with a partner:
* What small improvement would you propose?
* Who would you send this suggestion to (your supervisor, team lead)?
* Why is this improvement important for you and your colleagues?
* What's the goal of your message?

Analyzing a Workplace Brief

Read the brief below. It's a short message to a supervisor about a workplace improvement.
As you read, think about:
* What is the main problem identified?
* What solution is suggested?
* How is the message structured for clarity?

Materials

Brief: South Loading Bay Lighting

Subject: Request for improved lighting in South Loading Bay
To: [Supervisor's Name]
From: [Your Name]
Date: [Date]

Dear [Supervisor's Name],

I am writing to respectfully request an assessment of the lighting conditions in the South Loading Bay, specifically near bays 3 and 4.

Over the past few weeks, several colleagues and I have noted that the current illumination in this particular section is often insufficient, especially during early morning shifts and on darker afternoons. This makes it challenging to accurately read small print on shipping labels and to safely operate equipment, increasing the potential for errors or minor incidents.

To address this, I would like to recommend the installation of additional overhead LED lighting fixtures in this area. LED lights would provide brighter, more consistent light, improving visibility and reducing eye strain. This improvement would directly enhance safety protocols and the efficiency of loading and unloading operations in a critical section of the warehouse.

Thank you for considering this request. I am available to discuss this further at your convenience.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Your Department]

Building Your Brief

Now it's time to plan your own brief. Remember the problem you identified in the Lead-In stage? We'll use the structure of the model brief to help you.

Look at the useful phrases below. These will help you write clearly and professionally.

1. Opening the brief: How will you introduce your request?
2. Stating the problem: How will you describe the issue?
3. Explaining the impact: Why is this problem important?
4. Proposing a solution: What is your recommendation?
5. Closing the brief: How will you end professionally?

My Workplace Brief

Now, write your own short brief (around 100-150 words) to your supervisor.

Audience: Your supervisor or team lead.
Purpose: To clearly identify a small infrastructure or routing issue, explain its impact, and propose a concrete, practical solution.
Length: Approximately 100-150 words.

Use the ideas from your planning and the useful phrases from the previous activity.

Make sure your brief is:
* Clear and easy to understand.
* Professional in tone.
* Concise – get straight to the point.

Reviewing Our Briefs

Now you will review your brief, or exchange it with a partner for feedback.

First, focus on the content:
1. Is the problem clearly stated? Can you understand exactly what the issue is?
2. Is the proposed solution practical and specific?
3. Is the impact of the problem clearly explained (e.g., delays, safety, efficiency)?
4. Is the tone professional and respectful?

Next, look at the language:
5. Are there any phrases that could be clearer or more concise?
6. Check for spelling and punctuation errors.
7. Did the writer use appropriate vocabulary (e.g., from our column banks)?

Use this feedback to make any necessary improvements to your brief. Remember, the goal is to communicate effectively and professionally.
