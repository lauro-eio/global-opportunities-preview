Your Daily Journey

Think about your daily commute or any recent travel. Discuss with a partner or small group:

1. What are the biggest challenges you face getting to and from work?
2. Have you ever had a 'last-mile' problem – like missing a connection, not having a lift, or a blocked road near your destination?
3. How do your work shifts (early, late, night) affect your ability to access reliable transport?
4. What kind of impact do these transport issues have on your day or your work?

Workplace Transport Talk

Read the conversation between Maria and Ben, two colleagues discussing their travel to work:

Maria: Ben, rough morning! My bus was delayed by almost an hour, completely messing up my scheduling. It’s always an issue with the early shift; public transport access is so limited before 6 AM.

Ben: Tell me about it, Maria. I work late sometimes, and getting home is a nightmare. The company shuttle stops running at 10 PM, so I'm often left to figure out the last mile on my own. Taxis are expensive, and walking feels unsafe.

Maria: Exactly! It feels like the local infrastructure just isn't designed for people who don't work standard 9-to-5s. It impacts productivity when you're stressed before you even start.

Ben: Absolutely. I've even considered moving closer, but housing is so expensive. We really need a better solution for staff mobility outside peak hours.

Maria: I agree. It's a genuine problem for many of us.

Brainstorming Solutions

Now, prepare to discuss a 'last-mile' or commute problem you know well. Think about its causes, impacts, and possible solutions. Use the phrases below to help you structure your ideas. Make some notes, but don't write full sentences.

The Mobility Meeting

Imagine you are in a team meeting. Your task is to discuss the common mobility access and last-mile problems that employees in your company face, especially those on varied shifts or living in areas with poor transport links.

Working in small groups:

1. Each person should briefly present a specific problem they or colleagues experience.
2. As a group, discuss the root causes and consequences of these problems.
3. Brainstorm and propose practical, realistic solutions that your company could implement to improve employee mobility and access to the workplace.
4. Aim to reach a consensus on the best one or two solutions to present to senior management. Focus on clear communication and professional language.

Reflecting on Solutions

Think about your discussion in the 'Mobility Meeting':

1. Did your group effectively identify and describe the key mobility problems?
2. Were your proposed solutions practical and realistic for a workplace?
3. How well did you communicate your ideas and listen to your colleagues?

Listen to your teacher's feedback on the content of your discussion and the language you used.
