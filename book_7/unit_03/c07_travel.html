TCH

Clarification: Concessive Clause Mastery

Teacher notes

1. Review the answers from T1, highlighting the common errors observed.
2. Introduce/re-clarify the function and form of concessive subordinate clauses for strategic arguments.
3. Write the target forms on the board:
* Albeit + clause, main clause. (e.g., Albeit the initial cost was steep, the returns justify it.)
* Notwithstanding + noun phrase, main clause. (e.g., Notwithstanding budget constraints, the security upgrade is critical.)
* Be that as it may, main clause. (often a standalone phrase, then main clause, e.g., Be that as it may, we cannot compromise on safety standards.)
4. Explain the meaning: Formulating balanced, strategic executive logic that overrides logistical counter-arguments, layout risks, or spatial planning limits during system expansions.
5. Use concept check questions:
* "Are we acknowledging a clear obstacle or counter-argument? (Yes)"
* "Does the main clause remain valid and prioritized despite this spatial or funding block? (Yes)"
6. Provide controlled practice activities for students to apply the correct structures.

Student handout

Sharpening Your Strategy Language

We often need to acknowledge a challenge or limitation while still emphasizing the importance and priority of a core decision or plan. This requires precise, advanced language. Let's look at how to use concessive clauses like 'Albeit,' 'Notwithstanding,' and 'Be that as it may' to frame these strategic statements effectively, ensuring clarity and impact.

Materials

Tasks

Complete the sentence using 'Notwithstanding' followed by a suitable noun phrase, maintaining a professional tone. You are acknowledging a temporary inconvenience but focusing on the long-term benefit.

The new routing system initially caused significant delays. ___________ the initial rollout delays, the long-term benefits for global supply chain efficiency are undeniable.

Choose the sentence that correctly uses a concessive clause to acknowledge a challenge while asserting the main point, suitable for an executive summary.

Which statement best presents a strategic override?

  • Albeit the budget was tight, but we approved the expanded zone mapping project.
  • Be that as it may, the project faced resistance, but it will proceed as planned.
  • Notwithstanding the complexities of the regulatory landscape, the drone delivery network is still a feasible and necessary investment.
  • Even though the current system was flawed, yet they insisted on maintaining it for another quarter.
T2

Production: Proposing Global System Adjustments

Teacher notes

1. Set the context: Students are managers or project leads presenting a proposal.
2. Instruct students to draft a short proposal (approx. 50-70 words) for a system upgrade or expansion in their workplace.
3. Emphasize that the proposal must acknowledge at least two significant obstacles or challenges using the concessive clauses (albeit, notwithstanding, be that as it may) taught in TCH.
4. The core of the proposal should still be the necessity and benefits of the plan, overriding the acknowledged obstacles.
5. Monitor for accurate and appropriate use of the target structures. Provide constructive feedback.
6. Sample model answer: "Notwithstanding the significant upfront investment required for the new satellite tracking system, its integration is critical for optimizing our global fleet management. Albeit the transition period may present some logistical challenges, the long-term efficiency gains and real-time coverage benefits are undeniable, making this upgrade mandatory."

Student handout

System Upgrade Proposal

Imagine your company needs to implement a new system or expand an existing one – perhaps a new delivery route across regions, a wider communication network for field teams, or an updated facility layout to improve workflow. There are clear challenges or obstacles to this plan (e.g., high cost, technical issues, potential disruption, regulatory hurdles). Your task is to draft a short proposal or argument (approximately 50-70 words) to your manager or a project board. In your proposal, you must strategically acknowledge at least two of these obstacles using concessive clauses (albeit, notwithstanding, be that as it may) while still emphasizing why the plan is essential and should proceed despite these acknowledged difficulties. Focus on the practical utility and professional register.

Materials

Tasks

From your proposal, write one sentence where you use 'Notwithstanding' to acknowledge an obstacle and then state an overriding strategic reason. Ensure it follows the C2 structure precisely.

Write one sentence from your proposal using 'Notwithstanding + noun phrase, main clause' to highlight a challenge that is overcome by a strategic imperative.
