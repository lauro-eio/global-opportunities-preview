L-IN

Lead-In: Talking About Money at Work

Teacher notes

1. Greet learners and introduce the topic: talking about money in different situations.
2. Ask learners to discuss the first two questions in pairs, then open up for a brief whole-class share.
3. Transition to the third question about work contexts. Emphasize the difference in tone and privacy.
4. Do not pre-teach the target language, focus purely on the pragmatic context.

Student handout

Money Talk: Office vs. Home

Imagine you need to talk about money. How do you do it?

1. Who do you talk about money with at home (family, friends)? What kind of language do you use? (e.g., casual, serious, joking)
2. Is it a private topic or an open one?
3. Now, think about work. How is talking about money different in these situations?
* Joking with colleagues in the break room about being 'broke'.
* Asking your supervisor about overtime pay or a pay rise.
* Discussing company expenses with a client.

Think about the tone you use and the level of privacy.

GIST

Gist Reading: Overtime Policy Update

Teacher notes

1. Tell learners they will read a memo about a new company policy.
2. Ask them to read the memo quickly to get the main idea.
3. Set a time limit for reading (e.g., 2 minutes).
4. Have them answer the multiple-choice question to check their overall understanding. Emphasize that they do not need to understand every word at this stage.

Student handout

New Company Guidelines

Read the memo quickly to understand the main purpose. Choose the best answer.

Materials

Memo: Update on Overtime Approval Process

Subject: Update: Overtime Approval Process
To: All Staff
From: HR Department
Date: [Current Date]

Dear Team,

This memo provides an update on our company's overtime approval process, effective next month. Our goal is to ensure fair compensation and efficient project management.

You can claim overtime as long as it has been pre-approved by your direct supervisor. Please submit your overtime request form at least 24 hours before the intended extra work. Approval is necessary to comply with budget guidelines.

Payment for overtime hours will be processed with your regular monthly salary. Unless your request is submitted with all required details, there may be a delay in processing. Incorrect forms will be returned for completion.

Provided that all departmental project targets are met, supervisors have the discretion to approve reasonable overtime requests for urgent tasks. We understand that sometimes extra effort is needed.

For any questions, please consult your supervisor or the HR department.

Thank you for your cooperation.
HR Department.

GD

Guided Discovery: Conditional Conjunctions

Teacher notes

1. Draw learners' attention to the bold phrases in the memo: as long as, unless, provided that.
2. Ask learners to match each phrase to its meaning using the match_pairs task. Discuss answers to ensure correct understanding.
3. Discuss the function: These words link conditions. Elicit from learners how these words set a condition for an approval, payment, or action.
4. Focus on pronunciation: Write 'You can claim overtime' and 'as long as it has been pre-approved'. Highlight the connected speech in as long as (weak forms, /əz lɒŋ əz/) and the contraction of 'will' in other sentences (e.g., 'there'll be', 'we'll approve'). Model and drill these. Explain that these words are common in formal/workplace communication about rules and agreements.

Student handout

Conditions for Agreement

Look at the memo again. Pay attention to the bold phrases:

* as long as
* unless
* provided that

These phrases are used to talk about conditions or agreements. They tell you what must happen for something else to be true or possible.

FP

Freer Practice: Workplace Scenarios

Teacher notes

1. Explain that learners will now use the new phrases in a more realistic work context.
2. Divide learners into small groups (3-4 people).
3. Assign each group one or two scenarios from the student_content or have them choose.
4. Instruct them to discuss the scenario, using as long as, unless, and provided that to negotiate or set conditions.
5. Circulate and monitor, noting good usage and errors related to the target language. Do not interrupt for corrections unless communication breaks down.
6. After 5-7 minutes, bring the class back together for a brief feedback session. Ask groups to share one key condition they discussed for their scenario.
7. Provide delayed error correction on target language, focusing on clarity and appropriacy.

Student handout

Discussing Work Agreements

Work in small groups. Discuss the following workplace scenarios. Use as long as, unless, and provided that to talk about conditions and agreements.

Scenario 1: New Project Deadline
Your team has a new, urgent project. Your supervisor says you can work extra hours from home to finish it, but there are conditions.
* What conditions would you expect for working from home on a tight deadline?
* What conditions might your supervisor set for your team to work from home?

Scenario 2: Company Training Course
Your company offers a free online training course to improve skills. It's a great opportunity, but it's outside work hours.
* What conditions would make you decide to do the course?
* What conditions might the company have for you to join this course?

Scenario 3: Office Supplies Budget
Your department needs new office chairs. The manager says they can be bought, but there are budget rules.
* What conditions might the manager give for approving new chairs?
* What might happen if those conditions aren't met?

Example sentences:
* 'We can work from home as long as we complete our tasks on time.'
* 'The company won't pay for the course unless you finish all modules.'
* 'Provided that we find a cheaper supplier, we can buy new chairs.'
