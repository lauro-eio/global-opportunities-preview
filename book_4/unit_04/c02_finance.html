Lead-In: Budgeting and Expenses

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and establish a friendly atmosphere.
2. Introduce the topic: 'Today, we're going to talk about money, specifically your expenses. Not about saving, but about understanding where your money goes.'
3. Write 'Work Expenses' and 'Personal Expenses' on the board.
4. Ask students to think about the last month and list three different costs they had. Prompt with examples if needed: 'Think about things like transport to work, a new tool for your job, or maybe food and bills at home.'
5. In pairs or small groups, have students categorize their listed costs as 'Work-related', 'Personal', or 'Mixed'. Circulate and monitor, encouraging use of vocabulary like expenses, categories, work_costs, personal_costs.
6. Briefly elicit a few examples from the class, focusing on the categorization process, not the amounts or personal financial details.
7. Transition to the next stage by explaining that managing these costs often involves making decisions about future actions.

Test 1: What will happen?

Teacher notes

1. Explain that this is a short test, not for a grade, but to see what they already know about talking about future possibilities and decisions.
2. Hand out the task or display it.
3. Instruct students to read the email and choose the best option to complete the sentences.

TCH

Teach: First Conditional for Consequences

Teacher notes

1. Begin by reviewing the answers from T1, highlighting the common errors (e.g., 'If we will cut...' and 'Unless we don't control...').
2. Meaning: Explain that we use the First Conditional to talk about a possible future condition and its certain future consequence. Write 'If + present, will + infinitive' on the board.
- CCQ: 'Are we talking about past events or future possibilities?' (Future possibilities) - Elicit an example related to their work.
- CCQ: 'Is it possible that we will overspend next month?' (Yes) - Connect to a real-world scenario.
- CCQ: 'If we overspend, is the fine guaranteed?' (Yes) - Emphasize the certainty of the consequence if the condition is met.
3. Form (Affirmative): Write 'If + Subject + Present Simple, Subject + will + infinitive' on the board. Use an example: 'If we cut costs, we will save money.' Correct error 1: 'If we will cut the logistics expenses, we will save money.' -> 'If we cut the logistics expenses, we will save money.' Explain that 'will' is only in the main clause, not the 'if' clause.
4. Form (Negative): Write 'If + Subject + do/does not + verb, Subject + will not + infinitive' on the board. Example: 'If we don't track our budget, we won't know our personal_costs.' Introduce 'Unless' as meaning 'if not'. Correct error 2: 'Unless we don't submit the payslip details, we won't get taxed correctly.' -> 'Unless we submit the payslip details, we won't get taxed correctly.' or 'If we don't submit the payslip details, we won't get taxed correctly.' Explain 'Unless' already includes the negative idea.
5. Form (Question): Write 'Will + Subject + infinitive + if + Subject + Present Simple?' Example: 'Will we get a bonus if we meet all our targets?'
6. Pronunciation: Model sentences, drawing attention to contractions (e.g., 'I'll', 'we'll', 'they'll', 'won't'). Have students repeat.
7. Provide a quick practice task to check understanding of form and meaning. Circulate and provide immediate feedback.

Student handout

Conditions and Outcomes

We use the First Conditional to talk about a real possibility in the future and its likely result.

Meaning: We're talking about future possibilities and certain consequences.

Form:

Affirmative: If + Subject + Present Simple, Subject + will + infinitive
Example: If we reduce our work_costs, we will improve our budget.

Negative: If + Subject + do/does not + verb, Subject + will not + infinitive
Example: If we don't track our expenses, we won't know where our money goes.

'Unless' means 'if not'. We use it with an affirmative verb after it.
Incorrect: Unless we don't check the budget, we'll overspend.
Correct: Unless we check the budget, we'll overspend. (Meaning: If we don't check the budget...)

Question: Will + Subject + infinitive + if + Subject + Present Simple?
Example: Will we meet our budget targets if we cut staff overtime?

T2

Test 2: Budget Meeting Scenarios

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now practice using the First Conditional in a more realistic work scenario.
2. Divide students into pairs or small groups (3-4).
3. Present the scenario: 'Imagine your team needs to discuss future spending. You have some difficult decisions about work_costs and personal_costs (e.g., team lunch budget, travel for training, office supplies).' Give them 2-3 specific scenarios to discuss, e.g., 'Scenario A: Your travel budget is cut by 15% next quarter.'
4. Instruct them to use the First Conditional to discuss the consequences and make decisions. Encourage them to use both affirmative and negative forms, and to ask questions.
5. Circulate and monitor, providing support and correcting errors, especially those from T1 and TCH. Pay attention to natural use of 'Unless'.
6. After sufficient practice, bring the class back together. Ask each group to share one decision they made and the conditional sentences they used to explain it.
7. Model Answer Example (for teacher reference): 'If we cut the office supply budget, we will have to reuse more items.' / 'Unless we find a cheaper supplier, we won't be able to buy new tools.' / 'Will we still be able to attend the conference if we don't have enough travel funds?'

Student handout

Planning for the Future

Imagine you are in a team meeting to discuss your department's budget for the next three months. You need to make some decisions about work_costs and how they might affect the team or individual personal_costs.

Use the First Conditional to discuss the possible consequences of different decisions. Make sure to use 'If...' and 'Unless...' statements, and also ask questions.

Discuss the following scenarios with your partner:

1. Travel Expenses: Your department's budget for work travel is cut by 20%. How will this affect your projects and team?
Example: "If we cut travel, we'll miss important client meetings."

2. Office Supplies: The budget for office supplies (pens, paper, printing) is frozen for the next quarter. What will you do?

3. Team Training: There's a chance to attend a useful training course, but it costs a lot. Your manager says you can only go if you find the money from elsewhere in your budget.

Be ready to share some of your decisions and conditional sentences with the class.
