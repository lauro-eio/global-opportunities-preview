Lead-In: Financial Priorities

Teacher notes

1. Start by asking learners to think about their personal or household budgets.
2. Explain that everyone has to make choices, especially when money is tight.
3. Ask: "Imagine you have less money next month. What is one bill or goal you must pay first? Why is it essential for your work or family?"
4. Then ask: "What is one expense you would cut immediately if money was tight?"
5. Encourage learners to share examples like rent, tools for work, food for kids, or essential transport, and contrast with non-essentials.
6. Elicit phrases related to budgeting, cutting costs, prioritising, saving, expenses.

TCH

Teach: First Conditional for Financial Planning

Teacher notes

1. Review answers from T1, highlighting common errors noticed during monitoring.
2. Focus on the First Conditional. Write the form on the board: "If + Present Simple, will + infinitive."
3. Explain the meaning: "We use it for future possibilities and their certain consequences dependent on conditions."
4. Use Concept Check Questions (CCQs):
- "Are we talking about past events or future possibilities? (Future possibilities)"
- "Is it possible that we will overspend next month? (Yes)"
- "If we overspend, is the fine guaranteed? (Yes)"
5. Address the predicted errors:
- "If we will cut the logistics expenses, we will save money." -> Explain no 'will' in the 'if' clause.
- "Unless we don't submit the payslip details, we won't get taxed correctly." -> Explain 'unless' already implies negation, so no 'don't'.
6. Provide a short task for form practice.

Student handout

Understanding Consequences

Let's look at the First Conditional for talking about financial consequences.

We use the First Conditional to talk about things that are possible in the future and their certain results.

Form: If + Subject + Present Simple, Subject + will + infinitive
Example: If we cut our travel budget, we will save money.

Complete the sentences with the correct form of the verbs in brackets.

Tasks

T2

Test 2: Budgeting for New Projects

Teacher notes

1. Divide learners into small groups or pairs.
2. Explain the scenario: a small business needs to decide on budget cuts and new investments for the next quarter.
3. Instruct them to discuss and decide, using First Conditional language to explain the consequences of their choices.
4. Monitor for correct usage of the First Conditional.
5. Provide an example sentence on the board if needed: "If we invest in training, our team will be more efficient."
6. After group discussion, ask one or two groups to share their top priorities and why.

Student handout

Planning for the Future

Your company has a tight budget next quarter but also wants to invest in a new project. You need to make some decisions.

Scenario: A small logistics company wants to expand its delivery routes but needs to save money first.

Discuss in pairs or small groups:

1. What three expenses could the company cut to save money? (e.g., reduce fuel, delay equipment upgrades, cut overtime hours)
2. What one new investment should the company make to improve? (e.g., new software, staff training, marketing)

Use the First Conditional to explain your choices and their consequences.

Example: If we delay new truck purchases, we will have more money for marketing.
