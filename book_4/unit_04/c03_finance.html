L-IN

Lead-In: Sharing Costs Stories

Teacher notes

1. Divide learners into pairs or small groups.
2. Ask learners to discuss the provided questions about shared costs.
3. Encourage them to share real experiences without focusing on blame, but rather on how disagreements were handled.
4. Monitor for initial ideas and identify any common challenges or vocabulary gaps related to the theme.

Student handout

When Bills Get Tricky

Have you ever had to split costs with someone, like rent, utilities, fuel, or even a shared work meal?

Discuss these questions with a partner:
1. What was the situation? (e.g., with a housemate, a coworker, a family member)
2. What was the cost you had to split?
3. Was it easy or difficult to decide what was fair?
4. How did you discuss it? Did you reach an agreement?

MOD

Model: Discussing a Shared Expense

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the model dialogue as two colleagues discussing an office lunch bill.
2. Ask learners to read or listen to the dialogue.
3. Instruct them to complete the short text task to check their understanding of the main points.
4. Clarify any specific vocabulary that learners ask about, but avoid pre-teaching extensively.

Student handout

The Office Lunch Bill

Read the conversation between David and Sarah about their recent team lunch. They need to decide how to split the expense.

David: Hey Sarah, about that team lunch bill from yesterday.
Sarah: Oh, right. What do you think is the best way to handle it? We usually just split it equally, but some people only had a salad.
David: Exactly. I think it’s fairer if everyone pays for what they ordered. My main course was a bit more expensive than a sandwich.
Sarah: I agree. That makes sense. So, we should ask everyone to look at their receipt and just pay their own amount?
David: Yeah, that's what I was thinking. It avoids any awkwardness about someone paying too much for something they didn't really have. It's about fairness, right?
Sarah: Definitely. And what about the tip? Should we just divide that by the number of people who were there?
David: Good point. I think dividing the tip and any shared drinks, like a bottle of water for the table, equally among everyone who attended would be the most straightforward.
Sarah: Okay, so everyone pays their own food, and the tip/shared drinks are split equally. Sounds like a plan. I'll send out an email about it.
David: Perfect. Thanks, Sarah.

FP

Fluency Practice: Negotiating Shared Expenses

Teacher notes

1. Divide learners into pairs. Assign 'Manager' and 'Employee' roles for a roleplay scenario.
2. Clearly explain the situation and the task: they must agree on a fair way to split the cost of shared office supplies.
3. Emphasize that the goal is fluent communication and negotiation.
4. Set a time limit for the discussion (e.g., 5-7 minutes).
5. Monitor for effective communication, use of target language (shared costs, splitting, fairness, negotiation, expenses), and overall fluency. Do not interrupt for error correction.

Student handout

The Shared Office Supplies

Work with a partner. You are going to roleplay a situation about splitting shared office supply expenses.

Scenario:
You and your coworker bought some office supplies together last month. The total bill was quite high, and you both used different amounts of certain items. You need to discuss and agree on a fair way to split the expenses for these shared costs.

Role A: Manager
You manage a small team. You bought most of the printer paper and pens, but your coworker used more of the specialized design paper and ink. You want to make sure the splitting is fair for everyone.

Role B: Employee
You are part of a small team. You used a lot of the specialized design paper and ink for a big project, but didn't use much of the general printer paper or pens. You want to discuss a fair way to pay.

Task: Discuss and negotiate how to split the office supply bill. Try to reach an agreement that you both think is fair.

FBDC

Feedback and Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Open with content feedback: Ask pairs if they reached an agreement and how they felt about the outcome. Did they find a fair solution?
2. Address any general communication issues observed during the activity.
3. Move to delayed language correction: Write anonymized errors (lexical or phonological) observed during the activity on the board.
4. Guide learners to self-correct or peer-correct the errors, focusing on clarity and appropriacy in the context of discussing shared costs.
5. Re-practice any phrases that were problematic or could be improved.

Student handout

Reflecting on Our Discussion

Think about your discussion:
1. Did you manage to agree on a fair way to split the expenses?
2. What was the most challenging part of the negotiation?
3. What phrases did you use that were particularly helpful?

Now, let's look at some of the language we used during the task. Can we improve anything?
