L-IN

Lead-In: Discussing Work Expenses

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and introduce the topic: work-related expenses.
2. Ask students to think about their current or past jobs.
3. Facilitate a brief discussion using the prompts in student_content.
4. Encourage students to share one type of cost they might need to claim back (e.g., a specific tool, travel for work, uniform repair).
5. Discuss who would need to approve such a claim in their workplace.
6. Emphasize that the focus is on practical, work-related costs and the process of getting reimbursed, not personal budgeting tips.

Student handout

Work Costs & Your Job

In your job, have you ever paid for something you needed for work and expected to get the money back from your company?

Think about:
1. What kind of items or services might you need to pay for yourself, but are for your job?
2. Who would you need to ask for approval before or after buying it?
3. Why is it important to claim these costs back?

Share one work cost you might claim back and who approves it.

MOD

Model Analysis: Expense Claim Note

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the model text as an example of an expense claim note.
2. Ask students to read the note silently or aloud in pairs.
3. Instruct students to complete the tasks individually.
4. Monitor students as they work, providing help with vocabulary if needed.
5. Conduct a whole-class feedback session to check answers for each task.
6. Discuss key features of the note: clarity, conciseness, necessary information, and professional tone.

Student handout

Understanding a Claim Note

Materials

Expense Reimbursement Request

Subject: Expense Reimbursement Request - Safety Boots Purchase
Date: 22nd October 2023
To: Sarah Davies, Operations Manager
From: Marco Rossi, Warehouse Operative

Dear Sarah Davies,

I am writing to request reimbursement for a pair of safety boots purchased on 18th October 2023. These boots are essential personal protective equipment for my role in the warehouse, ensuring my safety while handling heavy items and operating machinery.

The total cost of the safety boots was £65.00. I have attached the original receipt (Receipt ID: SB-2023-XYZ) for your review.

Please let me know if any further information or documentation is required to process this claim.

Thank you for your time and assistance.

Sincerely,

Marco Rossi
Employee ID: 78901

PREP

Preparation: Useful Phrases for Your Claim

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now prepare to write their own expense claim notes.
2. Ask students to recall the work cost they discussed in the Lead-In or think of a new one (e.g., travel expenses, tools, uniform repair).
3. Direct students to the 'Useful Phrases for an Expense Claim' table.
4. Go through each column, explaining the purpose of the phrases and checking understanding.
5. In pairs or small groups, ask students to discuss which phrases they would use for their chosen expense and why.
6. Encourage students to brainstorm any other relevant vocabulary related to their specific claim.

FP

Freer Practice: Writing Your Expense Claim

Teacher notes

1. Clearly explain the writing task to students.
2. Remind them to choose one work cost they want to claim back, identify the manager as the audience, and aim for a clear, concise note (approx. 50-100 words).
3. Advise students to use the model text and the useful phrases from the PREP stage.
4. Students should write their claim individually.
5. Monitor students' writing, offering support with vocabulary and sentence structure but allowing them to produce their own text.
6. Do not correct every error at this stage; focus on clarity of message and inclusion of key information.
7. Prepare a sample answer for teacher reference (e.g., for claiming bus tickets for a client meeting).

Student handout

My Expense Reimbursement

Now it's time to write your own expense claim or reimbursement note.

Choose ONE work cost you would claim back from your employer. It could be the one you thought about earlier, or a new one.

Your task: Write a short email or note to your manager requesting reimbursement for this expense.

Audience: Your manager or supervisor.
Purpose: To get money back for a work expense.
Length: Approximately 50-100 words.

Remember to:
* State clearly what you are claiming for.
* Explain why it was necessary for your job.
* State the cost.
* Mention that you have attached (or will attach) the receipt.
* Be polite and professional.

FBDC

Feedback & Correction

Teacher notes

1. Explain the feedback process: first, content and clarity (peer feedback), then language (teacher feedback).
2. Pair students up and ask them to exchange their notes.
3. Instruct students to use the questions in student_content to guide their feedback. Emphasize constructive criticism focusing on the message and necessary information.
4. Circulate and monitor peer feedback, listening for common errors or strengths.
5. Collect the notes for teacher correction. Focus on recurring language errors relevant to the target language (e.g., prepositions, verb tense related to claims, clarity of phrasing).
6. Provide written feedback on individual notes or conduct a short whole-class feedback session addressing common errors without identifying individual writers.

Student handout

Reviewing Your Claim

Now, exchange your expense claim note with a partner. Read your partner's note carefully and answer these questions. Give them helpful feedback.

Content Feedback:
1. Is it clear what expense your partner is claiming for?
2. Is the reason for the expense clear and professional?
3. Does the note mention the cost and the receipt?
4. Is the tone polite and appropriate for a manager?

Language Feedback:
1. Can you understand everything easily?
2. Are there any small mistakes that make it less clear?

After feedback, you can make improvements to your note.
