A Proactive Welcome

Read the conversation between Maria, a team leader, and Tom, a supervisor, discussing the onboarding of a new colleague, Lena. Pay attention to the strategies they mention for making Lena feel included.

Materials

Integrating Lena

Maria: Hi Tom, can we quickly chat about Lena? It’s her second week, and I want to make sure she feels fully integrated.
Tom: Absolutely, Maria. How's she settling in?
Maria: She’s doing well with the tasks, but I'm thinking about the social side. We’ve done the basic onboarding process, like showing her the systems and introducing her to key people.
Tom: Right. Did you assign her a buddy?
Maria: Yes, Mark's been great. He’s been taking her to lunch and explaining some of our team’s unwritten rules. We also made sure to invite her to the team's Friday after-work gathering.
Tom: That’s good. And culturally, is there anything specific we should consider? I know she's from a different background.
Maria: We’ve encouraged everyone to share a bit about their backgrounds during coffee breaks, which helps build a sense of community. Also, I’ve scheduled a follow-up with her next week to check in on how she feels about our communication style and any cultural nuances she might have observed.
Tom: Excellent. It's crucial to foster inclusion from day one. We want her to feel like a truly valued part of the team, not just someone completing tasks. What about involving her in a small project she can lead, to give her ownership quickly?
Maria: Great idea. That would definitely help with her long-term team integration. I’ll look for something suitable. We want her to thrive here.

Making Them Feel at Home

Work in pairs or small groups. Role-play the following scenario:

Scenario: A new colleague from a different country has just joined your team. You (as the team leader/team member) are meeting them on their first day or early in their first week.

Student A (New Colleague): You are new to the company and the country. You might feel a bit overwhelmed or unsure about how things work. Ask questions if you need to.

Student B (Team Leader/Manager): Your goal is to make the new colleague feel welcome, explain the onboarding process, foster a sense of community, and demonstrate strategies for team integration. Use phrases and ideas from the previous activity.

Student C (Team Member - optional): Support the team leader by introducing yourself, offering help, and contributing to the welcoming atmosphere. Highlight aspects of the team's culture that promote inclusion.

Focus on:
* Making the new colleague feel comfortable.
* Clearly explaining initial steps.
* Showing how your team supports cultural assimilation and inclusion.
* Practicing natural, fluent conversation.
