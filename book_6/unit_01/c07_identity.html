Lead-In: Global Teams, Local Realities

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking students if their current or past companies operate in multiple countries.
2. Pose the lead-in brief question: "Have you noticed differences in how policies are implemented or how team members interact based on their location? Give an example."
3. Facilitate a brief discussion, encouraging students to share personal experiences and observations.
4. Elicit advanced vocabulary related to cultural nuances, corporate identity, local customs, and adaptation.
5. Note down any interesting expressions or common pitfalls related to advanced sentence structures for later reference.

TCH

Teach: Clarifying Cleft Sentences for Emphasis

Teacher notes

1. Based on errors observed in T1, clarify the use of cleft sentences ('It' and 'What' clefts).
2. Present the forms clearly: 'It + is/was + emphasized element + that/who + clause' and 'What + clause + is/was + emphasized element'.
3. Explain the meaning: to isolate and emphasize a specific part of the sentence (subject, object, or adverbial).
4. Use the Concept Check Questions (CCQs) from the linguistic target during explanation:
- "Are we emphasizing a specific ethical standard or cultural factor over others?" (Yes)
- "Does this structure make the primary source of workplace friction or alignment stand out?" (Yes)
5. Provide clear examples related to corporate culture and ethical nuances.
6. Guide students through the practice tasks, encouraging them to think about what element they want to emphasize.

Student handout

Focusing Your Message

Cleft sentences are powerful tools for emphasizing a particular piece of information. They help us highlight ethical parameters, diversity requirements, or compliance codes that are central to a discussion.

Look at the two main forms we use:

Form 1: It-clefts
It + is/was + emphasized element + that/who + clause
Example: It is _our strong commitment to ethical sourcing_ that defines our global supply chain policy.

Form 2: What-clefts
What + clause + is/was + emphasized element
Example: What _many international teams struggle with_ is _balancing global directives with local cultural norms_.

T2

Test 2: Analyzing Corporate Dilemmas with Emphasis

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the case study scenario. Explain that students will read about a common dilemma in international businesses.
2. Ask students to read the scenario individually.
3. Facilitate a group discussion. Encourage students to identify the core issues and brainstorm solutions.
4. Crucially, remind students to use 'It-cleft' and 'What-cleft' sentences to emphasize key aspects of the problem, proposed solutions, or ethical considerations.
5. Circulate and provide support, prompting students to use the target structures.
6. After the discussion, use the short_text tasks to check understanding of the scenario and specific application of the grammar.
7. For question 3, prompt students to generate a few sentences orally or in writing, checking for correct cleft sentence usage.

Student handout

Bridging the Cultural Divide: A Case Study

Read the following scenario about a common challenge in international business. Then, discuss the questions with your group, making sure to use cleft sentences to emphasize the most important points.

---
Scenario: The Global Wellness Initiative

_Horizon Logistics, a company with branches in 15 countries, has launched a 'Global Wellness Initiative' from its European headquarters. The initiative includes a mandatory daily 15-minute 'mindfulness break' for all employees, encouraging meditation or quiet reflection. While this was well-received in some regions, the branch in a rapidly developing Asian market reported low engagement. Local managers explained that in their culture, public displays of quiet reflection are often seen as unproductive or even disrespectful of team effort, especially during peak work hours. Furthermore, many employees prefer active group sports for stress relief, a practice not covered by the current initiative. The headquarters insists on a unified corporate identity, while the local team stresses cultural nuances and the need for adaptation._

---

1. What is the core tension in this scenario?
2. What do you think headquarters is trying to achieve, and what is the local team emphasizing?
3. How could Horizon Logistics adapt its initiative to better suit local cultural norms while maintaining a sense of global corporate identity? Use cleft sentences to highlight your key suggestions.
