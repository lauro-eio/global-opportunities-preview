First Impressions at Work

Before we listen, let's think about how we perceive others in a work environment.

1. Have you ever heard a comment that generalized about a group of professionals or a person based on where they come from or their personal background?
2. How might these kinds of assumptions or preconceptions make it difficult for a team to work well together?
3. What challenges could arise in a diverse workplace if people rely on stereotypes instead of getting to know their colleagues individually?

Understanding Key Terms

We're going to hear about unconscious bias in the workplace. Before we listen, let's make sure we understand some important words. Match the terms to their definitions.

Materials

Vocabulary
  • unconscious bias
  • microaggressions
  • preconceptions
  • diversity
  • equity
Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • unconscious bias — Automatic judgments or assumptions made without conscious awareness, often influencing decisions.
  • microaggressions — Subtle, often unintentional remarks or actions that communicate negative or hostile messages to a person based on their background.
  • preconceptions — Ideas or opinions formed before having full knowledge or experience; often a bias.
  • diversity — The presence of a wide range of different people, backgrounds, or qualities within a group or organization.
  • equity — Fairness and impartiality, ensuring everyone has access to the same opportunities and resources, considering individual needs.

Listening for Main Ideas: The Team Meeting

You will hear a conversation between three colleagues, Sarah, Mark, and Lena, during a team meeting. Listen for the main point of their discussion. Don't worry about every detail for now.

Materials

Team Meeting: Addressing Workplace Perceptions

(Sound of a video conference connecting, gentle background hum)

Sarah: Alright team, let's get started. Thanks for joining this morning. As you know, we're focusing on improving team cohesion, especially as we bring more new people into the department. I wanted to open a discussion about unconscious bias.

Mark: Unconscious bias? What exactly does that mean in our day-to-day, Sarah? We all work well together, don't we?

Sarah: Absolutely, Mark. Our team is fantastic. But sometimes, without even realizing it, we can make quick judgments or hold preconceptions about people. For example, assuming someone isn't interested in a leadership role because they're quiet in meetings, or thinking a new hire from a certain background won't grasp our technical systems as quickly. These aren't intentional, but they can create subtle barriers.

Lena: I think I understand. So, it's about those small, often overlooked interactions? Things that might seem harmless but actually send a message?

Sarah: Exactly, Lena. Those are often called microaggressions. They can erode trust and make colleagues feel undervalued over time. Think about how we assign tasks or choose who presents a report – are we always thinking about equity and giving everyone a fair chance, or are we defaulting to what feels familiar?

Mark: Hmm, I see your point. I might have unintentionally done that a few times, assigning a presentation to someone I know is naturally outgoing, without considering others. So, how do we actively address these unconscious bias issues without making everyone feel like they're being accused?

Sarah: Good question, Mark. It's not about blame, it's about awareness and growth. We need to focus on understanding our own internal filters. One practical step is to pause before making assumptions. Ask yourself: 'Am I making a decision based on facts, or on a gut feeling about this person's background or personality type?' We also need to actively seek out different perspectives. Our diversity is our strength, and we need to make sure everyone feels truly included and empowered to contribute.
