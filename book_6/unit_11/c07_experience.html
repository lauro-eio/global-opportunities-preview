L-IN

Lead-In: Reflecting on Work-Life Integration

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and briefly review the unit theme of 'Experience' focusing on personal well-being.
2. Introduce the concept of 'switching off' from work.
3. Ask students to discuss the lead-in questions in pairs or small groups.
4. Elicit initial ideas and common challenges, focusing on their experiences with work-life balance and personal boundaries.
5. Encourage the use of advanced language related to regret or hypothetical past situations, if it naturally arises, but do not pre-teach.

Student handout

Before and After Hours

In today’s demanding work environments, many professionals find it challenging to truly 'switch off' from work once they leave the office or finish their shift. The lines between professional and personal life can blur, impacting mental well-being and work-life balance.

Discuss in small groups:
1. What specific challenges do you face when trying to create separation between your work and personal life?
2. What strategies do you currently use, or wish you could use, to establish stronger personal boundaries and protect your off-work hours?
3. Have you ever felt regret about a past decision that negatively impacted your self-care strategies or led to increased stress reduction needs?

T1

Test 1: Diagnosing Regret Structures

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will complete a short diagnostic task related to expressing regret or hypothetical past situations.
2. Emphasize that this is to identify areas for review, not a formal assessment.
3. Instruct students to choose the best option to complete each sentence, focusing on grammatical accuracy and natural expression.
4. Circulate and monitor, noting common errors without correcting immediately. Pay close attention to the use of verb tenses after 'wish', 'if only', and 'would rather'.

TCH

Teach: Clarifying Hypothetical Regrets

Teacher notes

1. Based on errors observed in T1, clarify the use of 'wish/if only' and 'would rather' followed by the past perfect.
2. Present the form clearly using the provided column banks.
3. Elicit or explain the meaning: expressing regret about a past situation that cannot be changed. Use the Concept Check Questions (CCQs) from the linguistic target: 'Did the specific strategic challenge or structural failure happen in a past timeframe? (Yes)', 'Can we physically alter the historical outcome of this professional choice now? (No)'.
4. Highlight common errors (e.g., using 'would have' instead of 'had').
5. Guide students through the controlled practice task, encouraging them to think about past work-related decisions.
6. Check answers and provide further examples as needed.

Student handout

Understanding What Could Have Been

When we want to express regret about something that happened or didn't happen in the past, and which we cannot change now, we use specific grammatical structures. These are powerful ways to reflect on strategic challenges and operational setbacks.

Look at the structures below. Notice how the past perfect tense is used after 'wish', 'if only', and 'would rather' to talk about a past that is now fixed. Remember, the challenge or failure did happen in the past, and we cannot change its historical outcome.

T2

Test 2: Integrating Regrets and Boundary Setting

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the reading passage as a scenario relevant to work-life balance.
2. Ask students to read the passage and identify the core issue.
3. Instruct students to complete the comprehension check task. Check answers quickly.
4. Explain the second task: students should reflect on Maria's situation and write sentences expressing advice or regret from her perspective, or from the perspective of someone observing her.
5. Circulate and monitor, looking for accurate use of 'wish/if only' and 'would rather + past perfect'.
6. Provide feedback on structure and meaning. Acknowledge different valid responses for the open question, but ensure grammatical accuracy. Sample answer for Task 2-2: 'Maria wishes she had communicated her limits more clearly from the start.' or 'If only Maria had realized the impact on her mental well-being sooner.'

Student handout

Learning from Past Work Experiences

Read the following scenario about Maria. Think about her situation and how it relates to work-life balance and personal boundaries.

Materials

Maria's Overload

Maria is a dedicated logistics coordinator. Six months ago, her company undertook a major digital transformation project. Eager to prove herself, Maria voluntarily took on significant extra responsibilities, often working late into the evenings and checking emails on weekends. She felt immense pressure to maintain high performance. Recently, Maria started experiencing constant fatigue and found it difficult to concentrate even on simple tasks. Her friends noticed she had become withdrawn and her usual vibrant personality was fading. She realizes now that she failed to establish crucial personal boundaries and didn't implement proper self-care strategies when the project intensified.
