L-IN

Lead-In: Initial Reactions to a Merger

Teacher notes

1. Write 'Merger Announced!' on the board.
2. Ask students if they've ever experienced a company merger or acquisition, or know someone who has.
3. Put students in pairs or small groups.
4. Introduce the scenario: 'Your company just announced it's merging with another firm.'
5. Give students 3-4 minutes to discuss the prompts.
6. Elicit some initial positive and negative thoughts from the class, writing key words on the board.

Student handout

First Thoughts on the News

Your company has just announced it's merging with another firm. This is big news!

In pairs, discuss your initial reactions. Consider both the good and the bad.

1. What are your immediate positive thoughts about this merger?
2. What are your immediate concerns or negative thoughts?
3. How do you think this news might be affecting colleagues in other departments?

MOD

Model Dialogue: Discussing Merger Impacts

Teacher notes

1. Tell students they will read a conversation between two colleagues, Maria and Tom, discussing a recent merger announcement.
2. Ask them to read the dialogue to get a general idea of their concerns.
3. For a second reading, ask students to identify specific impacts Maria and Tom discuss regarding departmental structures and employee roles.
4. Check understanding: 'What is Maria worried about?' 'What is Tom trying to emphasize?'

Student handout

Colleagues Talk Strategy

Maria and Tom are colleagues. They’ve just heard about the merger and are discussing its potential impacts.

Maria: I'm still processing the news about the merger. It's a lot to take in.

Tom: I know, right? My mind immediately jumped to our department. I'm wondering how this corporate restructuring will affect us.

Maria: Exactly! I'm particularly worried about employee retention. Will our key people stay, or will they look for new opportunities if things feel uncertain?

Tom: That’s a valid concern. Management is probably talking about synergy expectations and streamlining operations, but on the ground, it often means job overlaps or redundancies.

Maria: And what about our roles? Will there be significant changes to our responsibilities? I can see some serious integration challenges ahead, especially with different company cultures and systems.

Tom: Absolutely. I heard rumors about potential changes to the sales team's structure. If they merge, it could impact how we collaborate, and even our reporting lines could shift.

Maria: And departmental budgets? Will we have the resources to adapt to new directives? It feels like we're heading into uncharted territory.

Tom: It does. We need to stay informed and be ready to adapt. It's probably wise to start thinking about what skills might become more valuable post-merger.

Maria: Good point. I guess it's all about navigating the uncertainty and preparing for the changes ahead.

PREP

Preparation: Useful Phrases for Discussion

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will prepare for a detailed discussion about the merger.
2. Direct students to the 'Useful Phrases' and 'Discussion Points' columns.
3. Advise them to read through the phrases, clarifying any new vocabulary.
4. Ask students to make notes under 'My Thoughts & Questions' for each discussion point, using some of the useful phrases.
5. Circulate and assist with vocabulary and idea generation. Allow 5-7 minutes for preparation.

FP

Fluency Practice: Merger Impact Discussion

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs or small groups (3 maximum).
2. Explain that they will have a discussion with their colleagues about the merger, focusing on specific impacts.
3. Instruct them to use the phrases and their notes from the PREP stage.
4. Encourage them to listen actively and respond to their partners' points.
5. Set a time limit for the discussion (e.g., 8-10 minutes).
6. Monitor for fluency and effective communication, noting down any common errors or excellent uses of language for FBDC.

Student handout

Discussing the Future

Imagine you are meeting with colleagues to discuss the recent merger announcement in more detail. Use your notes and the useful phrases from the previous activity.

Discuss the following with your partner(s):

* What specific changes do you anticipate for departmental structures?
* How might your own employee roles or responsibilities change?
* What are the biggest integration challenges you foresee, and how could they be managed?
* What are your hopes for synergy expectations? Where do you see potential benefits?
* What strategies could the company use for employee retention during this period of corporate restructuring?

Remember to listen to your colleagues and ask follow-up questions.

FBDC

Feedback & Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Content Feedback (3-4 mins): Bring the class back together. Ask groups to share one key point or concern they discussed. Elicit any surprising insights or common themes.
2. Language Feedback (5-7 mins): Refer to notes taken during the FP stage. Write 3-5 anonymous errors on the board (e.g., grammatical mistakes, awkward phrasing) and ask students to correct them together. Also, highlight 2-3 examples of good language use (e.g., specific target vocabulary, fluent expressions).
3. Reiterate the importance of clear communication during periods of change in the workplace.

Student handout

Reflecting on Our Discussion

Well done everyone! It's important to be able to discuss complex topics like mergers clearly and professionally.

Now, let's reflect on our discussions and some of the language we used.
