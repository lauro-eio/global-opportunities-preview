L-IN

Lead-In: Your Annual Review Highlight

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and briefly introduce the topic of performance reviews.
2. Read the lead-in question aloud to the class.
3. Ask students to think for a minute individually about their specific accomplishment.
4. Put students in pairs or small groups to discuss their ideas. Encourage them to explain why their chosen accomplishment is valuable to the company's mission.
5. Bring the class back together and ask a few volunteers to share their highlights and reasons.
6. Emphasize that linking individual work to company goals is key to successful reviews.

Student handout

MOD

Model Analysis: Articulating Achievements

Teacher notes

1. Distribute the model memo to students.
2. Ask students to read the memo quickly to understand its general purpose.
3. Instruct students to complete the MCQ tasks based on the memo.
4. After students have completed the tasks, check answers as a class, discussing why each option is correct or incorrect.
5. Highlight the phrases in the memo that link individual work to broader strategic objectives.
6. Discuss the tone and register of the memo (professional, confident, concise).

Reviewing an Achievement Memo

Read the memo from Alex to their manager, Ms. Davies. This memo is being used to prepare for an upcoming performance review. Then, answer the questions below.

Materials

Memo: Pre-Review Contribution Summary

To: Ms. Davies, Department Head
From: Alex Chen, Logistics Coordinator
Date: October 26, 2023
Subject: Summary of Key Contributions (Q4-Q1)

Dear Ms. Davies,

I hope this memo finds you well. With my annual performance review approaching, I wanted to provide a brief overview of my key contributions during the last fiscal year (Q4-Q1) that I believe align with our departmental objectives and broader corporate strategy.

My primary achievement was spearheading the implementation of the new inventory management system. This initiative directly addressed our objective of enhancing operational efficiency. By redesigning the stock rotation protocol and training five team members on the new software, we successfully reduced average inventory holding costs by 12% and minimized stockouts by 8% over six months. These are significant performance metrics that reflect a direct impact on our departmental budget and contribute to the company’s overall cost-saving initiatives.

Furthermore, I took the lead on a cross-departmental project to optimize our delivery routes, collaborating with the transport team. This project, completed two weeks ahead of schedule, improved delivery times by an average of 15% in our busiest region, thereby directly supporting our strategic goal of improving customer satisfaction and service reliability. This experience has been crucial for my development path in project management and cross-functional collaboration.

Looking ahead, I am eager to explore opportunities for further career progression within the operations team. I believe my skills in process optimization and team training can be leveraged for future initiatives focused on scalability and innovation. My strategic contribution is rooted in identifying and implementing solutions that translate into measurable improvements for the business.

I look forward to discussing these points and my future objectives during our review.

Best regards,
Alex Chen

PREP

Preparation: Language for Contribution

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now prepare to write their own summaries of contributions.
2. Introduce the column banks of useful phrases. Go through each section, explaining the nuance of the phrases.
3. Ask students to think about their own work and try to fit their accomplishments into these categories or use the phrases to describe them.
4. In pairs, students can briefly discuss how they might use these phrases to describe their accomplishments from the L-IN stage.
5. Encourage students to select 3-5 phrases they find most useful for their own context.

FP

Free Practice: Writing Your Contribution Summary

Teacher notes

1. Explain the task: students will now write their own contribution summary.
2. Emphasize the audience (their manager) and purpose (prepare for review, highlight value).
3. Remind students to use the language from the PREP stage and to make sure they link their accomplishments to broader company goals.
4. Set a clear word count (e.g., 150-200 words) and a time limit for writing.
5. Circulate and monitor, offering support with vocabulary or structure as needed.
6. Do not correct language errors at this stage; focus on content and successful communication of the message.

Student handout

Your Performance Review Brief

Now, it's your turn to write a short memo or email to your manager, preparing for your annual performance review. Highlight one or two key accomplishments from the past year.

Audience: Your Manager
Purpose: To summarize your key contributions, show your value, and link your work to your department's and the company's strategic goals.
Approximate Length: 150-200 words (about 10-15 lines)

Include the following:
* An opening stating the purpose of the memo.
* Clearly state your accomplishment(s).
* Use performance metrics (numbers, percentages) where possible to show the impact.
* Explain how your accomplishment links to a departmental or corporate strategic goal.
* Mention any personal development path or career progression you experienced.
* A professional closing.

Use the phrases from the previous activity to help you.

FBDC

Feedback: Content and Language

Teacher notes

1. Pair students up and instruct them to exchange their written memos/emails.
2. Guide students through the content feedback questions first. Emphasize constructive criticism focusing on clarity, impact, and strategic alignment.
3. After content feedback, instruct students to reread their partner's memo, focusing on language accuracy, formality, and appropriate vocabulary (e.g., use of lexical tags).
4. Circulate and provide individualized feedback, correcting key errors and reinforcing positive use of target language. Prioritize errors that hinder meaning or sound unprofessional.
5. Collect the memos for further teacher review if time allows, or for homework assignment of revision.

Student handout

Improving Your Contribution Summary

Now, you will exchange your memo with a partner for feedback. Read your partner's memo carefully and answer the questions below.

Part 1: Content Feedback (Focus on the message)
1. Is the main accomplishment clear and easy to understand?
2. Does the writer use specific details or performance metrics to show the impact of their work?
3. Does the memo clearly explain how the accomplishment links to departmental or corporate strategic goals?
4. Is the tone professional and confident?
5. What is one thing your partner did very well?
6. What is one suggestion you have for improving the clarity of their contribution or its link to strategy?

Part 2: Language Feedback (Focus on words and phrases)
1. Does the memo use appropriate vocabulary related to work, strategy, and performance (e.g., performance metrics, development path, career progression, strategic contribution)?
2. Are there any sentences that are unclear or could be phrased more professionally?
3. Check for any grammar or spelling mistakes.

After getting feedback, revise your memo to make it even stronger.
