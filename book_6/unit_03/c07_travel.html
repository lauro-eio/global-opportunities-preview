Teach: Advanced Participle Clauses Review

Teacher notes

1. Based on errors observed in T1, clarify the use of advanced participle clauses.
2. Focus on: Meaning (synthesizing simultaneous/sequential events, reducing adverbial clauses for efficiency). Form (Present Participle 'V-ing' for simultaneous/ongoing actions, Past Participle 'Having V-ed' for prior actions, 'Not V-ing/Not Having V-ed' for negatives).
3. Emphasize the shared subject rule and the avoidance of dangling modifiers. Ask: 'Does the participle clause share the exact same subject as the main clause? (Yes)'. 'Does this structure reduce an adverbial clause to make written briefs more efficient? (Yes)'.
4. Go through T1 answers, explaining the corrections and the reasons behind them.
5. Distribute the practice task. Students work individually or in pairs.
6. Review answers as a class, reinforcing the rules.

Student handout

Mastering Concise Reports

Participle clauses can make your written reports and briefs more concise and professional, especially when describing sequences of actions or reasons. They reduce adverbial clauses.

Key points:
* Present Participle (V-ing): Describes an action happening at the same time as the main clause, or the result of the main clause action.
Example: Analyzing the data, the team identified key trends.* (The team was analyzing and identified trends simultaneously.)
* Past Participle (Having V-ed): Describes an action that happened before the main clause action.
Example: Having evaluated the border policy, the regional board adjusted migration paths.* (The evaluation happened first, then the adjustment.)
* Negative Form: Use 'Not' before the participle.
Example: Not wanting to breach geopolitical sovereignty, the state halted the rail infrastructure line.* (The state did not want to breach, and so halted.)

Remember: The subject of the participle clause must be the same as the subject of the main clause to avoid dangling modifiers.

T2

Test 2: Drafting a Global Strategy Update

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will apply what they've learned by drafting a short section of a professional document.
2. Present the scenario: a company is expanding its global workforce and needs a strategy update.
3. Instruct students to write a short paragraph (3-5 sentences) outlining communication strategies, ensuring they integrate at least two participle clauses correctly.
4. Monitor students as they write, offering support but encouraging self-correction.
5. After drafting, have students peer-review each other's work, specifically looking for correct participle clause usage.
6. Conclude with a quick check task to assess understanding of the context and structure.
