L-IN

Lead-In: Local Changes and Their Impact

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the unit theme: major urban development and its impact.
2. Elicit initial thoughts on changes happening in the students' own areas.
3. Guide students to discuss the potential effects of new businesses and developments on their workplaces and communities.
4. Encourage them to consider both positive and negative aspects.
5. Circulate and monitor discussions, noting any emerging vocabulary or ideas.

Student handout

Your Neighborhood: What's Changing?

Your local area might be seeing a lot of changes, like new shops, offices, or residential buildings going up. These are often part of larger urban renewal efforts.

Discuss with a partner:

1. What kinds of new developments are happening, or have recently happened, in your neighborhood or around your workplace?
2. How might these changes affect your job or the business you work for?
3. What are some potential benefits of these developments for the local community?
4. What are some potential downsides or challenges?

PTV

Pre-Teach Vocabulary: Urban Development Terminology

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the target vocabulary related to urban development.
2. Have students work individually or in pairs to match the terms to their definitions.
3. Check answers as a class, clarifying any misunderstandings and providing example sentences if necessary.
4. Encourage students to predict how these terms might relate to the upcoming listening.

Student handout

Understanding Key Terms

Before we listen, let's make sure we understand some important terms that might come up. Read the terms and their definitions, then match them correctly.

Materials

Vocabulary
  • zoning laws
  • eminent domain
  • gentrification
  • community displacement
  • urban renewal
  • property rights
Tasks

Match each term to its correct definition.

  • zoning laws — Rules set by local government about how land can be used (e.g., for homes, shops, or factories).
  • eminent domain — The power of the government to take private land for public use, even if the owner doesn't want to sell, usually with compensation.
  • gentrification — The process of renovating and improving a house or district so that it conforms to middle-class taste, often leading to increased property values and the displacement of poorer residents.
  • community displacement — When people are forced to leave their homes or businesses in an area, often due to new development or rising costs.
  • urban renewal — Programs or projects designed to revitalize old or rundown parts of a city, often involving demolition and new construction.
  • property rights — The legal rights that allow individuals or companies to own, use, and transfer land, buildings, or other assets.

First Listen: General Understanding of the Impact

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will listen to a discussion about urban development.
2. Instruct students to listen for the main idea of what Maria and David are concerned about.
3. Play the audio once.
4. Have students complete the multiple-choice question individually.
5. Check the answer as a class, discussing why other options are incorrect.

Student handout

Listening for the Main Point

You will hear Maria, a local business owner, and David, a community organizer, discussing a new urban development project. Listen once to get the general idea. What is their main concern?

Materials

Community Voices: Urban Development Concerns

Maria: "Good evening, everyone. My name is Maria, and I've run 'Maria's Café' for 20 years. We're here tonight because the city's proposed urban renewal project on Elm Street is causing a lot of concern. They're talking about new luxury condos, and while I understand the need for progress, it feels like small businesses like mine are being pushed out. The new zoning laws they're pushing through? They effectively price us out of the area."

David: "That's right, Maria. And it’s not just businesses. My name's David, representing the Elm Street Neighborhood Alliance. This isn't just about gentrification; it's about our community. Many residents have lived here for generations. The city mentioned eminent domain as a last resort, but even the threat of it causes immense stress. Where do we go? The proposed relocation packages are inadequate, leading to widespread community displacement. We're fighting for fair property rights for everyone affected, not just big developers. Our goal is to find a solution that supports existing businesses and residents, not just clears the way for corporate interests."

Maria: "Exactly. We've built our lives here. This isn't just a business; it's a hub for local people. If we're forced to move, the character of this neighborhood will be lost forever."

David: "We need the city to listen. We're proposing alternative plans that integrate new development with respect for the existing fabric of our community. This isn't just about buildings; it's about people."

Tasks

What is the main concern of Maria and David regarding the urban renewal project?

  • They believe the new buildings will be ugly.
  • They are worried about the loss of their community and local businesses.
  • They think the project is moving too slowly.
  • They want the city to build more luxury condos.
DET

Second Listen: Specific Details and Arguments

Teacher notes

1. Instruct students that they will listen again, this time for specific details.
2. Explain the tasks: identify Maria's business and David's organization, and understand the terms used.
3. Play the audio a second time.
4. Have students complete the short-text questions individually.
5. Check answers as a class, prompting students to justify their answers with information from the listening.

FBDC

Follow-Up Discussion and Feedback

Teacher notes

1. Facilitate a class discussion, encouraging students to share their opinions on the situation presented in the listening.
2. Prompt students to use the vocabulary learned in the PTV stage.
3. Ask them to consider what actions they might take if they were in a similar situation.
4. Provide feedback on listening strategies (e.g., listening for keywords, predicting content).
5. Address any persistent vocabulary issues or pronunciation difficulties related to the target lexis.
6. Highlight good use of the target vocabulary from the lesson.

Student handout

Reflecting on Urban Development

Now that you've listened to Maria and David's discussion, let's talk about their situation and the wider impact of urban development.

Discuss with your partner or the class:

1. How do you feel about the city's approach to urban renewal in Maria and David's neighborhood?
2. What are some challenges that businesses like Maria's Café face when zoning laws change or gentrification occurs?
3. If you or your workplace were facing community displacement due to eminent domain, what steps might you take to protect your property rights or advocate for your community?
4. Do you think there's a way to balance urban development with the needs of long-standing communities and businesses?
