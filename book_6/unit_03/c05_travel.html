Lead-In: Addressing Workplace Inappropriacy

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking students to think about the lead-in scenario.
2. Elicit initial thoughts on how one might feel and respond.
3. Guide the discussion towards professional and strategic responses, rather than emotional or confrontational ones.
4. Focus on the importance of maintaining a respectful and inclusive workplace.

Student handout

Speaking Up Professionally

Imagine a scenario at your workplace: a colleague makes a comment about someone's accent or national origin that is clearly inappropriate and makes others uncomfortable.

1. How would you feel hearing such a remark?
2. What are the immediate challenges in deciding how to respond?
3. What kind of impact could such a comment have on workplace morale and productivity?
4. What steps could a professional take to address this, ensuring workplace dignity and inclusion are upheld, without escalating unnecessarily?

T1

Test 1: Diagnosing Strategic Language Gaps in HR Communication

Teacher notes

1. Distribute the internal memo. Tell students it's a draft from HR regarding recent workplace incidents.
2. Explain that the memo aims to communicate a strong, strategic message about inclusion.
3. Students should read the memo and then complete the tasks, choosing the most appropriate and strategic phrasing for each blank.
4. Do not provide answers or correct errors at this stage; this is a diagnostic test to identify existing knowledge and gaps in register and strategic lexis.

Drafting an Inclusive Response

Read the draft internal memo from Human Resources. For each numbered blank, choose the option that best conveys a professional, strategic, and inclusive approach to addressing workplace issues.

Materials

Internal Memo: Reinforcing Workplace Standards

Subject: Reinforcing Our Commitment to an Inclusive Workplace Environment

Dear Team,

Following recent discussions and feedback, we wish to reiterate our steadfast commitment to fostering a truly inclusive workplace. Ensuring a respectful environment where every individual feels valued is paramount to our collective success.

It has come to our attention that certain interactions may not have always upheld these standards. To proactively address this, we propose the following strategic actions:

1. We aim to [ ] on addressing the issue of insensitive comments and discriminatory practices. This involves not only reactive measures but also fostering a culture of proactive cultural sensitivity.

2. We plan to [ ] our inclusion policies, ensuring they are not only comprehensive but also effectively communicated across all departments. This is vital to preventing future incidents and promoting equal opportunity.

3. Our leadership is committed to [ ] to diversity within our organizational structure. This means ensuring that policies against prejudice and discriminatory practices are visible and actively supported.

4. To enhance efficiency in addressing concerns, we will [ ] the process for reporting and resolving grievances. Clear, accessible pathways are essential for building trust.

5. Finally, we will [ ] to support affected personnel and educate staff on workplace inclusion. This includes training programs and dedicated support services.

Your cooperation in upholding these standards is crucial. We value your contributions to a respectful workplace.

Sincerely,

The Human Resources Department

TCH

Teach: Strategic Mobility in Professional Communication

Teacher notes

1. Begin by reviewing the answers to T1, explaining why the correct options are more appropriate and strategic.
2. Write the target exponents on the board.
3. Meaning: Elicit or explain the precise meaning of each phrase in this professional, strategic context. Emphasize that these phrases convey deliberate planning, efficiency, and influence.
4. Register: Discuss the formal, professional register of these phrases, contrasting them with the more informal options students saw in T1. Explain why this high register is crucial for workplace dignity and official communication (e.g., HR, policy, management).
5. Pragmatics: Discuss the pragmatic effect – how these phrases project conviction, clarity, and agency. They signal a serious, well-considered approach.
6. Phonology: Model the phrases, focusing on placing stress on content words that highlight strategic importance and agency (e.g., gain traction, broaden scope, visible commitment, streamline process, allocate resources). Practice a falling intonation to convey conviction and clarity in statements. Drill individual words and then the full phrases.
7. Use CCQs to check understanding of meaning and appropriacy (e.g., 'Would you use 'gain traction' when talking to your friends about a hobby?', 'What's the difference in impact between 'deal with the issue' and 'gain traction on addressing the issue'?').
8. Lead into the controlled practice task, encouraging students to apply their understanding.

Mastering Formal Responses

These phrases are used in professional settings to discuss strategic actions, showing purposeful planning and a high level of commitment. They help to convey conviction and clarity in important communications.

Now, let's look at more precise and impactful ways to phrase your responses to workplace challenges.

Materials

Vocabulary
  • gain traction on addressing the issue
  • broaden the scope of our inclusion policies
  • ensure a visible commitment to diversity
  • streamline the grievance resolution process
  • allocate resources to support affected personnel
Tasks

Match the informal workplace phrase with its more strategic and formal equivalent.

Connect informal language with more formal, strategic phrasing.

  • Get things moving on the problem. — Gain traction on addressing the issue.
  • Make our policies bigger. — Broaden the scope of our inclusion policies.
  • Show everyone we care about diversity. — Ensure a visible commitment to diversity.
  • Make the complaint process quicker. — Streamline the grievance resolution process.
  • Give money/help to people who need it. — Allocate resources to support affected personnel.

Rewrite the following informal statement into a more strategic and formal sentence, using one of the target phrases.

Informal: 'We really need to get better at handling complaints quickly.' Formal: 'It is imperative that we _______________________.'

Rewrite the following informal statement into a more strategic and formal sentence, using one of the target phrases.

Informal: 'Management has to be seen to be serious about diversity.' Formal: 'Leadership must _______________________.'

T2

Test 2: Strategic Dialogue Simulation

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs or small groups.
2. Present the role-play scenario and task instructions.
3. Emphasize that students should aim to use at least 2-3 of the target strategic phrases naturally within their discussion or proposal.
4. Monitor carefully for accurate use of the target lexis, appropriate register, and confident intonation.
5. Provide feedback on language use, focusing on corrections and highlighting effective examples.
6. Model Response Key Points:
* Start by acknowledging the issue professionally.
* Propose concrete actions using the target phrases (e.g., 'I believe we need to gain traction on addressing this issue by initiating a series of facilitated discussions.' 'It's also crucial that we ensure a visible commitment to diversity from senior leadership.' 'Perhaps we could streamline the grievance resolution process to make it less intimidating for staff.').
* Maintain a respectful, solution-oriented tone.
* Stress content words and use falling intonation for conviction.

Student handout

Proposing Solutions

You are a team leader in a department where recent inappropriate comments about national origin have created tension. Your manager has asked you to prepare a brief proposal (3-5 sentences) to present to the HR department and senior leadership, outlining strategic actions to restore a positive and inclusive work environment.

Working with a partner, discuss and draft your proposal. Make sure to:

* Identify the core problems clearly.
* Propose concrete, strategic actions.
* Use at least two to three of the strategic phrases you learned in the previous stage.
* Maintain a professional and solution-oriented tone.
* Practice delivering your proposal with conviction, paying attention to stress and intonation.
