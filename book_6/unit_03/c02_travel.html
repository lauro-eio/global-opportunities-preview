L-IN

Lead-In: Global Trade & Logistics

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and briefly introduce the topic of international trade and logistics.
2. Pose the lead-in question from the brief: 'Think about the last time you ordered something from another country. What are some potential hidden costs or delays that businesses face when shipping goods across borders?'
3. Facilitate a short whole-class or small-group discussion. Encourage students to share personal experiences or insights related to international shipments, customs, and unexpected challenges.
4. Elicit examples of hidden costs (tariffs, warehousing, re-routing fees) and delays (customs hold, paperwork issues, inspections). Connect these to the broader concept of strategic planning in logistics.

GIST

Gist Reading: Navigating Customs Complexity

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the reading task. Tell students they will read an internal company memo/report about recent challenges in international shipping.
2. Instruct students to read the passage quickly for general understanding, focusing on the main purpose of the report and the core issues discussed.
3. Explain the MCQ task and ensure they understand they need to choose the best option that summarizes the report's main point.
4. Set a time limit for reading and completing the task (e.g., 3-4 minutes).
5. Collect answers and briefly discuss the correct gist, clarifying any immediate misunderstandings without going into detail.

Materials

Internal Memo: Strategic Logistical Review - Q3

To: Regional Operations Leads
From: Eleanor Vance, Head of Global Logistics
Date: October 26, 2023
Subject: Navigating Recent Shifts in International Freight & Customs Policies

Having evaluated the recent bilateral trade agreements and updated customs declaration protocols, this memo outlines the significant operational challenges we’ve encountered during Q3. The primary concern remains the unprecedented delays affecting our crucial transit routes, particularly those impacting the East Asian corridor.

Not wanting to breach geopolitical sovereignty, several bonded warehouse facilities along key border hubs have initiated stricter inspection regimes, leading to considerable backlogs. This has directly impacted our ability to optimize logistical movements and meet delivery deadlines. Freight cargo manifests are undergoing more granular scrutiny, increasing processing times by an average of 48 hours per consignment.

Furthermore, revised import tariffs in several emerging markets have escalated landed costs, significantly eroding our profit margins. Our initial projections, having considered the standard customs clearance procedures, did not fully account for these rapid policy shifts. This necessitates a proactive adjustment to our supply chain strategy.

To counter these pressures, we must urgently secure a strategic presence in alternative regional hubs. We are currently exploring partnerships that would allow us to expand our operational reach without incurring prohibitive re-routing expenses. Having identified potential bottlenecks early, our task force has begun drafting contingency plans to circumvent these challenges, focusing on diversification of carriers and port entries. Our goal is to deploy resources effectively to gain a foothold in new markets while maintaining service integrity and compliance with new regulations regarding prohibited items. We aim to finalize these adjustments by the end of Q4.

Tasks

What is the main purpose of this memo?

The memo primarily serves to:

  • Announce the successful opening of new bonded warehouse facilities.
  • Propose an increase in global shipping tariffs for next quarter.
  • Detail challenges caused by new trade and customs policies and suggest strategic responses.
  • Commend regional operations leads for meeting Q3 delivery deadlines.
DET

Detailed Reading: Policy Impacts & Strategic Responses

Teacher notes

1. Remind students they will reread the same memo, this time focusing on specific details.
2. Explain that the tasks require them to identify particular problems and proposed solutions mentioned in the text.
3. Instruct students to work individually or in pairs to complete the short-text questions. Encourage them to highlight or underline relevant parts of the text.
4. Monitor students as they work, providing support with vocabulary or comprehension as needed without giving direct answers.
5. After students have completed the tasks, check answers as a class. Facilitate discussion on why certain answers are correct and where the information can be found in the text.

GD

Guided Discovery: Advanced Participle Clauses & Strategic Mobility

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the language analysis stage. Inform students that they will look closely at how certain phrases in the memo make the communication more efficient and professional.
2. Participle Clauses: Draw students' attention to the phrases: 'Having evaluated...', 'Not wanting to breach...', 'Having considered...', 'Having identified...'.
a. Ask students to identify these phrases in the text. (Concept Check: Does this structure reduce an adverbial clause to make written briefs more efficient? (Yes) Does the participle clause share the exact same subject as the main clause? (Yes))
b. Ask them what meaning these clauses convey (reason, sequence, cause, condition). For example, 'Having evaluated...' means 'Because we evaluated' or 'After we evaluated'. 'Not wanting to breach...' means 'Because we did not want to breach'.
c. Discuss how these structures make the writing more concise and formal, suitable for professional reports.
3. Strategic Mobility Lexis: Guide students to re-examine the words highlighted with double asterisks.
a. Ask students to identify phrases related to strategic movement or resource allocation (e.g., 'optimize logistical movements', 'secure a strategic presence', 'expand our operational reach', 'deploy resources effectively', 'gain a foothold in new markets').
b. Discuss the nuance of 'strategic' in these phrases – it's not just moving, but moving with a purpose, influence, and planning.
c. For phonology, model the phrases, emphasizing content words and using a falling intonation to convey conviction and clarity. Have students repeat. (e.g., 'OPTIMIZE logistical MOVEMENTS', 'secure a STRATEGIC PRESENCE').
4. Use the matching task to consolidate understanding of the functions of participle clauses.

Student handout

Analyzing Language: Efficiency & Influence

The memo uses advanced structures and specific vocabulary to convey information efficiently and professionally. Let's look at two key areas.

Part 1: Advanced Participle Clauses
Locate the following phrases in the memo. They often appear at the beginning of a sentence:
1. "Having evaluated..."
2. "Not wanting to breach..."
3. "Having considered..."
4. "Having identified..."

Discuss:
What does each of these phrases tell you about the main action in the sentence? (e.g., 'Having evaluated...' tells you why the memo outlines challenges, or after* evaluation.)
* How do these structures make the report sound more concise and formal compared to using 'Because we evaluated' or 'After we evaluated'?
* Does the action in the participle clause (e.g., 'evaluating') happen at the same time or before the main clause's action? (Before or simultaneously, usually before for 'having' clauses).

Match the participle clause examples to their function in the memo.

Part 2: Strategic Mobility in Professional Contexts
Find these phrases in the memo. They are often marked with double asterisks:
* optimize logistical movements
* secure a strategic presence
* expand our operational reach
* deploy resources effectively
* gain a foothold in new markets

Discuss:
* What do these phrases mean in the context of international shipping and business strategy? How do they differ from simply saying 'move goods' or 'be present'?
* Listen to your teacher and repeat these phrases, paying attention to which words are stressed and how your voice falls at the end of the phrase. This conveys conviction and clear planning.

  • Having identified bottlenecks early... — Highlights a completed analysis that informs subsequent planning.
CP

Controlled Practice: Crafting Concise Policy Statements

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now practice using advanced participle clauses to make sentences more formal and concise, mirroring the style of the memo.
2. Go through the first example together to ensure students understand the transformation from an adverbial clause to a participle clause.
3. Instruct students to rewrite the given sentences using either a present or past participle clause. Remind them to ensure the participle clause shares the same subject as the main clause to avoid dangling modifiers.
4. Monitor carefully, paying attention to potential errors like dangling modifiers or incorrect participle forms ('Arrived at the border hub...' vs. 'Having arrived...'). Provide immediate feedback and correction.
5. Check answers as a class, writing correct versions on the board if necessary and discussing why each change improves the sentence's professionalism and efficiency.

FP

Freer Practice: Discussing Future Logistics Strategy

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the freer practice activity. Tell students they will now apply the language they've learned to a hypothetical business scenario.
2. Divide students into small groups (3-4). Explain that they are a logistics advisory team tasked with proposing solutions for future international shipping challenges.
3. Present the discussion prompts. Encourage them to use participle clauses to justify decisions and the strategic mobility vocabulary to articulate their plans.
4. Remind them to pay attention to intonation and stress when using the strategic mobility phrases.
5. Monitor group discussions, noting effective use of target language and providing prompts or corrections as needed. Avoid interrupting fluency unless absolutely necessary.
6. After 8-10 minutes, bring the class back together and ask each group to share one or two key proposals or observations. Facilitate a brief whole-class discussion, encouraging peer feedback.

Sample Answer Considerations:
* Participle Clauses: 'Having analyzed the recent geopolitical shifts, we recommend...' 'Not wanting to risk further delays, we are diversifying our port entry points.'
* Strategic Mobility: 'Our goal is to gain a foothold in new markets in Southeast Asia.' 'We must deploy resources effectively to optimize logistical movements across the continent.' 'We are looking to secure a strategic presence in key trade corridors.' 'This will help us expand our operational reach and mitigate risk.'

Planning for Tomorrow: Your Company's Next Move

Imagine you are a logistics advisory team for a company that imports and exports goods globally. You've just read a report similar to the memo we discussed, highlighting new challenges in international shipping and customs.

Work in small groups to discuss and propose solutions for the next quarter. Try to use:

* Participle clauses (e.g., 'Having evaluated...', 'Not wanting to...') to explain your analysis and decisions.
* Vocabulary for strategic mobility (e.g., 'gain a foothold', 'expand our operational reach', 'optimize logistical movements', 'deploy resources effectively').

Consider these questions:
1. What are the biggest risks your company faces in international shipping next year?
2. What strategic moves can you make to mitigate these risks?
3. Where should you prioritize deploying resources?
4. How will you expand your operational reach or secure a new presence in a challenging market?
