Mastering Emphasis in Financial Reporting

In advanced communication, especially in finance and business, we often need to make one piece of information stand out. This is crucial when presenting critical data or findings to stakeholders. We use 'cleft sentences' to achieve this specific emphasis.

Meaning: Cleft sentences allow us to isolate and highlight a particular part of a sentence – be it a cause, an effect, or a key factor – making it clearer and more impactful. This is highly valuable in performance reviews or strategic discussions to draw attention to specific financial data points or liabilities.

Form 1: Emphasizing the Subject or Object ('What... is/was...')
Use 'What + clause + is/was + emphasized element' to focus on a particular action or thing. This form emphasizes a single part of the sentence.
* Example: What shocked the board was the scale of the liability scaling.
* Concept Check: Are we emphasizing a single part of the sentence with 'What... was...'? (Yes)

Form 2: Emphasizing a Clause or Fact ('It is/was... that...')
Use 'It is/was + emphasized element + that + clause' to highlight a specific fact, cause, or consequence. This also helps make the financial cause clearer to stakeholders.
* Example: It was the expenditure inflation that triggered the audit.
* Concept Check: Does this structure make the financial cause clearer to stakeholders? (Yes)

Let's practice using these forms to create clearer, more impactful financial statements.
