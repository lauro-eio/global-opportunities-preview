L-IN

Lead-In: Inflation's Personal Impact

Teacher notes

1. Begin by eliciting personal experiences with recent price increases.
2. Encourage students to share specific examples from their household budgets.
3. Facilitate a brief group discussion on common coping strategies.
4. Link the discussion to the broader concept of cost of living and purchasing power.

Student handout

How Rising Prices Affect Us

Think about your household budget in the last year.
1. How have recent price increases affected your daily expenses and your ability to save? Give specific examples.
2. What are some common reactions or strategies people adopt when the cost of living rises?
3. Have you or anyone you know had to adjust their spending habits significantly? How?

Workplace Talk: Inflation Challenges

Read the conversation between two colleagues, Maria (HR Manager) and Ben (Operations Supervisor). They are discussing the impact of current inflation rates on their company and employees.

Materials

Inflation's Ripple Effect

Maria: Good morning, Ben. I wanted to catch up on the latest economic outlook and its impact on our team. The latest consumer price index figures are quite concerning.
Ben: Good morning, Maria. Absolutely. Morale is certainly a topic of discussion among the staff. They're feeling the pinch, no doubt. Their purchasing power has definitely been eroded.
Maria: Exactly. We're seeing a clear effect on their household budgets. From an HR perspective, it’s leading to increased pressure for wage negotiation during reviews. Employees are struggling to keep up with the cost of living.
Ben: And from Operations, we're seeing our own set of challenges. Our raw material costs have skyrocketed, and shipping expenses are up too. We're having to justify price increases to clients, which isn't easy.
Maria: So, it's a double-edged sword. Employees need more to maintain their living standards, and the company's operational costs are also rising. How are your team leaders addressing these concerns?
Ben: We're encouraging open communication, assuring them we understand their situation. But without a significant increase in our revenue, it's a tough balancing act. We need to find ways to maintain productivity without overburdening the staff further, while also managing our expenses.
Maria: Indeed. We're exploring options, maybe some non-monetary benefits or support programs. It's crucial that we articulate these challenges transparently to the wider management team and find sustainable solutions.

PREP

Preparation: Gathering Your Thoughts

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will prepare for a discussion on inflation's impact.
2. Direct students to the column banks and encourage them to familiarize themselves with the phrases.
3. Give students a few minutes to silently brainstorm their own ideas and how they might use these phrases.
4. Encourage them to think about both employee and business perspectives, similar to the model dialogue.

FP

Freer Practice: Inflation Impact Discussion

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into small groups (3-4).
2. Assign roles if desired (e.g., HR rep, Operations Manager, Employee rep, Finance rep) or allow open discussion.
3. Remind students to use the language from the preparation stage.
4. Monitor groups, noting down examples of good language use and areas for delayed correction.
5. Circulate and provide minimal error correction, focusing on encouraging fluency.

Student handout

Discussing Solutions to Inflation

Imagine you are members of a management team or employee representatives in a company. Your task is to discuss the current inflation rates and their specific effects on both employee well-being and the company's operational costs.

Discuss the following points:
1. What are the most pressing concerns for employees right now due to the cost of living increases?
2. How is the current economic situation impacting the company's budget, material costs, and pricing strategies?
3. Propose and discuss potential solutions or strategies the company could implement to mitigate these impacts, considering both employee support and business sustainability.

Aim for a professional and constructive discussion.

FBDC

Feedback and Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Begin with content feedback: Ask each group to briefly summarize their proposed solutions or key discussion points.
2. Facilitate a short class discussion using the student reflection questions.
3. Transition to delayed language correction:
a. Write target language errors (anonymously) observed during the FP stage on the board.
b. Elicit corrections from students.
c. Highlight any particularly good examples of language use or accurate use of target vocabulary.
4. Reinforce correct usage of vocabulary related to finance and inflation.

Student handout

Reflecting on Our Discussion

Think about the discussion you just had.
1. What were the most challenging aspects of discussing inflation's impact on your hypothetical company?
2. What new insights did you gain from your group members' perspectives?
3. Which of the phrases from the preparation stage did you find most useful, or which new phrases did you learn from your peers?
4. What specific solutions did your group propose, and do you think they are realistic for a company in the current economic outlook?
