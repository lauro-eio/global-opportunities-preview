L-IN

Lead-In: Workplace Green Initiatives

Teacher notes

1. Write the lesson title on the board.
2. Ask students to read the lead-in question: 'Imagine your company wants to cut operational costs and improve its environmental record. What practical green initiatives would you propose for your workplace?'
3. Divide students into small groups (2-3).
4. Give groups 5 minutes to brainstorm and discuss their ideas.
5. Circulate and monitor, encouraging them to think of specific, actionable steps.
6. Bring the class back together and ask each group to share one or two of their best ideas with the whole class.

Student handout

2. Who would be involved in these initiatives?
3. What benefits would these changes bring?

Materials

Vocabulary
  • waste reduction
  • energy efficiency
  • carbon footprint
  • corporate responsibility
  • sustainable practices
Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • waste reduction — Minimizing the amount of trash or unwanted materials produced.
  • energy efficiency — Using less energy to perform the same task or provide the same service.
  • carbon footprint — The total amount of greenhouse gases (like CO2) caused by an organization, event, or product.
  • corporate responsibility — A business's duty to act ethically and contribute to economic development while improving the quality of life for its workforce, families, and society.
  • sustainable practices — Methods or actions that meet the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
GIST

Gist Reading: Sustainable Solutions Memo

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will read a memo about green initiatives.
2. Tell them to read the memo quickly to understand the main idea, not to focus on every detail.
3. Set a strict time limit (e.g., 2 minutes) for skimming.
4. Instruct students to complete the multiple-choice question after reading.
5. Check the answer as a class, encouraging students to explain why they chose their option.

Student handout

After reading, choose the best option to answer the question about the memo's primary purpose.

Materials

Update: Our Commitment to a Greener Workplace

Subject: Update: Our Commitment to a Greener Workplace
Date: October 26, 2023
From: The Green Initiatives Task Force

Dear Team,

As part of our ongoing commitment to corporate responsibility and improving our environmental record, the Green Initiatives Task Force is pleased to share an update on our progress and introduce some new proposals. Our goal is to implement practical, impactful changes that not only benefit the planet but also enhance our operational efficiency.

Over the past quarter, we have seen significant success in our waste reduction efforts. Thanks to everyone's participation in our new recycling program, we’ve cut down on landfill waste by 15%. This initiative proves that small, collective actions can make a big difference.

Looking ahead, we are focusing on two key areas: energy efficiency and reducing our overall carbon footprint. We are currently evaluating several proposals for upgraded LED lighting in all offices and warehouses, which promises substantial energy savings. Additionally, we are exploring options for carpooling programs and encouraging remote work where possible to minimize travel emissions. These steps are crucial for lowering our environmental impact.

We believe that by adopting more sustainable practices across all departments, we can set a new standard for our industry. This isn't just about compliance; it's about building a healthier, more resource-conscious future for our company and community.

We invite all employees to share their ideas and feedback on these initiatives. Your input is invaluable as we continue to shape a greener, more sustainable workplace. Please look out for upcoming workshops and discussion forums where you can learn more and contribute.

Thank you for your continued dedication.

Sincerely,
The Green Initiatives Task Force

Tasks

What is the primary purpose of this memo?

  • To announce new mandatory environmental regulations for employees.
  • To update employees on current green initiatives and propose future plans.
  • To request employee donations for various environmental charities.
  • To criticize employees for not participating enough in recycling programs.
DET

Detailed Reading: Analyzing Green Proposals

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now read the same memo again, but this time for specific details.
2. Instruct them to read the memo more carefully to find the answers to the questions.
3. Give students 5-7 minutes to read and answer the short_text questions.
4. Encourage them to highlight or underline the information in the text that helps them answer.
5. Have students compare their answers in pairs.
6. Go through the answers as a class, asking students to point to where they found the information in the text.

FBDC

Follow-up Discussion: Implementing Initiatives

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking students if they found the memo easy or difficult to understand, and why.
2. Review any vocabulary or phrases that students still have questions about, relating them back to the context.
3. Transition to the discussion questions.
4. Divide students into groups or facilitate a whole-class discussion.
5. Encourage students to connect the ideas from the memo to their own workplaces and the initial lead-in discussion.
6. Monitor discussions, prompting deeper engagement and encouraging all students to participate.
