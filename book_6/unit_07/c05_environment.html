Your Community's Voice

Imagine a new development project is planned in your local area. While it promises jobs, many residents are worried about its impact on the environment – perhaps increased traffic, air quality, or local green spaces.

As a representative from your community, you need to speak to the project developers. How would you professionally articulate these community worries to ensure they are taken seriously?

Reviewing Developer Communications

Materials

Memo: Initial Community Feedback – Green Valley Development

TO: Project Management Team
FROM: Maria Sanchez, Community Liaison Officer
DATE: [Current Date]
SUBJECT: Initial Community Feedback – Green Valley Development

Team,

This memo summarizes the preliminary feedback gathered from local residents regarding the proposed Green Valley development. Overall, there's a recognized need for economic growth in the area, and the potential for new jobs has been noted positively.

However, several residents have raised points regarding the project's environmental considerations. There's a general wish that we should 'do more to protect the local environment' (1) and 'not cause too much pollution' (2). Some specific mentions included the future of a small wetland area and the local air quality near proposed construction sites.

Residents also mentioned wanting us to 'use less electricity and water' (3) during and after construction. A few individuals also spoke about the need for us to 'make sure we aren’t creating a huge carbon problem' (4) with the increased vehicle traffic during the construction phase.

I believe we need to respond to these points with clear, professional language that shows we are taking their concerns seriously and have robust plans in place. Please review this feedback, and we can discuss a strategy for addressing these points in our next community outreach meeting.

Tasks

Which is the most professional and impactful way to rephrase 'do more to protect the local environment' (1)?

Choose the best option:

  • get serious about being ecological
  • implement sustainable practices
  • think about green stuff

Which is the most professional and impactful way to rephrase 'not cause too much pollution' (2)?

Choose the best option:

  • avoid messing up the environment too much
  • keep pollution levels down
  • mitigate environmental impact

Which is the most professional and impactful way to rephrase 'use less electricity and water' (3)?

Choose the best option:

  • be careful with resources
  • resource efficiency measures
  • cut back on how much we use

Which is the most professional and impactful way to rephrase 'make sure we aren’t creating a huge carbon problem' (4)?

Choose the best option:

  • reduce our overall carbon emissions
  • carbon footprint reduction initiatives
  • try to be carbon neutral
TCH

Teach: Professional Discourse on Sustainable Initiatives

Teacher notes

1. Start by reviewing the answers from T1, eliciting 'Why?' to gauge student understanding of appropriacy.
2. Present the target exponents using the provided explanations, linking them to the meaning from the 'compiled_linguistic_targets'.
3. Discuss the register: explain why these phrases are more professional, clear, and impactful than the informal alternatives.
4. Focus on phonology: Model each phrase, highlighting the primary stress on content words and demonstrating a confident falling intonation. Have students repeat chorally and individually.
5. Use CCQs (Concept Checking Questions) to ensure understanding of meaning and appropriacy: 'Can you use 'implement sustainable practices' when chatting with a friend about gardening?' (No). 'Does 'mitigate' mean to completely stop, or to reduce?' (Reduce).
6. Give students the 'match_pairs' task to reinforce the connection between informal concerns and professional language.
7. Monitor and provide individual feedback on pronunciation and usage.

Student handout

Mastering Impactful Language

In professional settings, using precise and impactful language helps you communicate effectively and ensures your message is taken seriously. Let's look at some key phrases for discussing environmental issues professionally.

Understanding the Phrases:
These phrases help articulate concerns and solutions clearly and authoritatively.

1. implement sustainable practices: To put into action methods that protect the environment and can continue for a long time without depleting resources.
2. mitigate environmental impact: To reduce the harmful effects a project or activity has on the natural world.
3. resource efficiency measures: Actions taken to use natural resources (like water, energy, materials) more effectively and with less waste.
4. carbon footprint reduction initiatives: Plans or actions aimed at lowering the total amount of greenhouse gases released by an organization or activity.

Practice:
Match the general concern on the left to the more professional and impactful statement on the right.

Now, think about the stress and intonation when you say these phrases.
* 'implement sustainable practices'
* 'mitigate environmental impact'
* 'resource efficiency measures'
* 'carbon footprint reduction initiatives'

Notice how the main stress often falls on the content words (nouns and verbs) that convey the core meaning. When making statements, use a professional falling intonation at the end to project confidence and authority.

T2

Test 2: Professional Proposal

Teacher notes

1. Explain the T2 scenario: students are now actively proposing solutions to developers.
2. Divide students into pairs or small groups.
3. Instruct them to prepare a short presentation (3-5 minutes) following the outlined points, ensuring they use the target professional language.
4. Circulate, providing support, checking for accurate usage of the target exponents, and offering pronunciation feedback.
5. Emphasize the importance of professional tone, confident delivery, and appropriate register.
6. Have pairs/groups deliver their presentations to another pair/group or to the whole class.
7. After each presentation, facilitate a brief Q&A session where 'developers' can ask questions and 'community representatives' can respond, further practicing the language in a semi-authentic context.
8. Provide positive feedback on language use and delivery.

Student handout

Presenting Your Solutions

You are now a key member of a community action group. You have been invited to a meeting with the developers of the Green Valley project to present your community's concerns and propose solutions in a professional and constructive manner.

Prepare a short presentation (3-5 minutes) where you will:
1. Acknowledge the positive aspects of the project (e.g., jobs).
2. Clearly articulate 2-3 specific environmental concerns using professional language.
3. Propose specific actions or commitments the developers could make, using phrases like:
* 'We urge you to implement sustainable practices...'
* 'It's crucial to mitigate environmental impact by...'
* 'We recommend exploring resource efficiency measures such as...'
* 'Consider initiating carbon footprint reduction initiatives through...'

Work with a partner to outline your presentation points, then practice delivering your presentation. Be prepared to answer questions professionally.
