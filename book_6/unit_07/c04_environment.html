L-IN

Lead-In: Addressing Ethical Sourcing Concerns

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and briefly introduce the broader topic of ethical business practices.
2. Present the scenario: 'Imagine your company is facing public scrutiny for its material sourcing practices in a region known for environmental exploitation and poor working conditions.'
3. Elicit initial reactions and discuss the seriousness of such a situation for a company and its stakeholders.
4. Facilitate discussion using the prompts in 'Student Content', ensuring students use professional language.

Student handout

Company Under Scrutiny

Imagine your company is facing serious public scrutiny. Reports are emerging about your material sourcing practices in a region known for environmental exploitation and poor working conditions. You need to address internal and external stakeholders.

Discuss with a partner:
1. What are the immediate concerns for the company in this situation?
2. Who are the key stakeholders (e.g., employees, customers, investors, local communities) and what might their expectations be?
3. How might such scrutiny impact the company's reputation and financial stability?
4. What would be your initial steps to investigate and respond to these allegations?

Pre-Teach Vocabulary: Key Terms for Ethical Supply Chains

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the purpose of this stage: to clarify key terms essential for understanding the upcoming listening task.
2. Go through each vocabulary item, eliciting or explaining meanings, providing examples, and checking pronunciation.
3. Ask students to complete the matching task individually or in pairs.
4. Review answers as a class, clarifying any misunderstandings and ensuring accurate usage in a business context.

Student handout

Understanding Key Terms

Before we listen, let's make sure we understand some important terms related to our topic. Use the definitions provided to match each term correctly.

Materials

Vocabulary
  • ethical sourcing
  • supply chain transparency
  • fair trade principles
  • resource governance
  • corporate ethics
Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • ethical sourcing — Obtaining materials and products in a way that is morally acceptable, without harming people or the environment.
  • supply chain transparency — The ability to track and verify the origins and journey of materials and products through every stage of production.
  • fair trade principles — Standards designed to ensure producers in developing countries receive fair prices for their products and operate under decent working conditions.
  • resource governance — The rules, institutions, and practices that determine how natural resources are managed, allocated, and used.
  • corporate ethics — The moral principles and standards that guide a company's behavior and decision-making, particularly concerning social and environmental responsibilities.
GIST

Gist Listening: Identifying Core Issues

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will listen to a segment of a company meeting discussing the recent scrutiny related to sourcing.
2. Tell students their first task is to listen for the main idea or overall purpose of the discussion, not every detail.
3. Play the audio once.
4. Ask students to answer the multiple-choice question individually.
5. Review the answer as a class, checking general comprehension and briefly discussing why the other options are incorrect.

Student handout

First Listen: What's the Main Point?

You will hear a recording of an urgent company meeting. Listen once to get a general understanding of the discussion. Focus on the overall purpose of the meeting and the main issues being addressed. Then, choose the best option below.

Materials

Urgent Meeting: Sourcing Strategy Review

Sarah: Good morning, team. As you know, we're here to address the escalating concerns regarding our supply chain in Region X. The media reports detailing environmental damage and poor labor practices are damaging. We've always prided ourselves on our corporate ethics, but these allegations are hitting hard. David, can you summarize the legal implications for us?

David: Certainly, Sarah. From a legal standpoint, we're looking at potential breaches of international labor standards and environmental regulations, even if indirectly. Our immediate priority is to demonstrate robust supply chain transparency. We need verifiable data, not just promises. The public and our investors expect a clear, actionable response.

Maria: And from a sustainability perspective, this is critical. We've been making strides towards ethical sourcing, particularly with our rare earth minerals, but clearly, it's not enough. The local communities in Region X are suffering, and we need to re-evaluate our resource governance strategies entirely. Are we sure our local partners are adhering to the fair trade principles we champion?

Sarah: That’s precisely the point, Maria. The current audit reports aren't sufficient. We need to go beyond basic compliance. David, what’s the legal risk if we fail to act decisively?

David: Significant, Sarah. Beyond fines, there's the long-term impact on brand value and consumer trust. We could face boycotts, investor divestment, and a major talent drain. This isn't just a PR problem; it's a fundamental challenge to our operational integrity.

Maria: We need to send a team to Region X immediately to conduct a deep dive. Not just an audit, but a collaborative assessment with local stakeholders. We need to understand the root causes and implement corrective measures that are sustainable. This is about building trust, not just checking boxes.

Sarah: I agree. We need to communicate our commitment clearly to all stakeholders. We'll outline our strategy for enhanced supply chain transparency and our renewed focus on genuinely ethical sourcing. We also need to develop a clearer framework for resource governance in all high-risk regions. The objective is not just to mitigate damage but to emerge from this stronger, with a truly resilient and responsible supply chain. This is a chance to redefine our corporate ethics and set a new standard. We have to move quickly.

Tasks

Listen to the company meeting. What is the main purpose of this discussion?

The main purpose of the meeting is to:

  • Discuss new marketing strategies for products from Region X.
  • Address allegations of unethical sourcing practices and plan a response.
  • Evaluate the company's quarterly financial performance.
  • Introduce new team members for the procurement department.
DET

Detailed Listening: Specific Concerns and Actions

Teacher notes

1. Tell students they will listen to the same audio again, this time focusing on specific details, problems, and proposed solutions.
2. Explain the task: complete the short_text tasks individually.
3. Play the audio again.
4. Review answers for short_text tasks as a class.
5. For the open inference question in 'Student Content', facilitate a class discussion, noting common themes and diverse opinions. There isn't one 'right' answer but rather a range of ethical considerations. Key points for discussion include: 'demonstrate robust supply chain transparency', 're-evaluate resource governance strategies', 'send a team to Region X immediately to conduct a deep dive', 'communicate our commitment clearly to all stakeholders', impact on 'corporate ethics' and 'brand value'.

FBDC

Follow-Up Discussion & Feedback

Teacher notes

1. Initiate a discussion based on the open inference question from the DET stage, allowing students to share their interpretations of the ethical dilemmas and speaker priorities.
2. Broaden the discussion by asking: 'What actions do you think are most effective for companies facing such ethical challenges?'
3. Provide feedback on listening comprehension strategies, acknowledging what students found challenging or easy.
4. Briefly review any problematic vocabulary or phrases that emerged during the discussion, particularly those related to ethical sourcing, supply chain transparency, and corporate ethics.
5. Conclude by linking back to the importance of proactive and responsible business practices in today's global economy.

Student handout

Reflecting on Responsibility

Now that we've analyzed the company's situation, let's discuss:
1. If you were a stakeholder, which speaker's argument (Sarah, David, or Maria) would you find most convincing, and why?
2. What are the long-term benefits for a company that genuinely commits to strong ethical sourcing and supply chain transparency?
3. What challenges might a company face when trying to implement effective resource governance in complex international supply chains?
4. How important do you think corporate ethics are to customers, employees, and investors today?
