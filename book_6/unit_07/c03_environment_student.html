Workplace Weather Watch

Considering recent shifts in global weather patterns and the increase in extreme events like floods, heatwaves, or storms, how might these events directly affect your daily work or the long-term stability of your profession?

Discuss in pairs or small groups:
1. Have you observed any immediate impacts of climate events on your workplace or industry?
2. What are some potential future challenges your job or industry might face due to climate change?
3. How might these changes impact your job security or required skills?

Talking About Climate at Work

Read the conversation between Maria, a logistics manager, and Ben, a factory floor supervisor, discussing how climate change is affecting their work and what their companies are doing.

Maria: Morning, Ben. Rough week, huh? Another supply chain disruption thanks to those floods up north. My deliveries are all over the place.

Ben: Tell me about it, Maria. We had a power outage at the plant yesterday – extreme heat put too much strain on the grid. Production was down for hours. It really hits our targets, and the team worries about job security if this keeps happening.

Maria: Exactly! Our company's trying to improve our climate resilience. We’re investing in more diverse routes and storage facilities away from flood plains. It’s expensive, but essential for disaster preparedness.

Ben: Sounds like a smart move. We're looking at workforce adaptation programs. Training staff on new, more energy-efficient machinery and cross-training them for different roles so we can be more flexible during disruptions. It's a big shift, but vital.

Maria: Absolutely. It's not just about infrastructure; it's about the people too. We've even started discussions on local sourcing to shorten our supply chains, making us less vulnerable to global weather events.

Ben: That's a good approach. We're exploring better insulation and backup power solutions. The challenge is getting everyone on board, from management to the shop floor, to truly embrace these changes. It's about securing our future, though.

Maria: Couldn't agree more. It’s a collective effort to build that climate resilience.

Our Climate Challenge Meeting

Imagine you are colleagues from different departments or companies within the same industry (e.g., logistics, manufacturing, construction, hospitality). You are meeting to discuss the growing challenges posed by climate change and to brainstorm practical adaptation strategies for your sector.

Your Task:
1. Share specific examples of how climate change is currently affecting, or could affect, your area of work.
2. Discuss the impact on daily operations, employee roles, and overall business stability.
3. Propose and discuss potential solutions and adaptation strategies for your industry.
4. Aim to use the vocabulary and phrases from the preparation stage.

Discussion Points to Cover:
* Immediate challenges from extreme weather.
* Long-term threats to job security and operations.
* Ideas for improving climate resilience.
* How to implement disaster preparedness plans.
* Training for workforce adaptation.

Reflecting on Our Discussion

Take a moment to reflect on your discussion:

1. What was the most significant challenge your group identified for your industry regarding climate change?
2. What was the most innovative or practical adaptation strategy suggested?
3. Did you feel confident discussing these complex issues in English? What was easy or difficult?
4. What new vocabulary or phrases did you try to use?

Listen as your teacher gives feedback on the content of your discussions and helps you review some language points.
