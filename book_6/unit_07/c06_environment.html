L-IN

Lead-In: Discussing Green Economy Transitions

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and introduce the topic of the green economy.
2. Write the lead-in question on the board or project it.
3. Put students into small groups (3-4).
4. Ask them to discuss the questions, encouraging them to connect ideas to their current industries or career paths.
5. Monitor discussions, noting any interesting points or common challenges raised.
6. Bring the class back together and elicit key points from each group. Summarize briefly on the board.

Student handout

Your Career in a Greener Future

With the global push towards a greener economy, what new skills or opportunities do you foresee emerging that could impact your career path or industry? Discuss the following questions with your group:

1. Have you noticed any green initiatives or changes towards sustainable practices in your current workplace or industry?
2. What new skills or training do you think might become important for jobs in the future, especially within the renewable energy sector or related fields?
3. Are there any potential challenges or obstacles that you anticipate for workers transitioning to new roles in a green economy?

MOD

Model Analysis: Internal Report on Green Opportunities

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the model text as an internal report from a company moving towards greener practices.
2. Ask students to read the report quickly to get the main idea (gist).
3. Distribute the comprehension tasks.
4. Have students complete the tasks individually or in pairs.
5. Check answers as a whole class, discussing any vocabulary or points of interest.
6. Focus on the purpose, audience, and key information conveyed in the report.

Materials

Internal Update: Workforce Transition to Sustainable Practices

To: All Staff
From: Operations & Sustainability Department
Date: October 26, 2023
Subject: Workforce Transition to Sustainable Practices & New Opportunities

Dear Team,

As part of our company's commitment to environmental responsibility and economic diversification, we are actively transitioning towards more sustainable employment practices across all departments. This strategic shift is in response to global trends and growing demand within the renewable energy sector and related industries.

We understand that such a transition presents both opportunities and potential challenges. To support our valued employees, we are launching comprehensive upskilling programs designed to equip you with the necessary expertise for emerging green jobs within our organization and the wider economy.

Over the next six months, we will focus on:

1. Skill Gap Analysis: Identifying specific areas where current skills can be adapted or enhanced for new roles.
2. Training Modules: Developing practical, hands-on modules covering areas like energy efficiency, waste management, and sustainable logistics.
3. Career Pathways: Mapping out clear progression routes for individuals interested in specialized green jobs, such as sustainability coordinators, eco-logistics specialists, and renewable energy technicians.

We encourage all staff to actively participate in upcoming information sessions to learn more about these upskilling programs and how they can benefit your long-term career development. Your input is crucial as we navigate this exciting and essential transformation. Together, we can build a more resilient and environmentally conscious future for our company and community.

Sincerely,
The Operations & Sustainability Department

Tasks

What is the main purpose of this internal report?

  • To announce new company products in the renewable energy sector.

  • To request staff to volunteer for environmental clean-up initiatives.
  • To provide a financial update on the company's environmental investments.

Teacher notes

2. Present the 'Useful Phrases' and 'Key Green Economy Terms' column banks.
3. Go through the phrases and terms, clarifying meaning and pronunciation as needed.

FP

Free Practice: Writing a Professional Update

Teacher notes

1. Clearly explain the writing task: students will write a short internal email/memo.
2. Emphasize the audience (manager/team lead), purpose (propose/report), and approximate length.
3. Remind students to use language from the previous stage and aim for a professional register.
4. Encourage them to focus on clarity and making a convincing point.
5. Set a time limit for writing (e.g., 15-20 minutes).
6. Circulate and offer support, but avoid correcting language mistakes directly unless they impede understanding. Focus on content and organization.

Model Answer (for teacher reference):

Subject: Proposal: Exploring Green Training for Our Logistics Team

Dear [Manager's Name],

I am writing to propose an initiative to explore upskilling programs for our logistics team, aligning with the company's broader move towards sustainable employment and the growth in the renewable energy sector.

I believe investing in training related to eco-logistics and sustainable supply chain management would offer significant benefits. This would not only enhance our team's capabilities for potential green jobs within the company but also contribute to greater efficiency and reduced carbon footprint in our operations. While there might be an initial investment in these upskilling programs, the long-term benefits of improved resource efficiency and economic diversification are substantial.

I would appreciate the opportunity to discuss how we can implement a pilot training module, focusing on practical skills for sustainable practices relevant to our daily work.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]

Student handout

Drafting Your Green Economy Proposal

You work for a company that is beginning to explore green initiatives and sustainable employment practices. Write a short email or memo to your manager or team lead (around 150-200 words) proposing an idea or reporting on a relevant opportunity or challenge for your department.

Your writing should:

* Audience: Your manager or team lead.
* Purpose: To inform, propose, or suggest a course of action related to green transition/upskilling.
* Content: Focus on a specific green job opportunity, an upskilling program idea, or a challenge related to economic diversification in your sector.
* Length: Approximately 150-200 words.
* Tone: Professional and clear.

Try to include some of the key terms and phrases from the previous activity.

FBDC

Feedback & Correction: Refining Your Report

Teacher notes

1. Explain the purpose of feedback: to improve content first, then language.
2. Pair students up or put them in small groups.
3. Instruct them to exchange their written pieces.
4. Guide them through the content feedback questions first. Emphasize constructive criticism and focusing on whether the message is clear, relevant, and achieves its purpose.
5. After content feedback, instruct them to look for language points. They can highlight areas they are unsure about or suggest improvements for clarity and professional tone.
6. Monitor groups, providing guidance and facilitating feedback.
7. Conclude by collecting any common errors or excellent examples to discuss with the whole class. Encourage students to revise their writing based on the feedback.

Student handout

Reviewing and Improving Your Writing

Now, you will exchange your writing with a partner to give and receive feedback. Use the questions below to guide your review.

Part 1: Content Feedback (Focus on the message)

1. Clarity of Purpose: Is the main point (proposal, opportunity, challenge) of the email/memo clear? What is it?
2. Relevance: Does the content directly relate to the green economy and sustainable employment as intended?
3. Impact: Does the writer effectively communicate the potential benefits or challenges?
4. Audience: Is the tone appropriate for a manager or team lead? Is it professional?

Part 2: Language Feedback (Focus on accuracy and style)

1. Vocabulary: Are key green economy terms used effectively and accurately (e.g., upskilling programs, green jobs, renewable energy sector)?
2. Clarity & Flow: Are there any sentences or phrases that are unclear or could be expressed more effectively?
3. Professionalism: Does the language sound professional and appropriate for a workplace context?

After receiving feedback, revise your email or memo to improve it.
