L-IN

Lead-In: Strategic Health Procurement

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into small groups (2-3).
2. Present the scenario and discussion questions.
3. Ask groups to discuss the critical criteria for selecting an occupational health provider.
4. Encourage them to think about different aspects: cost, quality, service delivery, and long-term sustainability.
5. Facilitate a brief whole-class share-out of key criteria identified by each group.

Student handout

Choosing Our Health Partner

Your company needs to select a new occupational health provider for all its sites. This is a significant decision affecting employee well-being and operational efficiency.

Discuss in your groups:
1. What are the most critical criteria your company would consider when evaluating potential providers?
2. How would you balance factors like cost, quality of care, range of services, and provider reputation?
3. What are the potential risks of making a poor decision, and how can these be mitigated?

MOD

Model Dialogue: Evaluating Health Tenders

Teacher notes

1. Tell students they will read a dialogue between a Procurement Manager and an HR Manager discussing tenders for an occupational health provider.
2. Ask students to read the dialogue and identify the key points each manager is making about the different providers.
3. After reading, use the discussion questions in the student content to check their understanding of the issues raised and the language used.
4. Encourage students to highlight any new or useful phrases related to procurement and evaluation.

Student handout

A Procurement Meeting

Read the following dialogue between Anna, a Procurement Manager, and Mark, an HR Manager, as they discuss the tenders for a new occupational health provider.

---

Anna (Procurement Manager): "Right, Mark, thanks for joining. We've got three strong tenders for the new occupational health provider, and we need to make a solid decision. First up, 'Healthy Horizons'. Their proposal highlights extensive on-site services, which is a definite plus for our multiple sites."

Mark (HR Manager): "Agreed, Anna. On-site access is key for employee uptake and minimizing lost work time. However, I'm slightly concerned about their cost-effectiveness. Their initial quoted price is higher than the others, and I'm not seeing enough flexibility in their contract negotiation terms for a long-term agreement."

Anna: "You've hit on a critical point. While their service quality seems high – excellent references and accreditations – we need to scrutinize that pricing. We can't compromise on quality, but our procurement strategy demands we get the best value."

Mark: "Exactly. Then there's 'WorkWell Solutions'. Their pricing is very competitive, almost too good to be true. I've been looking into their quality assurance protocols, and they seem a bit less robust, especially regarding response times for urgent cases. We need to ensure swift and expert care for our workforce."

Anna: "My thoughts too. Competitive pricing is attractive, but if it means a drop in service, it's a false economy. We need assurances on vendor selection – who are their doctors and specialists, what are their qualifications? A good procurement strategy isn't just about the initial price, but the total cost and benefit over the contract lifecycle. And finally, 'VitalCare Services'. They seem to strike a good balance. Their proposal includes a clear breakdown of services, a reasonable pricing structure, and they've shown more willingness to discuss bespoke terms during preliminary talks."

Mark: "Yes, VitalCare looks promising. Their emphasis on preventative care aligns well with our long-term wellness goals. I think we need to deep dive into VitalCare's quality assurance and get firmer commitments on response times and specialist availability. We also need to understand their onboarding process for our current employees."

Anna: "Good point. Let's list the top three critical areas we need to clarify with VitalCare before we proceed to contract negotiation. Our goal is a high-quality, sustainable service that supports our entire workforce effectively."

---

Discussion Questions:
1. What are the main concerns Anna and Mark have about 'Healthy Horizons' and 'WorkWell Solutions'?
2. Why do they find 'VitalCare Services' more promising?
3. What specific areas do they want to clarify with 'VitalCare Services' before moving forward?
4. What phrases did Anna and Mark use to discuss:
a. Evaluating proposals?
b. Expressing concerns?
c. Suggesting next steps?

PREP

Preparation: Language for Procurement Decisions

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will prepare for a decision-making meeting.
2. Direct students to the phrase banks and encourage them to familiarize themselves with the language.
3. Give students time to make notes, thinking about which phrases they might use and what specific points they want to make. They should consider the different perspectives of various departments.
4. Circulate and assist with vocabulary or sentence structure as needed. Remind them to think about both the criteria and the language to express them.

FP

Fluency Practice: Strategic Procurement Meeting

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into groups of 3-4.
2. Assign roles: e.g., Procurement Manager, HR Manager, Finance Manager, Operations Manager. Each role should have slightly different priorities.
3. Present the scenario: The committee has reviewed three tenders and must now discuss and agree on which provider to select, justifying their choice.
4. Give students 15-20 minutes for the discussion and decision-making.
5. Observe students, making notes on effective communication, use of target language (but without interrupting).
6. Remind students to aim for a consensus decision.

Student handout

The Boardroom Decision

It’s time for the final decision-making meeting. You are part of your company’s Procurement Committee, tasked with selecting the new occupational health provider. You have reviewed the tenders and discussed initial findings.

Your Task:
As a group, discuss the strengths and weaknesses of the potential providers. Consider their proposals in terms of cost-effectiveness, quality assurance, and overall suitability for your company’s needs. Your goal is to reach a consensus on which provider to recommend, clearly stating your reasons.

Roleplay:
* Procurement Manager: Focus on procurement strategy and contract terms. Look for value for money and favorable contract negotiation possibilities.
* HR Manager: Prioritize employee well-being, range of services, and ease of access for staff across all sites. Emphasize vendor selection and provider reputation.
* Finance Manager: Focus on budget adherence, long-term costs, and overall financial viability of the chosen provider. Challenge any proposals that seem too expensive or risky.
* Operations Manager: Emphasize service reliability, response times, and minimal disruption to operations. Consider how the provider integrates with existing company processes.

FBDC

Feedback and Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Content Feedback (5-7 mins):
a. Ask each group to report their final decision and the main reasons for it.
b. Prompt them to reflect on the challenges they faced in reaching a consensus.
c. Did they feel they successfully balanced competing priorities?
2. Language Feedback (5-7 mins):
a. Refer to notes taken during the FP stage.
b. Write up a selection of common errors (e.g., pronunciation, sentence structure, lexis misuse) on the board.
c. Encourage students to self-correct or peer-correct before providing the answers.
d. Highlight examples of good use of target language from the PREP stage (e.g., 'We need to scrutinize that pricing', 'Our procurement strategy demands...').
e. Emphasize that the goal is fluency and effective communication, and errors are a natural part of that process.

Student handout

Reflecting on Our Choices

Now that you've completed your meeting and made a decision:

1. Which occupational health provider did your group select, and what were the primary reasons for your choice?
2. What were the most challenging aspects of reaching a consensus? Did different roles have conflicting priorities?
3. Reflect on your use of the language from the preparation stage. Were you able to effectively use phrases for evaluating, expressing concerns, and making decisions?
