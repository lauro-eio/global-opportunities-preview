L-IN

Lead-In: Workplace Safety Updates

Teacher notes

1. Begin by drawing on the students' personal experiences with workplace safety.
2. Prompt discussion using the lead-in brief, encouraging them to think about their own companies.
3. Facilitate a brief open-class discussion, noting down key ideas and vocabulary that may emerge (without explicit correction at this stage).

Student handout

Talking About Safety at Work

Imagine your company is updating its safety manual to align with new health standards. What are the key areas you'd expect to see addressed in such an update?

Discuss these questions with a partner:
1. What does 'employee well-being' mean to you in the context of your job?
2. What kind of 'operational safety' procedures are most important in your workplace?
3. Have you ever seen your company update its safety policies? What was the reason?

PTV

Pre-Teach Vocabulary: Key Safety Terms

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the vocabulary items to the students.
2. Ask students if they already know any of the words and elicit their meanings.
3. Instruct students to complete the matching task individually or in pairs.
4. Review answers as a class, clarifying any misunderstandings and modeling pronunciation.

Student handout

Understanding Key Safety Words

Before we read a document about workplace safety, let's learn some important terms you might see. Match the safety terms with their definitions.

Materials

Vocabulary
  • hazard assessment
  • risk mitigation
  • policy review
  • compliance
  • incident reporting
Tasks

Match each term to its correct definition.

  • hazard assessment — Identifying potential dangers or risks in the workplace.
  • risk mitigation — Taking steps to reduce the likelihood or impact of identified dangers.
  • policy review — Examining and updating official rules and guidelines.
  • compliance — Following rules, standards, or laws.
  • incident reporting — Documenting any accidents, near-misses, or safety concerns.
GIST

Gist Reading: Company Safety Protocol Review

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the reading passage as a memo about new safety protocols.
2. Explain the concept of 'gist' reading – reading for the main idea, not every detail.
3. Set a strict time limit (e.g., 2-3 minutes) for students to read the memo quickly.
4. Instruct students to complete the multiple-choice question to check their understanding of the main purpose.
5. Review the answer as a class, ensuring students understand why the correct option is the best summary.

Student handout

First Look at the New Safety Guide

You are going to read a memo from a company about updates to their workplace safety protocols. Read it quickly to understand the main purpose of the memo. Don't worry about understanding every single word, just get the general idea. Then, answer the question below.

Materials

MEMORANDUM: Updated Workplace Safety Protocol Review

To: All Staff
From: [Company Name] HR Department
Date: October 26, 2023
Subject: Crucial Updates to Workplace Safety Protocols

This memorandum outlines important revisions to our existing workplace safety protocols. In response to evolving health standards and our commitment to employee well-being, we are implementing new strategies designed to create a safer and healthier work environment for everyone.

The core of these updates involves a thorough policy review to ensure all guidelines are current and effective. Our safety committee has been working diligently to integrate best practices for health and safety, considering feedback from various departments.

Key areas of focus include enhanced procedures for hazard assessment. We are introducing new tools and training to help teams identify potential risks proactively, not just reactively. This includes regular walkthroughs and a new digital reporting system for concerns. Following a comprehensive hazard assessment, we will implement targeted strategies for risk mitigation. These measures are designed to minimize exposure to hazards and prevent accidents before they occur. This could involve new equipment, revised work procedures, or additional personal protective equipment (PPE).

Crucially, compliance with these updated protocols is mandatory for all employees. Adherence to safety regulations protects not only yourself but also your colleagues. We will be conducting a series of workshops next month to ensure everyone understands the new guidelines and their responsibilities.

Finally, we are enhancing our incident reporting system. A new, simplified online form will be available to report any accidents, near-misses, or safety concerns promptly. Timely reporting is vital for our continuous improvement efforts and allows us to address issues effectively.

Your active participation in understanding and implementing these protocols is essential for our collective safety. Please attend the upcoming training sessions and review the full updated safety manual available on the company intranet by November 10th.

Tasks

What is the main purpose of this memo?

  • To announce new hires in the HR department.
  • To inform staff about upcoming changes to workplace safety protocols.
  • To request feedback on current company policies.
  • To remind employees about annual leave policies.
DET

Detailed Reading: Understanding the Protocols

Teacher notes

1. Tell students to read the memo again, this time more carefully.
2. Instruct them to complete the short-text questions individually.
3. After reviewing the factual answers as a class, put students in pairs or small groups for the discussion prompts.
4. Circulate and monitor discussions, encouraging the use of the target vocabulary.
5. Bring the class back together to share key discussion points. (Do NOT provide answers for discussion, just facilitate sharing).

FBDC

Follow-Up Discussion and Language Notes

Teacher notes

1. Open the floor for general feedback and questions regarding the memo's content.
2. Prompt students to share their personal opinions on the new protocols and their relevance.
3. Encourage the active use of vocabulary introduced earlier (e.g., 'hazard assessment', 'risk mitigation').
4. Listen carefully for good usage of target lexis and highlight these examples to the class. Avoid direct error correction of grammar; focus on content and lexical usage.
