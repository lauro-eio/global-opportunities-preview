Lead-In: Workplace Wellness & Benefits

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and introduce the day's theme: workplace health and wellness.
2. Pose the lead-in question to the class.
3. Facilitate a brief discussion, encouraging students to share their initial thoughts and experiences regarding current benefits and potential improvements.
4. Encourage students to consider the impact of these initiatives on workplace culture and productivity.

Understanding New Health Initiatives

Read the following memo quickly to understand its main purpose. Don't worry about every detail yet. After reading, answer the multiple-choice question below.

Materials

Interim Review: Enhancing Employee Wellness Strategy

To: Executive Management Committee
From: Dr. Eleanor Vance, Head of Human Resources
Date: October 26, 2023
Subject: Findings and Recommendations from Our Q3 Employee Wellness Program Review

Dear Committee Members,

Following a comprehensive internal review of our current employee benefits and wellness programs, I am pleased to present an interim report on their efficacy and propose strategic improvements. Our objective remains to ensure employee wellbeing and foster a productive, healthy workforce.

The review, which included anonymized staff feedback and a survey on access to support services, highlighted several areas for strategic enhancement. While current initiatives such as our gym membership subsidies are well-received, their impact on overall preventative care and mental health support is less significant than desired. Many employees feel disconnected from the broader health strategy, perceiving some benefits as isolated perks rather than an integrated system.

Specifically, we have identified that our existing psychological counselling service, while valuable, needs having its accessibility streamlined. Employees report difficulties navigating the booking system, which inadvertently creates a barrier to seeking medical advice promptly. Furthermore, our current HR policies regarding health leave, though compliant, often leave managers feeling ill-equipped to address health and safety concerns proactively. Training for team leaders on early intervention strategies needs developing urgently.

Looking forward, we propose a multi-pronged approach. Firstly, a new digital platform needs implementing to centralize all wellness resources and allow for anonymous feedback, which would significantly improve the perceived integration of our efforts. Secondly, we recommend that external experts have our current preventative care offerings audited against industry best practices to identify gaps. Thirdly, and critically, our internal mental health first aid network needs expanding across all departments; this will require substantial investment in specialized training. We are confident that these adjustments will not only elevate our commitment to health but also yield measurable improvements in productivity and morale.

We look forward to discussing these recommendations at our upcoming meeting.

Sincerely,
Dr. Eleanor Vance

GD

Guided Discovery: Structure and Professional Language

Teacher notes

1. Direct students to re-read the memo, this time focusing on specific sentence structures and professional phrases.
2. For Task 1 (Causative Passive/Gerundial), guide students to identify the sentences and then discuss the 'who' and 'why': Is the subject performing the action, or arranging for someone else? What does 'needs + -ing' imply?
3. For Task 2 (Pragmatic Lexis), have students find the phrases and discuss their function in a professional, diplomatic context.
4. Explicitly clarify the forms and meanings of the target structures after students have made their discoveries.

FP

Freer Practice: Proposing Improvements

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now work individually or in pairs to draft a short proposal, applying the learned structures and vocabulary.
2. Reiterate the scenario: imagining they are proposing improvements for their own workplace's health benefits.
3. Remind them to use causative passive, gerundial necessities, and professional communication phrases.
4. Encourage them to be specific with their suggestions and diplomatic in their language.
5. Circulate, offering support and feedback on language accuracy, appropriateness, and clarity.
6. After drafting, have students share their proposals in small groups or with the class, fostering a discussion about the practicality and impact of their suggestions.

Sample Answer/Key Points for Feedback:
- Introduction: Briefly state the purpose of the proposal (e.g., 'to enhance existing provisions and proactively ensure employee wellbeing').
- Current Situation (optional): Briefly acknowledge current benefits, perhaps noting where they 'need having their impact reassessed'.
- Proposed Improvements (using target language):
- 'A new mental health support helpline needs implementing for immediate access to support services.'
- 'We recommend that external consultants have our preventative care strategies audited to identify best practices.'
- 'Managerial training on how to address health and safety concerns needs developing urgently.'
- 'We also need to have a comprehensive survey conducted to gather more detailed feedback on employee benefits.'
- Conclusion: Briefly state expected outcomes (e.g., 'These measures will contribute significantly to overall staff health and morale, reducing the need to seek medical advice for preventable issues.').
- Register: Diplomatic, professional, solution-focused.

Student handout

Developing a Wellness Proposal

Imagine you are drafting a brief proposal to your company's management committee, suggesting improvements to your workplace's health and wellness initiatives.

Write a short proposal (around 100-150 words) incorporating at least:
* Two instances of the causative passive (e.g., 'We need to have our policies reviewed').
* Two instances of a gerundial operational necessity (e.g., 'The program needs expanding').
* At least three of the professional communication phrases we studied (e.g., 'ensure employee wellbeing', 'address health and safety concerns').

Focus on maintaining a professional and diplomatic tone, similar to Dr. Vance's memo. Be prepared to share your proposal and discuss your reasoning.
