Student handout

Navigating Data: Trust & Responsibility

Companies are increasingly collecting health-related data to optimize operations or improve employee well-being. This can include anything from fitness tracker data for wellness programs to sick leave records. Where do you draw the line between useful insights derived from this data and a potential invasion of employee privacy?

Discuss the following with a partner:
1. What kind of health-related data might your current or past employer collect, and for what purpose?
2. What are the potential benefits of collecting this data for both the company and the employees?
3. What are the main risks or concerns employees might have about their health data being collected and stored?
4. What measures do you think companies should put in place to protect employee data privacy?

Understanding a Sensitive Memo

Read the internal memo below from the HR Director regarding the management of employee health data. Pay attention to how it balances company needs with employee rights and privacy.

Then, complete the tasks that follow.

Materials

Internal Memo: Employee Health Data Guidelines

To: All Department Managers
From: Eleanor Vance, HR Director
Date: October 26, 20XX
Subject: Proposed Guidelines for Employee Health Data Management

Dear Managers,

Following our recent discussions on optimizing employee well-being initiatives and operational efficiency, it has become apparent that a clear framework for managing employee health data is essential. This memo outlines a proposed set of guidelines aimed at ensuring data privacy and maintaining trust within our organization.

Our approach will be guided by principles of transparency, necessity, and informed consent. We propose that any collection of health-related data will:

1. Be strictly limited to what is relevant and necessary for the stated purpose, such as administering health benefits or implementing approved wellness programs.
2. Require explicit and informed consent from employees, clearly detailing what data will be collected, why, how it will be used, and who will have access to it.
3. Prioritize anonymization wherever possible, especially when data is used for aggregated statistical analysis rather than individual identification.
4. Adhere rigorously to GDPR compliance standards and other relevant data security regulations, ensuring robust protection against unauthorized access or breaches.
5. Establish clear ethical guidelines for data retention and deletion, preventing indefinite storage of sensitive information.

We understand the importance of balancing organizational needs with individual rights. These guidelines are designed to foster a culture of respect and accountability. We invite your feedback on these proposals by November 10th, so we can finalize a comprehensive policy that supports both employee welfare and operational integrity.

Best regards,
Eleanor Vance
HR Director

Tasks

What is the primary purpose of Eleanor Vance's memo?

The primary purpose of the memo is to:

  • Announce a new mandatory health data collection program.
  • Seek feedback on proposed guidelines for managing employee health data.
  • Inform managers about a data breach incident.
  • Request managers to collect more employee health data.

According to the memo, what three guiding principles will the company's approach to health data collection follow?

List the three guiding principles mentioned in the memo.

Which of the following phrases from the memo best demonstrates a professional and cautious tone regarding a sensitive topic?

Which phrase reflects a cautious and professional tone?

  • it has become apparent that a clear framework is essential
  • We understand the importance of balancing organizational needs with individual rights
  • These guidelines are designed to foster a culture of respect and accountability
  • All of the above.

Preparation: Structuring Your Data Ethics Recommendation

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the writing task: preparing a recommendation for ethical health data management.
2. Brainstorm with students potential arguments for and against collecting certain types of data, keeping the target audience in mind.
3. Guide students through the column banks, explaining the usage of each phrase for a professional written recommendation.
4. Encourage students to select relevant phrases for their own planned writing.

Student handout

Your Recommendation: A Balanced Approach to Health Data

Imagine you are a department manager tasked with providing feedback to HR about the proposed employee health data guidelines. Write a short internal memo or email (approximately 150-200 words) to Eleanor Vance (HR Director).

Your memo should:
* Acknowledge the importance of the proposed guidelines.
* Suggest one or two additional considerations or recommendations regarding data privacy, informed consent, or data security from an employee's perspective.
* Maintain a professional, constructive, and persuasive tone.

Audience: HR Director
Purpose: To provide constructive feedback and recommendations on employee health data guidelines.
Approximate Length: 150-200 words

FBDC

Feedback: Content and Language Review

Teacher notes

1. Pair students for peer feedback. Emphasize constructive criticism focusing on clarity, adherence to the brief, and use of professional language.
2. Circulate and observe feedback sessions, noting common errors or areas for improvement.
3. After peer feedback, collect the revised memos. Provide teacher feedback, focusing on: a) Task achievement (did they meet the brief?); b) Content (clarity of recommendations, persuasive arguments); c) Language (accuracy, range, appropriate register, use of target lexis from the unit).
4. Highlight strong examples of language use and offer corrections for recurring errors.

Student handout

Refining Your Ethical Data Memo

Now you will review your memo and provide feedback on a partner's work.

Part 1: Peer Feedback
Swap your memo with a partner. Read their memo carefully and consider the following questions. Write down your feedback in a constructive way.
1. Is the memo's purpose clear? Does it clearly provide recommendations to the HR Director?
2. Does it address the topics of data privacy, informed consent, or data security effectively?
3. Is the tone professional and appropriate for an internal memo?
4. Are there any parts that are unclear or could be expressed more persuasively?
5. Did your partner use any of the key phrases from our 'Preparation' stage effectively?

Part 2: Self-Correction
Based on your partner's feedback and your own review, make any necessary revisions to your memo to improve its clarity, effectiveness, and professionalism.
