Lead-In: Ethical Reporting in the Workplace

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking students to think about the lead-in brief scenario, perhaps sharing a personal (hypothetical) experience or general observation related to workplace ethics.
2. Prompt a short group or pair discussion (3-5 minutes) about initial thoughts on discovering serious misconduct.
3. Elicit key vocabulary related to the theme, such as whistleblower protection, ethical reporting, internal investigation, code of conduct, and retaliation prevention, writing them on the board.
4. Guide students to consider the complexity of legal considerations and protections, and any advanced language they might use to discuss these complexities.

Student handout

Considering Your Options

Imagine you discover serious unethical or illegal behavior in your workplace. This could be anything from financial fraud to safety violations. It's a difficult situation that requires careful consideration.

Discuss with a partner or in a small group:
1. What are your immediate thoughts or concerns if you found yourself in this situation, weighing personal risk against professional ethics?
2. What legal considerations should guide your decision to report it, particularly regarding your rights and potential liabilities?
3. What kind of whistleblower protection or legal safeguards do you believe should exist for someone who reports such activities?
4. What complex language or structures would you use to describe the interconnectedness of ethical standards and legal consequences in such a scenario?

Teach: Clarifying Double Comparatives for Causal Analysis

Teacher notes

1. Review the answers from T1, explicitly highlighting common errors from the diagnostic exercises.
2. Re-explain the meaning of the double comparative: 'Demonstrating parallel increases or decreases between structural factors, jurisprudence parameters, bureaucratic hurdles, and ethical standards within state bodies.' Emphasize that a change in the first factor directly triggers a corresponding change in the second.
3. Clearly present the form: 'The + comparative adjective/adverb + clause, the + comparative adjective/adverb + clause'. Provide a negative example if necessary: 'The less transparency, the more distrust'.
4. Use the concept_check_questions:
- 'Does a change in the first legal system variable directly trigger a corresponding change in the second variable?' (Yes)
- 'Do we use standard base adjectives or comparative forms in this dual structure?' (Comparative forms)
5. Provide further practice with the match_pairs task to solidify the correct form and meaning, ensuring students can construct these complex sentences accurately.

Student handout

Mastering Interdependencies

We've seen how complex legal and ethical factors influence each other in the workplace. To express these parallel changes clearly and professionally, we use the 'double comparative' structure:

The + comparative adjective/adverb + clause, the + comparative adjective/adverb + clause

For example:
* _The more bureaucracy scales, the more complex the legal system becomes._
* _The less public opinion shifts, the more politicians respect the social contract._

This structure shows a direct, parallel relationship where one change causes a corresponding change in the other. Remember to always use comparative forms and include 'the' before each comparative clause.

Now, let's practice matching sentence halves to correctly use this structure, focusing on the meaning of direct consequence.

Materials

Tasks

Match the first half of each statement with its correct second half to form a complete double comparative sentence, reflecting legal or ethical dynamics.

  • The more stringent the oversight of internal investigation, — the greater the accountability for misconduct.
  • The less effective retaliation prevention policies are, — the lower the willingness of employees to report.
  • The more quickly a company responds to ethical reporting, — the less damage it causes to its reputation.
  • The more complex the legal framework for whistleblower protection, — the more crucial clear guidance becomes.
  • The less a code of conduct is communicated, — the more likely breaches are to occur.
T2

Test 2: Applying Double Comparatives in a Case Study

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the brief case study on a hypothetical whistleblower situation, ensuring students understand the professional context.
2. Instruct students to read the scenario and then answer the questions, explicitly using the double comparative structure (e.g., 'The more... the more...').
3. Emphasize linking different elements of the case study using the target structure to show causal relationships.
4. Monitor for accurate use of form and meaning of the double comparative, as well as the logical connection between ideas presented in their answers.
5. Model answers can vary, but should correctly apply the double comparative structure and reflect appropriate professional language. Example for Q1: 'The more comprehensive her documentation, the stronger her legal position, and the harder it is for management to implement retaliation prevention measures effectively.'

Student handout

Analyzing a Whistleblower Scenario

Read the following scenario about an employee considering reporting misconduct. Then, answer the questions, using the double comparative structure ('The + comparative... the + comparative...') to explain the potential outcomes and relationships between factors. Aim for concise, professional analysis.

Scenario: Maria's Dilemma
Maria works for a logistics company. She's discovered compelling evidence of systematic overbilling to clients, potentially involving senior management. She is contemplating reporting this but is deeply concerned about her job security and potential repercussions. She has been quietly gathering documents and emails for weeks. The company has a code of conduct that vaguely mentions ethical reporting, but the whistleblower protection policy is unclear, and past internal investigation outcomes have not always been transparent or fair. Maria needs to weigh the risks and benefits carefully, considering both company policy and external legal avenues.

Answer the following questions using double comparatives:
1. How do you think the quality of Maria's evidence relates to her potential legal standing and the effectiveness of retaliation prevention?
2. What is the relationship between the clarity of the company's whistleblower protection policy and Maria's willingness to report the misconduct?
3. How might the company's prior record of internal investigation transparency affect the public's perception if Maria's story becomes public?
4. In what way does the severity of the illegal activity relate to the potential for a larger, more impactful external investigation?
