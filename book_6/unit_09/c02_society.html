Essential Guide: Understanding Your Employment Contract

Subject: Essential Guide: Understanding Your Employment Contract
Dear New Colleague,

Welcome to the team! We are delighted to have you on board. As you prepare to embark on your journey with us, it’s crucial to understand the terms outlined in your employment contract. This document is a cornerstone of our professional relationship, designed to protect both your rights and the company's interests.

We strongly advise all new hires to conduct due diligence by carefully reviewing each clause. Pay particular attention to your probationary period duration, the specific details within your job description, and the termination clause. Understanding these sections fully is paramount. For instance, the more thoroughly you comprehend the conditions of your probationary period, the smoother your transition into a permanent role will be. Similarly, the clearer the expectations set out in your job description, the more effectively you can perform your duties.

Our contracts also include important confidentiality agreements and sections detailing your responsibility to adhere to regulations governing data protection and intellectual property. Any breach of contract can lead to serious repercussions. We are committed to operating transparently and to uphold the law in all our practices. If any clause seems unclear, or if you require further clarification, we highly recommend you seek legal counsel before signing.

Your proactive approach in understanding these terms and conditions ensures a strong and mutually respectful professional foundation.

Sincerely,
[Company Name] HR Department

GD

Guided Discovery: Legal Language and Causal Links

Teacher notes

1. Draw students' attention to the emphasized phrases in the memo, particularly the double comparatives. Ask them what they notice about their structure and what function they serve.
2. Elicit the form: 'The + comparative... the + comparative...'. Explain its meaning (one action/situation directly influences another).
3. Discuss the concept check questions from the target: 'Does a change in the first legal system variable directly trigger a corresponding change in the second variable? (Yes)', 'Do we use standard base adjectives or comparative forms in this dual structure? (Comparative forms)'.
4. Next, focus on the professional lexis. Ask students to identify other formal legal terms or phrases in the memo. Discuss why these specific words are used instead of more informal alternatives in a professional document like this (precision, formality, legal weight). Guide them to understand their pragmatic function in conveying professionalism and clarity in high-stakes contexts.
5. Go through the matching exercise for the double comparatives.
6. Review the meaning and form of the double comparatives explicitly. Highlight 'The + comparative adjective/adverb + clause, the + comparative adjective/adverb + clause'.
7. Review the meaning of the professional lexis, emphasizing their use in formal discussions related to legal concepts.

FP

Freer Practice: Discussing Contractual Implications

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs or small groups. Instruct them to discuss the scenario, encouraging them to use the double comparative structure and professional legal lexis where appropriate.
2. Circulate and monitor, noting examples of correct and incorrect usage, and providing support as needed.
3. Facilitate a brief whole-class feedback session, asking groups to share their key points. Highlight good examples of the target language. Sample answer could include: 'The more comprehensive your understanding of the termination clause, the better prepared you are for any future changes. Also, always conduct due diligence on any additional terms, and if in doubt, seek legal counsel to avoid a potential breach of contract.'

Student handout

Your Professional Judgment

Imagine a friend has just received a job offer, but the contract includes several clauses they find confusing or potentially unfair, particularly regarding hours, overtime, and annual leave. They ask for your advice. In pairs or small groups, discuss what advice you would give them. Use the double comparative structures and professional legal lexis we've studied today to explain the potential implications and suggest a course of action. Think about:
* What specific advice would you give regarding reviewing terms?
* How would you explain the importance of clarity in contracts?
* What are the consequences of not understanding or agreeing to terms?
* When should they seek external advice?
