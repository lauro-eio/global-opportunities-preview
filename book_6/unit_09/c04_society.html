Lead-In: Addressing Faulty Business Equipment

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and introduce the class theme around consumer rights in a business context.
2. Read the scenario aloud from 'student_content' and ask students to discuss the questions in pairs or small groups.
3. Facilitate a brief whole-class discussion, noting key ideas and initial steps they would consider on the board.
4. Link their ideas to the concept of advocating for consumer rights when dealing with problematic suppliers.

3. What kind of information would you need to support your claim according to commercial consumer law?

Materials

Vocabulary
  • warranty claim
  • refund policy
  • service agreement
  • dispute resolution
  • consumer protection act
Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • warranty claim — A formal request made to a seller or manufacturer to repair or replace a faulty product, based on the terms of a guarantee.
  • refund policy — The rules set by a business regarding the conditions under which a customer can return a product and receive their money back.
  • service agreement — A contract between a service provider and a customer that outlines the terms and conditions of a service to be provided.
  • dispute resolution — The process of settling a disagreement or argument between two or more parties, often through negotiation, mediation, or arbitration.
  • consumer protection act — Legislation designed to protect the rights of buyers of goods and services against unfair practices by sellers or manufacturers.
GIST

Gist Listening: Understanding a Consumer Rights Scenario

Teacher notes

1. Tell students they will hear a conversation between two colleagues about a business problem.
2. Instruct them to listen for the main idea of the discussion and answer the multiple-choice question.
3. Play the audio once.
4. Check answers as a class and briefly discuss the overall context without going into specific details.

Student handout

Who is Calling and Why?

Listen to the conversation between two colleagues. What is the main purpose of their discussion?

Materials

Discussion on Faulty Office Equipment

SOUND of busy office environment, faint.

Sarah: Morning, Mark. Do you have a moment? I need to discuss the new coffee machine we ordered last month.

Mark: Of course, Sarah. What’s up with it? I thought it was working fine.

Sarah: Well, it was at first. But for the past week, it’s been leaking water all over the counter, and yesterday it completely stopped brewing. It’s a huge problem for the team, especially with client meetings. We depend on it.

Mark: That’s not good at all. We paid a premium for that machine. Did you check the warranty claim details? I remember the supplier, 'BrewMaster Pro', offered a six-month warranty on parts and labor.

Sarah: I did. I pulled up our purchase order and the service agreement. It clearly states a full replacement or repair if it fails within the first three months. We’re only five weeks in.

Mark: Excellent. So, what’s our next step? Have you tried calling them?

Sarah: I called their customer service yesterday. The representative was polite but unhelpful. They said they'd send a technician out 'sometime next week,' which isn't acceptable. We need coffee for today's pitch!

Mark: 'Sometime next week'? That's not good enough. This is affecting our operations. We need to push for a faster resolution. What about their refund policy? Is that an option if they can't fix it promptly?

Sarah: The policy is tricky. It allows for a full refund within 30 days if the item is returned in 'as-new' condition, which ours isn't, given the leaks. But it also states that if repairs can't be completed in a reasonable timeframe, they must offer a replacement. And a week's wait is not reasonable for essential office equipment.

Mark: Agreed. We need to escalate this. Have you looked into our rights under the Consumer Protection Act for business-to-business transactions? Sometimes, even for commercial sales, there are provisions for timely service and fitness for purpose.

Sarah: I briefly scanned it, yes. It seems to support our position regarding the 'fitness for purpose' clause. The machine isn't fit for purpose if it breaks down after a month. I think we need to formalize a complaint, perhaps an email referencing the Act and our service agreement.

Mark: Good idea. If they don’t respond adequately, we might need to consider dispute resolution. Perhaps their internal complaints procedure or even mediation. Let’s draft a strong email together, outlining the facts and our expectations. We need a replacement, not a repair, if they can't guarantee a fix by tomorrow morning. We can't afford any more downtime.

Tasks

Listen to the conversation and choose the best option for the main purpose of their discussion.

What is the main topic of Sarah and Mark's conversation?

  • They are planning to buy new office equipment.
  • They are discussing a problem with a faulty coffee machine and how to get it resolved.
  • They are reviewing the company's internal purchasing policies.
  • They are complaining about the quality of their coffee.
DET

Detailed Listening: Specifics of the Equipment Dispute

Teacher notes

1. Tell students they will listen again, this time for specific details to answer the questions.
2. Instruct them to write down short answers or specific information from the audio.
3. Play the audio a second time.
4. Allow time for students to complete their answers.
5. Check answers as a class, prompting students to justify their responses by referring to parts of the conversation.
6. For the 'Think about it' question, discuss their suggested next immediate actions and compare them to Sarah and Mark's plans (drafting a strong email).

Student handout

Key Details of the Issue

Listen again to the conversation. Answer the questions with short answers or specific details from the discussion.

1. What specific problem is the coffee machine having?
2. How long has the company owned the coffee machine?
3. According to the service agreement, what is the supplier obliged to do if the machine fails within the first three months?
4. What is Mark's opinion about the customer service representative's proposed solution?
5. Name one piece of legislation Mark and Sarah mention might support their claim.

Think about it: What is the most immediate action Sarah and Mark plan to take after this call?

FBDC

Follow-up Discussion: Escalating a Business Dispute

Teacher notes

1. Lead a discussion about how students would handle a similar situation in their own workplace, encouraging them to use the vocabulary learned today.
2. Emphasize the importance of documenting all communications and understanding commercial consumer rights.
3. Discuss different stages of dispute resolution and when each might be appropriate.
4. Provide feedback on any language errors related to the target lexis or general comprehension strategies used during listening.

Student handout

Your Next Steps for Resolution

Sarah and Mark plan to draft a strong email to the supplier.

Discuss in pairs or small groups:
1. What specific points should they include in their email to make it effective and professional?
2. If the email doesn't work, what further steps might they need to take for dispute resolution?
3. How important is it for businesses to understand their rights under the Consumer Protection Act when dealing with problematic business suppliers or services? Why?

Think about a time you or your company had to deal with a problematic supplier or service. What did you do?
