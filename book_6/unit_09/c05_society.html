Lead-In: Workplace Data Sensitivity

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking students to think about the different types of sensitive information they encounter daily in their jobs (customer details, colleague medical info, financial records).
2. Elicit examples of how this information is handled and stored.
3. Facilitate a brief discussion using the provided questions, encouraging them to share any experiences or concerns they have regarding data privacy in their workplace.

TCH

Teach: Professional Lexis for Legal Discussions

Teacher notes

1. Go over the answers to T1, eliciting why certain options are more appropriate than others in a formal, professional context.
2. Present the target exponents: seek legal counsel, adhere to regulations, breach of contract, uphold the law, conduct due diligence.
3. Clarify meaning: Use CCQs to check understanding of each phrase. E.g., for 'seek legal counsel': 'What does 'counsel' mean here?' 'Why would you 'seek' it?' 'Is it formal or informal?'
4. Discuss register: Emphasize that these are formal, professional expressions crucial for workplace dignity and legal precision. Contrast with common, informal equivalents where useful (e.g., 'ask a lawyer' vs. 'seek legal counsel').
5. Phonology: Model pronunciation. Focus on clear articulation and stressing the main content words within these multi-word expressions (e.g., seek legal counsel, adhere to regulations, breach of contract, uphold the law, conduct due diligence). Drill chorally and individually.
6. Use the 'Formal Legal Phrases' table to summarize. Then, give students the matching task to consolidate meaning and form.

T2

Test 2: Applying Professional Legal Lexis

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs or small groups.
2. Present them with the two scenarios provided in 'Student Content'.
3. Instruct them to discuss each scenario, focusing on using the formal legal phrases taught in the previous stage.
4. Encourage them to consider what actions should be taken, what risks are involved, and how to communicate these issues professionally.
5. Monitor for accurate use of the target lexis and appropriate register. Note down any common errors for later correction.
6. At the end, invite groups to share their conclusions and solutions, providing feedback on their language use.

Student handout

Discussing Workplace Data Scenarios

Imagine you are discussing important data privacy issues with colleagues or management. Use the formal legal phrases you have learned to discuss the following scenarios:

Scenario 1: New Vendor Partnership
Your company is considering partnering with a new software vendor who will handle a large amount of customer data. You need to discuss the steps to ensure this partnership is secure and legally compliant.

* What steps should your team conduct due diligence on before signing any agreements?
* What specific regulations do you need to ensure the vendor will adhere to?
* What would be the consequences of a breach of contract if the vendor fails to protect data?

Scenario 2: Employee Data Misuse
An employee accidentally shared a confidential client list with an external, unauthorized party. This is a serious incident.

* What immediate action should the company take to uphold the law regarding data privacy?
* Who should the employee or management seek legal counsel from?
* How should this incident be communicated internally and externally (if necessary), using professional language?
