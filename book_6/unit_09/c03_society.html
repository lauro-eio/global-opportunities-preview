L-IN

Lead-In

Teacher notes

1. Begin by asking students to think about their own workplaces.
2. Pose the lead-in question to the whole class, then put students into pairs or small groups (3-4 people) to discuss their answers.
3. Encourage them to share specific examples of rules and the reasons behind them.
4. Monitor for early examples of target vocabulary, but do not correct at this stage. Note any common issues for later.

Student handout

Your Workplace, Your Safety

Think about your workplace. Discuss with a partner:
1. What are the top three safety rules you must follow daily?
2. Why are these rules legally important for everyone's well-being?
3. Have you ever seen a safety rule that seemed unnecessary, or one that was extremely important but often ignored?

MOD

Model Dialogue

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the dialogue as an example of a worker reporting a safety concern to their supervisor.
2. Ask students to read or listen (if read aloud by teacher) to the dialogue once to get the main idea: What is the main problem being reported?
3. Second reading/listening: Ask students to notice how the worker describes the issue and what specific actions are discussed.
4. Briefly check understanding (e.g., 'Who is reporting? What is the hazard? What's the next step?'). Avoid detailed comprehension questions, focus on noticing language for reporting.

Student handout

Reporting a Workplace Issue

Read the conversation between a team leader, Alex, and a worker, Ben, about a safety concern. Pay attention to how Ben reports the issue and what Alex suggests.

Alex: Good morning, Ben. What's on your mind?
Ben: Morning, Alex. I need to bring something to your attention regarding the loading bay. I've noticed a few forklifts operating too close to pedestrian walkways, especially during peak hours. It feels like a significant hazard.
Alex: Thank you for reporting that, Ben. That's a serious concern. Have you seen any near-misses?
Ben: Not yet, thankfully, but it's only a matter of time if we don't reinforce the current safety protocol. I'm worried about someone getting injured. We also haven't checked the PPE requirements for visitors in that area for a while, I think.
Alex: You're right to flag this. We need to conduct an immediate hazard assessment of the area. I'll also ensure our team re-reads the updated safety protocol guidelines on vehicle-pedestrian separation. We may need to file an internal incident report detailing your observations and our preventative actions, especially with the upcoming regulatory inspection next month. For now, please remind anyone you see operating unsafely.
Ben: Will do, Alex. Thanks for taking it seriously.
Alex: Workplace safety is everyone's responsibility, Ben. Your vigilance is appreciated.

PREP

Preparation

Teacher notes

1. Explain that these phrases are useful for discussing and reporting safety concerns in a professional manner.
2. Go through each column, clarifying meaning and pronunciation if needed.
3. Encourage students to think about which phrases they heard in the model dialogue and which ones they might use in a similar situation.
4. Give students a few minutes to silently review the phrases and choose some they want to try to use in the next activity.

FP

Fluency Practice

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs. Assign 'Worker' and 'Supervisor' roles.
2. Explain the scenario clearly, emphasizing that the 'Worker' needs to use the language from the PREP stage to effectively report the issue, and the 'Supervisor' needs to respond professionally.
3. Give students a few minutes to prepare their roles individually, perhaps making a few notes based on the scenario and the phrase bank.
4. Instruct students to begin their role-plays. Monitor closely, focusing on fluency and the effective use of target language. Do not interrupt for correction; note down language points for FBDC.
5. After 5-7 minutes, have pairs switch roles and try a different scenario if time allows, or simply reflect on their performance.

Student handout

Workplace Safety Challenge

Work in pairs. You will role-play a scenario involving a workplace safety issue. Try to use phrases from the previous activity.

Scenario:
A new piece of equipment has been installed in your factory/warehouse/office. You (Worker) have noticed that the operating instructions are unclear, and the emergency stop button is not easily accessible. You are also concerned that the required PPE requirements (e.g., safety glasses, gloves) are not being consistently enforced when operating it.

Role A: The Worker
You have a serious concern about the safety of the new equipment. You need to report this to your supervisor. Clearly explain:
* What the hazard is.
* Why it's a problem (potential consequences).
* Suggest what actions need to be taken (e.g., reviewing safety protocol, conducting a new hazard assessment).

Role B: The Supervisor
You are the supervisor. Your worker is coming to you with a safety concern. You need to:
* Listen carefully and professionally.
* Acknowledge the seriousness of the issue.
* Ask clarifying questions.
* Explain what steps will be taken next (e.g., checking PPE requirements, reviewing safety protocol, considering an incident report or a future regulatory inspection).
* Reassure the worker that their concerns are being taken seriously.

FBDC

Feedback and Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Content Feedback (5-7 mins): Open the floor for general feedback. Ask students: 'What were some of the main safety issues reported in your role-plays?' or 'How effectively did the supervisor respond to the concerns?' Focus on the clarity and effectiveness of their communication regarding the content of the safety reports.
2. Language Feedback (8-10 mins): Write down a selection of common errors (pronunciation, grammar, lexis) observed during the FP stage on the board. Do not attribute errors to specific students.
3. Guide students to collaboratively correct the mistakes. For example, 'How can we say this more professionally?' or 'Is there a more precise word for this?'
4. Highlight examples of good language use you heard, especially target vocabulary from the PREP stage. Encourage students to note down corrections or useful phrases for future use.
