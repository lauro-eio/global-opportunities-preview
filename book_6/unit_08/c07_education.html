Rethinking 'How We've Always Done It'

At work, we often follow routines simply because 'that's how it's always been done.' Sometimes, new evidence or insights suggest a better way. Think about your own workplace or a previous job.

1. Can you think of a particular practice, procedure, or policy you've encountered that you believe could be done differently and more effectively?
2. How would you respectfully present a challenge to this established norm, especially if it's something senior colleagues strongly support? What kind of language would you use to make a strong, evidence-based recommendation without causing offense?

Teach: Formal Recommendations and Requirements

Teacher notes

1. Review the errors from T1, highlighting the incorrect verb forms.
2. Introduce/re-clarify the form of the mandative subjunctive: Subject + (mandative verb like suggest/recommend/demand) + that + Subject + bare infinitive.
3. Explain the meaning: used for high-stakes professional suggestions, critical evaluations, and formal directives.
4. Concept Check Question 1: 'If the recommendation is about 'the manager', do we say 'the manager revises' or 'the manager revise'?' (Expected: 'revise').
5. Concept Check Question 2: 'Does this structure make a casual suggestion or a strong, formal directive in a professional context?' (Expected: 'strong, formal directive').
6. Provide controlled practice using the tasks below.

Student handout

Crafting Clear Directives

Let's review the structure we use for formal recommendations and requirements, often found in professional reports, policies, or proposals. This structure is called the mandative subjunctive.

We use specific verbs (like suggest, recommend, demand, insist, require, propose, advise) followed by 'that', then a subject, and then the bare infinitive form of the verb. This means the verb doesn't change, even for 'he', 'she', or 'it'.

Example (Affirmative): The safety committee recommends that *everyone wear* protective gloves in the assembly area.
Example (Negative): The new guideline insists that *staff not bypass* the security checks.

This structure is used for high-stakes professional suggestions, critical evaluations, and formal directives in a respectful but firm manner.

Materials

Tasks

Choose the correct verb form to complete the formal recommendation:

The new company guideline insists that every employee ______ the updated privacy policy.

  • reads
  • read
  • to read

Choose the correct verb form for the negative recommendation:

The management recommends that project leads ______ any changes to the original scope without prior approval.

  • not makes
  • not make
  • don't make

Rewrite the following informal suggestion as a formal policy statement using a mandative subjunctive verb (e.g., 'require that...').

Informal: 'It's crucial that she manages the client accounts more actively.' Formal: The firm ______ she ______ the client accounts more actively.

T2

Test 2: Drafting a Policy Proposal

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will apply the mandative subjunctive to draft a formal recommendation in a workplace scenario.
2. Instruct students to read the scenario carefully.
3. Students should formulate a professional recommendation that includes at least one sentence using the mandative subjunctive structure.
4. Monitor for correct use of the bare infinitive and appropriate mandative verbs.
5. Sample Answer Idea for teacher reference: 'The management committee recommends that *all staff arrive* punctually for scheduled meetings. Furthermore, it suggests that *team leaders ensure* a brief pre-meeting check-in for preparedness.'

Student handout

Proposing New Workplace Standards

Imagine you are part of a committee tasked with improving efficiency or safety in your workplace. Read the following situation and then write a formal recommendation or policy statement using the mandative subjunctive (e.g., 'We recommend that...', 'The policy stipulates that...'). Ensure your language is professional and clear, aiming to propose a change respectfully yet firmly.

Scenario: Many employees are frequently late to team meetings, disrupting discussions and wasting time. This has been identified as a significant barrier to productivity and effective communication.

Your Task: Draft a formal recommendation to address this issue. Your recommendation should include at least one sentence using the mandative subjunctive structure.

Tasks
