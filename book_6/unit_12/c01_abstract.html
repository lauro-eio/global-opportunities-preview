L-IN

Lead-In: Discussing New Workplace Safety

Teacher notes

1. Begin by drawing on students' personal experiences with workplace changes.
2. Elicit initial thoughts on new rules and how they are usually communicated.
3. Encourage pairs or small group discussion before opening up to the whole class.
4. Facilitate a brief whole-class share-out, noting any immediate concerns or positive reactions.

Student handout

Talking About New Safety Rules

Think about your own workplace or a workplace you know well.

Imagine a new safety guideline or protocol is about to be introduced. What comes to mind immediately?

Discuss with a partner:
1. What are the first things you would want to know about this new protocol?
2. What immediate benefits might it bring, or what concerns might you have?
3. How would you ideally like this information to be shared and discussed with you and your colleagues?

Tasks

Match each safety term to its correct definition.

  • protocol — A set of official rules or procedures for doing something.
  • implementation — The process of putting a decision or plan into effect.
  • feasibility — The possibility that something can be made, done, or achieved, or is reasonable.
  • risk mitigation — Actions taken to reduce the severity or likelihood of a potential danger or problem.
  • safety culture — The shared attitudes, beliefs, and practices that shape how safety is perceived and prioritized in an organization.
GIST

Gist Reading: New Workplace Safety Protocol Announcement

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will read an internal company memo.
2. Instruct them to read quickly for the general idea, not every detail.
3. Set a time limit for skimming (e.g., 2-3 minutes).
4. After reading, have students complete the multiple-choice question to check their understanding of the main purpose.
5. Briefly discuss the answer, but avoid getting into detailed comprehension at this stage.

Student handout

First Look: A New Safety Policy

You are going to read an internal memo about a new safety protocol. Read the memo quickly to understand its main purpose. Don't worry about every detail right now.

Then, choose the best option to describe the main purpose of the memo.

Materials

MEMORANDUM: Enhancing Our Safety Culture

To: All Employees
From: Operations Management
Date: 15 October 2024
Subject: Introduction of New Incident Reporting Protocol

Dear Team,

We are writing to inform you about an important update to our workplace safety procedures. Effective November 1st, we will be launching a new Incident Reporting Protocol aimed at significantly enhancing our overall safety culture.

This new protocol is the result of extensive review and feedback, including input from various departments. Its primary objective is to streamline the process for reporting near-misses and minor incidents, ensuring that all potential hazards are documented and addressed promptly. We believe that a more transparent and efficient reporting system is crucial for effective risk mitigation across all our operations.

We understand that the implementation of any new system can present challenges, and we are committed to making this transition as smooth as possible. We have carefully considered the feasibility of this new approach within our current operational structure and are confident it will integrate effectively. Training sessions for the new digital reporting platform will be mandatory for all staff and will be scheduled throughout October. Details on these sessions will be circulated next week.

Our commitment to your safety remains paramount. This enhanced protocol is a proactive step to ensure a safer working environment for everyone. We encourage open discussion and constructive feedback as we move forward with this vital implementation.

Thank you for your cooperation and dedication to maintaining a safe workplace.

Tasks

What is the main purpose of this memorandum?

The main purpose of this memorandum is to:

  • Announce a new policy and explain its benefits for improving safety.
  • Apologize for recent safety failures and promise improvements.
  • Ask employees for their ideas on how to improve workplace safety.
  • Detail the full schedule for all upcoming safety training courses.
DET

Detailed Reading: Understanding the New Safety Protocol

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now read the same memo again, but this time for specific details.
2. Instruct them to answer the short-text questions based on the information in the memo.
3. Allow students ample time to re-read and locate the answers.
4. After task completion, check answers together, referring back to the text to justify.
5. Transition to the discussion questions in 'student_content', encouraging deeper analysis and personal connection to the topic.
6. For discussion points, guide students to explain their reasoning, drawing on the memo's content and their own experiences. Ensure answers are not simply 'yes/no'.

FBDC

Follow-up: Debating Protocol Implementation

Teacher notes

1. Bring the class together for a whole-group discussion based on the reading and previous discussions.
2. Prompt students to share their opinions on the feasibility of the new protocol and its potential impact on safety culture.
3. Encourage students to use the new vocabulary from the PTV stage.
4. Guide the discussion to explore different viewpoints on how such changes are best introduced and managed.
5. Provide brief, constructive feedback on student contributions, focusing on the content of their ideas and effective use of target language.

Student handout

Reflecting on New Workplace Policies

Let's reflect on the memo and the discussion.

1. Based on the memo, what do you think are the strongest arguments for introducing this new protocol?
2. What potential difficulties might the company face during its implementation?
3. In your opinion, how can management best ensure the feasibility of new safety measures and encourage a strong safety culture among all employees?
4. What role do employees play in the successful implementation and long-term effectiveness of new safety protocols?
