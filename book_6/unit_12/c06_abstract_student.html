Your Professional Future

Consider your career path over the next few years. How would you effectively communicate your future goals and how they align with your company's vision?

Discuss the following questions with a partner:
1. Have you ever needed to discuss your career future or development needs with a supervisor?
2. What challenges did you face, or what went well?
3. Why is it important to clearly articulate your ambition and how it supports your company's goals?
4. What kind of growth do you envision for yourself in your current role or within the company?

Materials

Email to Supervisor: Discussing Future Opportunities

Subject: Discussing Future Opportunities and Development - [Your Name]

Dear Mr./Ms. [Supervisor's Last Name],

I hope this email finds you well.

I'm writing to you today to discuss my trajectory within [Company Name] and explore potential avenues for my continued growth and skillset development. I've greatly valued my time here and am enthusiastic about contributing further to our team's success.

Over the next few years, my ambition is to move towards a role with greater strategic oversight, specifically within [mention a specific area, e.g., 'project management' or 'operations efficiency']. I believe my experience in [mention current role/achievements] has prepared me for taking on more responsibility in this direction, and I am keen to develop the necessary competencies.

To support this growth, I would greatly appreciate the opportunity to discuss any potential mentorship programs or internal training courses that could enhance my strategic planning and leadership skills. I am particularly interested in [mention specific skills, e.g., 'advanced data analysis' or 'team leadership techniques'] that would directly contribute to [Company Name]'s objectives in [mention a specific company project or goal].

I am confident that by enhancing my skillset, I can take on more complex challenges and align my long-term career aspirations with the company’s evolving needs. I am committed to making a sustained impact and believe that targeted development will allow me to do so more effectively.

Would you be available for a brief meeting sometime next week to discuss this further? I am flexible and happy to work around your schedule.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Your Title]

Structuring Your Pitch

Before you write your own email, brainstorm your ideas. Think about:
1. What is your long-term career trajectory or ambition within your current company?
2. What specific skills or areas do you need to develop (e.g., leadership, technical skills, project management)? These are your skillset development needs.
3. How would this growth benefit the company? (e.g., improve efficiency, lead new initiatives, support company goals).
4. What kind of support are you looking for? (e.g., mentorship, training, specific projects).

Drafting Your Career Plan Email

Now, write an email to your supervisor articulating your long-term career aspirations and development needs. Remember to also explain how your goals align with your company's vision.

Audience: Your supervisor
Purpose: To express your career goals, identify development needs, and request a meeting to discuss how they align with company objectives.
Approximate Length: 200-300 words

Use the plan you made in the previous stage and incorporate some of the useful phrases.

Improving Your Written Request

Exchange your email with a partner. Read each other's emails and provide feedback using the questions below. Focus on content first, then language.

Content Feedback:
1. Is the purpose of the email clear? Does the writer clearly state their ambition and development needs?
2. Does the email effectively explain how the writer's growth aligns with the company's goals?
3. Is the tone appropriate (professional, proactive, polite)?
4. Is there a clear call to action (e.g., requesting a meeting)?

Language Feedback:
1. Are there any parts that are unclear or could be expressed more precisely?
2. Did the writer use effective vocabulary, especially terms related to career trajectory and skillset development?
3. Check for any errors in grammar or punctuation.
