L-IN

Lead-In: AI in Our Lives

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into pairs or small groups.
2. Explain the task: brainstorm AI tools encountered in professional or personal life.
3. Encourage discussion on how these tools simplify or complicate tasks, and initial thoughts on ethical concerns.
4. Monitor discussions, listen for initial ideas and vocabulary.
5. Bring the class together for a brief whole-group share-out.

Student handout

Smart Tools, Real-World Use

Think about your professional or personal life. What AI tools or automated systems have you encountered?

Discuss with a partner:
1. How do these tools make tasks simpler or, perhaps, more complicated?
2. What initial ethical concerns or benefits come to mind when using them?
3. Have any of these tools impacted your job tasks or daily routine? How?

MOD

Model: Discussing AI Integration

Teacher notes

1. Introduce the dialogue as a model of colleagues discussing AI integration.
2. Have students read the dialogue silently first.
3. Play/read the dialogue aloud for students, or have them read it in pairs.
4. Check gist understanding using the comprehension questions. Allow students to discuss answers in pairs before reviewing as a class.
5. Highlight any unfamiliar but relevant vocabulary from the dialogue if necessary (e.g., 'streamline processes', 'data privacy').

Student handout

Workplace AI: A Conversation

Read the conversation between two colleagues, Anna and Ben, discussing the potential integration of a new AI decision-support tool in their department.

---

Anna: Hey Ben, did you see the memo about trialling that new AI-powered decision-support tool for project allocation?

Ben: I did. It promises to streamline processes and improve productivity. The demo showed it could analyze workload and skill sets to suggest optimal team assignments.

Anna: On the surface, that sounds like a great practical benefit. It could really cut down on the manual planning hours. Less paperwork, more actual work.

Ben: Exactly. It's a clear example of AI automation making things more efficient. But I also have some reservations. What about the ethical implications?

Anna: You mean like job impact? Are people worried it might replace their roles?

Ben: That, and also the transparency. How does it make its decisions? Is it truly unbiased? We need to ensure ethical AI principles are being followed, especially when it affects people's daily tasks and potentially their career progression. We can't just blindly trust the algorithm.

Anna: Good point. It's about finding that balance, isn't it? Harnessing the productivity tools without compromising fairness or human oversight.

Ben: Absolutely. We should probably bring these points up at the next team meeting. It's a significant change to our routine workplace tasks.

---

Comprehension Check:
1. What is the main practical benefit of the AI tool Anna and Ben discuss?
2. What is Ben's primary ethical concern regarding the AI tool?

FP

Fluency Practice: AI Integration Meeting

Teacher notes

1. Divide students into groups of 3-4. Assign roles if desired (e.g., 'Pro-AI', 'Skeptical of AI', 'Neutral/Mediator').
2. Clearly explain the scenario and the task: discuss the proposal for an AI tool.
3. Remind students to use the notes and phrases they prepared.
4. Emphasize fluency and communicative effectiveness over accuracy at this stage. Encourage them to keep the conversation flowing.
5. Monitor from a distance, making notes on language points (not individual errors, but common issues or excellent usage) for delayed correction in FBDC. Avoid interrupting.

Student handout

Debating Smart Tools at Work

Imagine your department is considering integrating a new AI tool to assist with a routine workplace task (e.g., scheduling, inventory management, initial customer query handling). You are in a team meeting to discuss this proposal.

Your Task:
Discuss the ethical implications and practical benefits of integrating this AI tool. Each person should express their viewpoint, use the phrases and ideas you prepared, and try to reach a collective understanding or recommendation for your manager.

Consider these points in your discussion:
* What specific practical benefits could this AI tool bring to your daily tasks?
* What ethical concerns or potential problems might arise (e.g., job impact, fairness, data privacy)?
* How can you ensure the AI is implemented responsibly and follows ethical AI principles?
* What specific steps or guidelines should your department consider to make the most of this productivity tool while minimizing risks?

FBDC

Feedback and Delayed Correction

Teacher notes

1. Content Feedback (5-7 minutes): Bring the class together. Ask groups to briefly summarise their discussion, key points, and any conclusions/recommendations. Praise good ideas and strong arguments. Ask open questions: 'Did you manage to address the ethical concerns fully?' 'What was the most challenging aspect of your discussion?'
2. Language Feedback (5-7 minutes): Write up 3-5 common errors (e.g., pronunciation, misuse of target lexis, common fluency blockers, inaccurate phrasing) observed during the FP stage on the board. Do not attribute them to specific students. Elicit corrections from the class or provide them. Focus on errors that hindered communication or relate to the target language of the lesson.
3. Positive Feedback: Highlight specific examples of strong language use or effective communication observed during the activity.
