L-IN

Lead-In: Verifying Workplace Information

Teacher notes

1. Greet students and briefly explain the day's focus: critically evaluating information at work.
2. Introduce the lead-in question: 'Consider a recent instance where you received information via a work group chat or email. How did you determine its accuracy or trustworthiness before acting on it?'
3. Put students in pairs or small groups to discuss for 3-5 minutes.
4. Open the discussion to the whole class, inviting a few students to share their experiences and strategies.

Student handout

Is This Info Reliable?

Think about a time you received information from a work group chat or email. It could be about a new policy, a change in schedule, or an urgent update.

Discuss these questions with a partner:
1. What was the information about?
2. How did you feel when you first received it?
3. What steps did you take to check if the information was true or trustworthy?
4. What could be the risks of acting on unverified information in the workplace?

Materials

Vocabulary

  • internal communications
  • digital literacy
  • verify
Tasks

Match each term to its definition.

  • misinformation — Incorrect or misleading information, often spread unintentionally.
  • credible sources — Trusted and reliable places where information comes from, like official company channels.
  • fact-checking — The process of confirming whether information is true and accurate.
  • internal communications — Messages and information shared within an organization, usually to employees.
  • digital literacy — The ability to find, evaluate, create, and communicate information using digital technologies.
  • verify — To check or prove that something is true, accurate, or justified.
GIST

Gist Reading: Identifying Core Messages

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now read a workplace memo.
2. Instruct them to read the memo quickly to understand the general message or main purpose, without worrying about every detail.
3. Set a time limit for skimming (e.g., 2-3 minutes).
4. After skimming, ask students to answer the multiple-choice question.
5. Elicit the correct answer and briefly discuss why it is the main point.

Student handout

First Look: What's the Main Point?

Read the following memo from company management quickly. Don't worry about understanding every single word. Just try to get a general idea of what the memo is about. Then, choose the best answer to the question below.

Materials

Clarification Regarding Recent Workplace Discussions

Dear Team,

It has come to our attention that some misinformation has been circulating in unofficial work group chats regarding upcoming changes to employee shift patterns and potential adjustments to the benefits package. We understand that such discussions can cause concern and confusion.

We want to emphasize the importance of relying on credible sources for all company-related news and updates. Official announcements, policy changes, and important information will always be communicated through formal internal communications channels. These include your official work email, the company intranet, and direct memos from management or HR.

To help maintain clarity and prevent the spread of inaccurate information, we encourage everyone to practice fact-checking. If you encounter any unconfirmed details or rumours, especially those affecting your employment terms or benefits, please verify them directly with your line manager or the HR department. Do not solely rely on unverified messages in group chats, which are often not official.

Developing strong digital literacy skills is crucial in our fast-paced environment. This includes knowing how to distinguish between official company policy and casual, unverified discussions. Our goal is to ensure that everyone has access to accurate and timely information.

Thank you for your cooperation in ensuring our workplace remains well-informed and productive.

Sincerely,
[Company Name] Management

Tasks

What is the main purpose of this memo?

  • To announce new changes to employee shift patterns and benefits.
  • To warn employees about using work group chats for any communication.
  • To clarify that rumours are circulating and to guide employees on finding accurate information.
  • To introduce new training on digital literacy skills for all staff.
DET

Detailed Reading: Analyzing Workplace Information

Teacher notes

1. Explain that students will now read the memo again, this time more carefully, to answer specific questions.
2. Ask students to complete the short-text questions individually or in pairs.
3. Check answers for the short-text questions. (Answers: 1. Unofficial work group chats. 2. Official work email, company intranet, direct memos from management or HR. 3. Line manager or HR department. 4. To ensure everyone has access to accurate and timely information and to keep the workplace well-informed and productive.)
4. For the discussion questions in student_content, put students in small groups.
5. Facilitate a whole-class discussion for the inference questions. (Possible answers for discussion:
- Question 1: Risks include misunderstandings, panic, decreased trust, making incorrect decisions, impacting productivity or morale.
- Question 2: Look for official sender, consistent formatting, clear language, references to company policy, avoiding sensationalism. Unofficial might be forwarded messages, informal language, no clear sender, rumours.
- Question 3: Being a responsible communicator means thinking before sharing, confirming information, using official channels for important matters, clarifying uncertainties, correcting misinformation politely.)

Student handout

Diving Deeper: Accuracy and Trust

Read the memo again carefully. Answer the factual questions, then discuss the further points with your classmates.

Part 1: Factual Questions
1. According to the memo, where has misinformation been circulating?
2. What are the formal internal communications channels mentioned in the memo?
3. Who should employees contact to verify unconfirmed details or rumours?
4. What is the company's goal regarding information access for employees?

Part 2: Discussion and Inference
1. What are some potential negative consequences if employees act on the misinformation mentioned in the memo?
2. What are some clues that an internal communication is official and trustworthy, compared to something unofficial or a rumour?
3. In your opinion, how can employees contribute to being responsible communicators in the digital workplace?

FBDC

Feedback and Discussion: Applying Critical Thinking

Teacher notes

1. Lead a whole-class discussion to summarize the key takeaways from the lesson.
2. Encourage students to share their personal strategies for verifying information at work, building on the discussion questions from the DET stage.
3. Ask students how they will apply what they've learned to their own workplace communication practices.
4. Provide brief language feedback, noting any emergent language or pronunciation issues observed during the discussion, focusing on communicative effectiveness rather than structural correction.
5. Conclude the lesson by reinforcing the importance of digital literacy and critical evaluation in professional settings.
