  • professional branding — The process of consciously creating a distinctive professional image for oneself.
  • social media etiquette — The unwritten rules of behavior and conduct on social media platforms.
  • privacy settings — Controls that allow users to manage who can see their information on social media or other online platforms.

Student handout

Understanding the Main Message

Listen to the conversation between a career advisor and a client. What is the main purpose of their discussion?

Materials

Managing Your Digital Footprint

MARIA: Good morning, Alex. Thanks for coming in. Today, I want to talk about something crucial for your job search: your online reputation. More and more, employers are looking at candidates' digital footprints before they even call them for an interview. It's not just about what's on your CV anymore.

ALEX: Right, I get that. I mean, I'm careful with what I post, but I don't really think about it in a 'professional' way.

MARIA: That's a common misconception. Think of it as professional branding. Your online presence should reflect the kind of employee you want to be. Are your social media profiles consistent with your professional goals? Are you presenting yourself as reliable, knowledgeable, and a team player?

ALEX: Hmm, I hadn't really considered it like that. My Facebook is mostly photos from holidays and family events. LinkedIn is where I keep my work stuff.

MARIA: That's a good start. LinkedIn is definitely key for career advancement. But even on other platforms, it’s important to practice good social media etiquette. This means avoiding controversial posts, respecting others' privacy, and thinking twice before sharing anything that could be misinterpreted. Even if your settings are private, things can get shared. And remember, privacy settings are not always foolproof. Screenshots exist, and what’s private today might not be tomorrow.

ALEX: So, you're saying I should clean up my old posts?

MARIA: It's more about strategic management. Consider archiving old content that doesn't align with your current professional image. Think about what message each platform sends. For instance, if you're looking for a project management role, perhaps sharing industry articles on LinkedIn is more beneficial than just holiday snaps. Also, think about Google yourself. What comes up? Is it what you want a recruiter to see? If you have an uncommon name, it’s easier to control. If your name is common, adding a middle initial or professional title can help differentiate.

ALEX: That makes sense. I definitely don't want anything unprofessional showing up. So, beyond cleaning up, what else can I do?

MARIA: Proactively build your positive online reputation. Engage in professional communities. Share relevant content. Comment thoughtfully on industry news. Show your expertise. This isn't about being fake; it’s about curating an authentic but professional image. It’s an ongoing process, not a one-time clean-up.

  • That changing privacy settings will solve all his online reputation issues.

FBDC

Feedback and Discussion: Applying the Advice

Teacher notes

1. Lead a whole-class discussion, encouraging students to share their reactions to the advice given in the audio.
2. Ask students if they agree with Maria's advice and if they have similar experiences or strategies.
3. Focus on how students can apply the advice to their own online reputation and professional branding.
4. Briefly review the key vocabulary from the PTV stage, asking students to use them in context.
5. Provide brief feedback on any common errors or effective language use observed during the discussion.
