Teacher notes

Introduce the scenario: receiving a suspicious email or message. Elicit from students how they identify a scam. Write keywords on the board, e.g., phishing, sender, urgent, password, suspicious link. Encourage personal experiences or observations.

MFP

Meaning, Form, Pronunciation: Past Actions in Finance

Teacher notes

Review the pronunciation of -ed endings for regular past simple verbs. Drill booked /t/, delivered /d/, audited /ɪd/. Explain the meaning: these actions are complete. Use example sentences related to finance/work: 'The payment was booked yesterday.' 'The goods were delivered this morning.' 'The accounts were audited last week.' Pay close attention to distinguishing the final sounds as per phonological_focus.

Student handout

Finished Business: The Sound of the Past

We will quickly review how we talk about finished actions at work, especially with money or documents. Listen carefully to the ending sounds of these words to hear if an action is truly complete.

FP

Free Practice: Workplace Verification Strategy

Teacher notes

Set up a role-play or discussion scenario. Students work in pairs to discuss a potential fraud situation related to a new supplier or client. Encourage them to use the vocabulary from the unit and the correctly pronounced -ed verbs, e.g., 'The account details were booked by calling them directly.' Monitor for fluency and correct use of target language. Provide feedback on common errors.

Student handout

Planning Your Protection

You receive an email from a supposed new supplier, asking for your company's bank details 'urgently' for a large order. In pairs, discuss how you would verify this request. What specific steps would you take to make sure it's not a scam? Use the vocabulary we reviewed today.
