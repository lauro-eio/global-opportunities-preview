L-IN

Lead-In: Everyday Repairs

Teacher notes

- Begin by asking students about everyday items they use that might need repair or a service.
- Guide the discussion to specific examples like a worn shoe or a broken key.
- Prompt students to think about how they would ask for the price of such a service.
- Focus on activating prior knowledge and setting the context for inquiring about cost.

Student handout

Things That Need a Fix

Think about common things that sometimes break or need attention. For example, a shoe with a broken heel, or a key that needs a copy. What do you do in these situations? Where do you go? What do you say to find out how much it will cost?

MFP

Presentation: Asking and Stating Prices

Teacher notes

- Present the target exponents using the model dialogue. Ensure students understand the meaning for asking and stating prices politely in a workplace interaction.
- Highlight the fixed form of the phrases (questions and statements).
- Practice pronunciation: Model rising intonation for questions (stressing 'much', 'price') and falling intonation for statements (stressing the amount). Drill these phrases.
- Use the provided column_banks to display the phrases clearly.
- Check understanding of form and meaning with the match_pairs task.

Student handout

The Price Question

Here are polite ways to ask and state prices for a service. Listen and repeat. Pay attention to the stressed words.

Customer: Excuse me, I need a key copied. How much is it?
Shopkeeper: For a standard key, it's 5 euros.
Customer: And what's the price for two copies?
Shopkeeper: That's 10 euros total.

Controlled Practice: Completing Price Dialogues

Teacher notes

- Distribute the worksheet. Instruct students to complete the dialogues using the target exponents.
- Remind them to consider if the blank needs a question or a statement phrase for cost.
- Circulate and monitor, providing immediate error correction on form and pronunciation.
- Review answers as a class, reinforcing correct usage and intonation.

FP

Freer Practice: Roleplay at the Service Counter

Teacher notes

- Explain the roleplay scenario: one student is a customer needing a service (shoe repair, key cutting), the other is the shop employee.
- Instruct students to use the phrases for asking and stating prices learned in the previous stages.
- Encourage them to create short dialogues. Emphasize polite and clear communication relevant to a professional context.
- Circulate and observe, noting down errors for delayed correction. Focus on fluency and appropriate use of exponents.
- After some practice, ask a few pairs to perform for the class.
- Provide general feedback and correct persistent errors.

Model Response (for teacher reference):
Customer: Excuse me, I need my shoe fixed. How much is it?
Employee: For a simple repair, it's 15 dollars.
Customer: Okay. And for two pairs?
Employee: That's 30 dollars total.

Student handout

At the Shop Counter

Work in pairs. You are at a repair shop. One person is the customer, the other is the shop employee. The customer needs a service (e.g., shoe fix, key copy). Ask about the cost. The employee states the price. Switch roles. Act out different scenarios.
