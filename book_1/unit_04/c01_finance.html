Lead-In: Tools for the Job

Teacher notes

1. Display pictures of common work tools (e.g., hammer, wrench, screwdriver).
2. Ask learners if they use tools in their jobs.
3. Ask learners to imagine they need a new tool for work. Where do they go to buy it?
4. Elicit or introduce the situation: buying a work-related tool at a shop.
5. Ask: What do you say when you want to know the price?

MFP

MFP: Asking and Stating Prices

Teacher notes

1. Introduce target phrases: 'How much is it?', 'What's the price?', 'It's [number] [currency].', 'That's [number] total.'
2. Model meaning: Use realia or pictures of tools to demonstrate asking and stating prices (e.g., 'This hammer is ten pounds').
3. Model form: Highlight question structure vs. statement structure.
4. Model pronunciation: Emphasize 'In questions, stress the key information word (e.g., 'much', 'price') and use a rising intonation, while statements should have a falling intonation, stressing the amount.' Drill whole phrases.
5. Learners complete tasks to check understanding of key words.

Shop Talk: How Much?

Listen and repeat these phrases for buying tools.
1. How much is it? (Asking about one item)
2. What's the price? (Asking about the cost)
3. It's ten pounds. (Stating the price of one item)
4. That's twenty dollars total. (Stating a total price)

Remember to stress the important words in questions and the number in answers.

Controlled Practice: Asking for Tool Prices

Teacher notes

1. Divide learners into pairs.
2. Hand out cards with pictures of different work tools and prices (e.g., 'Hammer - £12', 'Wrench - £18').
3. One student is the customer, the other is the shopkeeper.
4. Customer asks for the price, shopkeeper responds.
5. Monitor for correct use of target phrases and pronunciation, especially 'How much is it?', 'What's the price?', 'It's [number] [currency].', 'That's [number] total.'
6. Learners complete gap-fill tasks.

FP

Freer Practice: Buying Tools for Work

Teacher notes

1. Divide the class into small groups (e.g., 3-4 learners).
2. Give each group a list of 3-4 tools they might need for their work and ask them to choose one to 'buy'.
3. One learner is the 'shopkeeper', others are 'customers'.
4. Customers take turns asking about the price of their chosen tool. The shopkeeper responds, and they make the 'purchase'.
5. Encourage natural interaction using the target language.
6. Monitor carefully, noting any errors for later delayed error correction.
7. Sample exchange: Customer: 'Excuse me, how much is this screwdriver?' Shopkeeper: 'It's seven pounds.' Customer: 'Great, I'll buy it. Here's the cash.' Shopkeeper: 'Thank you.'
